Two deep dives, before and after.

## Kenya: clean water, minus the razor blades

The source is a 6-week Grade 6 science strand: how water becomes unsafe, filtering, disinfection, handwashing, storage. It also asks learners to cut bottles with a razor, pour boiling water, climb on the roof, walk to the lake shore unsupervised, and keep a register of which families treat their water — and to buy testing kits.

The cards kept the germs explanation and the class chart, the filter build, the boiling and sunlight demonstrations, handwashing and covered storage. The razor, the unsupervised walk, the family register and the kits are gone, each listed with its reason. Added: whole-class finger checks during the explanation, recall-from-memory writing with the chart covered, and rotating roles (Holder, Smeller, Reporter) for comparing water jars — with no tasting, no fingers in the water, and handwashing at the end. Class votes were considered and abstained everywhere: no real trade-off for the class to decide.

## Peru: fractions, minus the market trip

The source is a 6-week Grade 5 unit: folding paper strips into equivalent fractions, comparing and adding, a class garden bed, a poster. It also sends pupils to a named market with coins from home, sets homework on the family's harvest earnings, posts weekly scores by name, and opens with a two-session lecture on Inca measuring.

The cards kept the strips (pupils keep them in their notebooks for the next unit), the garden bed and the poster, with sustained practice time: a full lesson of exercises, two lessons to draft the poster. The market trip, the harvest homework and the ranked scores are gone. Added: whole-class checks, recall from memory, a showcase of the posters for a younger class — and a class vote on the garden bed's two contested squares, with the counting rule announced first. The history lecture was set aside: no unit needs it.

## Read them as drafts, not verdicts

Each walkthrough shows its working: which sections were kept, what was removed and why, which cards were applied or refused, and the full final cards. If you teach one of these subjects, the most useful thing you can do is read one and say what's wrong: [issues](https://github.com/ashitpatel/lesson-scaffold-builder/issues). The [final post]({{ site.baseurl }}/adapt-it/) covers how to run the tool and adapt it to your school.
