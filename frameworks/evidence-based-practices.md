# Evidence-Based Practice cards (EB-01 to EB-05)

The cards the Evidence-Based stage (`prompts/25-evidence-based.md`) may apply to an MLS unit, and the critic (`prompts/50-critic.md`) checks against. The requirements ask the pipeline to anchor its loops "strictly to verified, evidence-based practices" and to disallow "unsupported learning outcome assumptions" (G14), but don't list the practices. These five were chosen because each has broad, replicated support across well-known research syntheses (listed under **Basis**), works offline with paper or chalk, and fits inside an existing classroom step. **This selection is a project decision that should be reviewed by the people who own LSB's content.**

Each card has **non-negotiables**: the parts the evidence is about. An edit that can't include all of them is a hollow version of the practice, and the right answer is to abstain.

---

## EB-01 Retrieval Practice
**Targets:** a unit where students must remember facts, terms, methods or steps for later work, and nothing asks them to recall it from memory.

**Allowed edits:**
- Open a later lesson with 3 to 5 short questions on earlier content, answered from memory on scrap paper, slates or orally, then show the answers so each student checks their own.
- Turn a re-reading or copying step into recall: students write what they remember, then check against the notes.

**Non-negotiables:** answers come from memory, with notes, charts and books out of sight; students see the correct answers straight after; nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

**Never:** grade or record the results; rank students; replace teaching with quizzing.

**Already present when:** the unit already has students recall earlier content without notes and then check it.

**Basis:** practice testing was rated high utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*; "use quizzing to promote learning" is a recommendation of the IES practice guide by Pashler et al. (2007), *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning*.

## EB-02 Spaced Practice
**Targets:** a unit whose key content is practised only in the week it is taught, when later units or the final task need it.

**Allowed edits:**
- Add a short review of earlier content in a later week of the unit (a few questions or one worked problem), rather than all practice in one block.
- In a unit that depends on an earlier one, fold a brief review of the upstream content into the "Recall from" step, as practice, not only as a reminder.

**Non-negotiables:** the review comes at least a week after first teaching; students do something with the content (answer, solve, explain), not just look at it.

**Never:** add review that cuts a unit's sustained writing or reasoning time; lengthen the unit past its declared weeks.

**Already present when:** the unit already returns to earlier content in a later week with student practice.

**Basis:** distributed practice was rated high utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013); "space learning over time" is a recommendation of Pashler et al. (2007).

## EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice
**Targets:** a step where students first meet a new procedure (a calculation, a method, a type of problem) and go straight to solving problems alone, or only watch examples.

**Allowed edits:**
- Show a complete worked example, then one with the last step left for students to finish, before independent problems.
- Alternate: one worked example, then one similar problem for students, then the next example.

**Non-negotiables:** at least one complete worked example is shown step by step before students solve alone; students then solve similar problems themselves.

**Never:** replace students' own problem-solving with watching; use it for open-ended creative or argument writing, where it would hand students the answer.

**Already present when:** the unit already models examples step by step and then has students solve similar problems.

**Basis:** "interleave worked example solutions with problem-solving exercises" is a recommendation of Pashler et al. (2007); "provide models" and guided practice are among Rosenshine's (2012) *Principles of Instruction*.

## EB-04 Checking for Understanding
**Targets:** a step where the educator explains or models something new and moves on without seeing whether every student has understood.

**Allowed edits:**
- Ask one or two questions every student answers at the same time (slates, scrap paper held up, fingers for a choice, cards), look at all the answers, and re-explain if many are wrong before moving on.

**Non-negotiables:** every student answers, not just volunteers; the educator looks at the answers during the lesson; what happens next depends on them (re-explain, or move on).

**Never:** record or display individual answers; call out who got it wrong; turn it into a test.

**Already present when:** the unit already has every student answer during teaching and adjusts to the answers.

**Basis:** "check for student understanding" is one of Rosenshine's (2012) *Principles of Instruction*; Black and Wiliam (1998), *Inside the Black Box*, review evidence on formative assessment used to adjust teaching.

## EB-05 Concrete and Visual Representations
**Targets:** a step that explains an abstract idea (a ratio, a process, a relationship) in words or symbols only.

**Allowed edits:**
- Pair the explanation with a simple drawing, diagram or physical model made from classroom or found materials, and say explicitly how each part of the picture or model matches the abstract idea.

**Non-negotiables:** the picture or model is shown together with the words or symbols, not instead of them; the link between them is stated.

**Never:** add decoration that doesn't carry the idea; require printing, devices or purchased materials.

**Already present when:** the unit already uses a diagram, model or drawing linked to the abstract idea.

**Basis:** "combine graphics with verbal descriptions" and "connect and integrate abstract and concrete representations of concepts" are recommendations of Pashler et al. (2007).

## Guardrails (every card)
- **EB-G1 All non-negotiables or abstain.** A card is applied with every one of its non-negotiables, or not at all.
- **EB-G2 Augment, don't replace.** A practice is added to existing teaching. It never removes or shortens skill practice, a mastery check, or sustained writing or reasoning, and the milestone and `macro_alignment` stay the same.
- **EB-G3 Low stakes, no records.** Quizzes, checks and reviews are never graded, collected, recorded or displayed by name. No ranking, no name lists.
- **EB-G4 No outcome claims in steps.** Steps describe what to do, never what it achieves. The **Basis** lines are for reviewers; never copy a claim, number or "research shows" into a step.
- **EB-G5 Offline and zero-cost.** Scrap paper, slates, chalk, fingers or cards made from reused paper. No devices, printing or purchases.
- **EB-G6 Fits the time.** The edit fits the unit's declared weeks and periods; a review or check takes minutes, not a lesson.
