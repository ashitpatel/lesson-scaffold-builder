# Evidence-Based Practice cards (EB-01 to EB-09)

The cards the Evidence-Based stage (`prompts/25-evidence-based.md`) may apply to an MLS unit, and the critic (`prompts/50-critic.md`) checks against. The requirements ask the pipeline to anchor its loops "strictly to verified, evidence-based practices" and to disallow "unsupported learning outcome assumptions" (G14), but don't list the practices. These nine were chosen because each has broad, replicated support across well-known research syntheses (listed under **Basis**) and works offline with paper or chalk. All but EB-06 fit inside an existing classroom step; EB-06 may reorganize practice across a unit's steps, using existing practice time without adding periods. **This selection is a project decision that should be reviewed by the people who own LSB's content.** EB-04 and EB-05 each carry one Stanford-translated routine (see docs/methodologies.md for sources and thresholds).

Each card has **non-negotiables**: the parts the evidence is about. An edit that can't include all of them is a hollow version of the practice, and the right answer is to abstain.

---

## EB-01 Retrieval Practice
**Targets:** a unit where students must remember facts, terms, methods or steps for later work, and nothing asks them to recall it from memory.

**Allowed edits:**
- Open a later lesson with 3 to 5 short questions on earlier content, answered from memory on scrap paper, slates or orally, then show the answers so each student checks their own.
- Turn a re-reading or copying step into recall: students write what they remember, then check against the notes.
- Add a confidence check around the recall — required unless the step is at the sentence cap: before answering, every student shows thumbs-up, sideways or down for how sure they feel; after checking their own answers, they show it again. The signals are never recorded or graded.

**Non-negotiables:** answers come from memory, with notes, charts and books out of sight; students see the correct answers straight after; nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

**Never:** grade or record the results or the confidence signals; rank students; replace teaching with quizzing.

**Already present when:** the unit already has students recall earlier content without notes and then check it, with a confidence signal before and after.

**Basis:** practice testing was rated high utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*; "use quizzing to promote learning" is a recommendation of the IES practice guide by Pashler et al. (2007), *Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning*. The confidence check adapts the EF+Math prediction/postdiction measure — the same self-rating immediately before and after a mathematics task — to a thumbs signal; prediction/postdiction calibration is studied in Erickson and Heit (2015), *Metacognition and confidence: comparing math to other academic subjects*, who find students better calibrated after completing a test. The paper-only port is a project adaptation.

## EB-02 Spaced Practice
**Targets:** a unit whose key content is practised only in the week it is taught, when later units or the final task need it.

**Allowed edits:**
- Add a short review of earlier content in a later week of the unit (a few questions or one worked problem), rather than all practice in one block.
- In a unit that depends on an earlier one, fold a brief review of the upstream content into the "Recall from" step, as practice, not only as a reminder.

**Non-negotiables:** the review comes at least a week after first teaching; students do something with the content (answer, solve, explain), not just look at it.

**Never:** add review that cuts a unit's sustained writing or reasoning time; lengthen the unit past its declared weeks.

**Already present when:** the unit already returns to earlier content in a later week with student practice.

**Basis:** distributed practice was rated high utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013); "space learning over time" is a recommendation of Pashler et al. (2007).

## EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice
**Targets:** a step where students first meet a new procedure (a calculation, a method, a type of problem) and go straight to solving problems alone, or only watch examples.

**Allowed edits:**
- Show a complete worked example, then one with the last step left for students to finish, before independent problems.
- Alternate: one worked example, then one similar problem for students, then the next example.
- On multi-step problems, add a plan gate — required unless the step is at the sentence cap: before touching numbers, each student writes or says one sentence stating what they will try first; the teacher nods them on, then they solve.

**Non-negotiables:** at least one complete worked example is shown step by step before students solve alone; students then solve similar problems themselves.

**Never:** replace students' own problem-solving with watching; collect or grade the plan sentences; use it for open-ended creative or argument writing, where it would hand students the answer.

**Already present when:** the unit already models examples step by step and then has students solve similar problems, with a one-sentence plan before multi-step problems.

**Basis:** "interleave worked example solutions with problem-solving exercises" is a recommendation of Pashler et al. (2007); "provide models" and guided practice are among Rosenshine's (2012) *Principles of Instruction*. The plan gate adapts the explore-plan-solve-review phases of CueThinkEF+, a technology-based platform that guides students through a four-phase problem-solving process with supports for working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility (AERDF; American Institutes for Research independent evaluation, 2023–24, which found early but not statistically significant effects). The paper-only gate is a project adaptation, not the tested platform.

## EB-04 Checking for Understanding
**Targets:** a step where the educator explains or models something new and moves on without seeing whether every student has understood.

**Allowed edits:**
- Ask one or two questions every student answers at the same time (slates, scrap paper held up, fingers for a choice, cards), look at all the answers, and re-explain if many are wrong before moving on.
- Add a confidence check around the questions — required unless the step is at the sentence cap: before answering, every student shows thumbs-up, sideways or down for how sure they feel; after the answers are checked, they show it again. The signals guide re-explanation and are never recorded or displayed by name.
- Add uptake moves around the answers (routine `uptake-moves`) — only when the run opted into `uptake` and the step has students explain or discuss (not silent drill): the educator revoices one student idea ("So you are saying..."), presses one student to explain their reasoning ("Why does that work?"), and waits silently after asking. Nothing is recorded. Name the routine in the change entry.

**Non-negotiables:** every student answers, not just volunteers; the educator looks at the answers during the lesson; what happens next depends on them (re-explain, or move on).

**Never:** record or display individual answers or confidence signals; record the classroom in any form; call out who got it wrong; turn it into a test.

**Already present when:** the unit already has every student answer during teaching and adjusts to the answers, with a confidence signal before and after. Uptake moves count as present only when the educator already revoices student ideas.

**Basis:** "check for student understanding" is one of Rosenshine's (2012) *Principles of Instruction*; Black and Wiliam (1998), *Inside the Black Box*, review evidence on formative assessment used to adjust teaching. The confidence check adapts the EF+Math prediction/postdiction measure — the same self-rating immediately before and after a mathematics task — to a thumbs signal; prediction/postdiction calibration is studied in Erickson and Heit (2015), *Metacognition and confidence: comparing math to other academic subjects*, who find students better calibrated after completing a test. The paper-only port is a project adaptation. The uptake moves adapt classroom-discourse coaching to unaided teacher prompts, without recording; measuring talk via lesson recordings is intentionally not adapted (TeachFX FAQ; Learning Forward (2026), *AI-enhanced Coaching: What Early Studies Show*).

## EB-05 Concrete and Visual Representations
**Targets:** a step that explains an abstract idea (a ratio, a process, a relationship) in words or symbols only.

**Allowed edits:**
- Pair the explanation with a simple drawing, diagram or physical model made from classroom or found materials, and say explicitly how each part of the picture or model matches the abstract idea.
- Offer a second channel (routine `udl-choice`) — only when the run opted into `udl`: each key point in the step is both heard and seen (said aloud and shown on the board), and each answer may be spoken, written or shown. Nothing beyond paper, chalk and voice. Name the routine in the change entry.

**Non-negotiables:** the picture or model is shown together with the words or symbols, not instead of them; the link between them is stated.

**Never:** add decoration that doesn't carry the idea; require printing, devices or purchased materials; offer a channel needing materials some students lack.

**Already present when:** the unit already uses a diagram, model or drawing linked to the abstract idea. Choice counts as present only when students already get two perception channels and two response modes.

**Basis:** "combine graphics with verbal descriptions" and "connect and integrate abstract and concrete representations of concepts" are recommendations of Pashler et al. (2007). Choice of channel adapts Universal Design for Learning (multiple means of Engagement, Representation, and Action and Expression) to paper, chalk and voice (CAST, *UDL Guidelines*).

## EB-06 Teaching at the Right Level (level groups)
**Targets:** a unit whose practice steps assume one level while learners span several — typically foundational reading or mathematics practice with mixed prior attainment.

**Allowed edits:**
- Add one quick level check at the start of practice (a few oral or slate items, unmarked), and group learners by what they show: two or three fluid groups, re-checked as they progress.
- Rewrite practice steps so each group practises the same skill at its level in the same time: different items, same minutes.

**Non-negotiables:** groups come from a fresh check of current level, not age, past marks or fixed tracks; every group gets the same learning time on the same skill; groups are fluid and re-checked as learners progress.

**Never:** fixed ability tracks or permanent groups; public labels, ranking, or different goals that strand a group; grouping by behaviour or background.

**Already present when:** the unit already groups by current level with targeted practice per group.

**Basis:** Banerjee, Cole, Duflo and Linden (2007), *Remedying Education: Evidence from Two Randomized Experiments in India*, on remedial level-group teaching; Banerjee et al. (2016), *Mainstreaming an Effective Intervention*, on randomized evaluations of Teaching at the Right Level at scale.

## EB-07 Interleaved Practice
**Targets:** a practice set that drills one problem type in a block before the next type, when each type has already been taught.

**Allowed edits:**
- Mix the practice items across types so learners meet them shuffled, keeping the same items and total count.
- Add a "which method fits this one?" line before each item or pair, answered before solving.

**Non-negotiables:** every interleaved type has already been taught and practised once in blocked form; the mix is deliberate, so each item needs choosing the method; same items, no new content.

**Never:** interleave types never taught; shuffle before strugglers have a foothold — each type starts blocked, then mixes.

**Already present when:** practice already mixes earlier-taught types with choose-the-method prompts.

**Basis:** interleaving was rated moderate utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013); Rohrer and Taylor (2007), *The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning*.

## EB-08 Self-Explanation
**Targets:** a worked example or explanation learners only read, watch or copy.

**Allowed edits:**
- After each example step (or pair of steps), add a prompt: "Turn to your neighbour and say why this step works" or "Write one line: why did we do this step?"
- Add one short paired step where learners explain the example's reasoning to each other before solving alone.
- On multi-step problems, add a plan gate before learners solve alone — required unless the step is at the sentence cap: each learner writes or says one sentence stating what they will try first, then solves.

**Non-negotiables:** prompts ask for the reasoning (why this step, why it works), not recall of facts; every learner explains — pairs, writing, or aloud-to-self all count; the correct reasoning is confirmed straight after.

**Never:** ask learners to explain what hasn't been taught; grade the explanations or the plan sentences.

**Already present when:** the unit already has learners explain example reasoning in their own words, with a one-sentence plan before multi-step problems.

**Basis:** self-explanation was rated moderate utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013); Chi et al. (1989), *Self-Explanations: How Students Study and Use Examples in Learning to Solve Problems*. The plan gate adapts the explore-plan-solve-review phases of CueThinkEF+, a technology-based platform that guides students through a four-phase problem-solving process with supports for working memory, inhibitory control and cognitive flexibility (AERDF; American Institutes for Research independent evaluation, 2023–24, which found early but not statistically significant effects). The paper-only gate is a project adaptation, not the tested platform.

## EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation
**Targets:** facts, definitions or cause-effect statements learners must remember, where a why/how question has a precise answer in the lesson.

**Allowed edits:**
- Turn a telling step into asking-then-telling: "Why does X happen?" or "How does X cause Y?" — learners answer from the lesson, then the educator states the precise answer.

**Non-negotiables:** questions are precise why/how questions with answers in the lesson; learners attempt first; the precise answer is stated straight after.

**Never:** vague "discuss" or open opinion prompts; questions whose answers aren't in the lesson; leaving answers unstated.

**Already present when:** the unit already asks precise why/how questions and then states the answers.

**Basis:** elaborative interrogation was rated moderate utility in Dunlosky et al. (2013); Woloshyn, Willoughby, Wood and Pressley (1990), *Elaborative interrogation facilitates adult learning of factual paragraphs*.

## Guardrails (every card)
- **EB-G1 All non-negotiables or abstain.** A card is applied with every one of its non-negotiables, or not at all.
- **EB-G2 Augment, don't replace.** A practice is added to existing teaching. It never removes or shortens skill practice, a mastery check, or sustained writing or reasoning, and the milestone and `macro_alignment` stay the same.
- **EB-G3 Low stakes, no records.** Quizzes, checks and reviews are never graded, collected, recorded or displayed by name. No ranking, no name lists.
- **EB-G4 No outcome claims in steps.** Steps describe what to do, never what it achieves. The **Basis** lines are for reviewers; never copy a claim, number or "research shows" into a step.
- **EB-G5 Offline and zero-cost.** Scrap paper, slates, chalk, fingers or cards made from reused paper. No devices, printing or purchases.
- **EB-G6 Fits the time.** The edit fits the unit's declared weeks and periods; a review or check takes minutes, not a lesson.
