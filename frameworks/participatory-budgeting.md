# Participatory Budgeting cards (PB-01 to PB-05)

The cards the Participatory Budgeting stage (`prompts/31-participatory-budgeting.md`) may apply to an MLS unit, and the critic (`prompts/50-critic.md`) checks against. They come from the requirements' section on PB framework cards. A technique that isn't on a card, or isn't in the source, may not be added.

PB cards are used "purely as a mechanism to drive civic agency, practical mathematical reasoning (resource allocation), and student voice". They are not a lever on test scores, and no edit may claim they are.

---

## The fit-gate (decide this first)
PB applies only when the unit has **a real, tangible or analytical resource trade-off that the students or the class must decide**: a fixed amount (a number, time, space or a set of items) and more options than fit, or competing proposals where only some can be chosen. If there's no such trade-off, and no card's target step is in the unit, the answer is **ABSTAIN**.

**Always skip PB for:**
- **Foundational decoding and fluency drills** (sound-symbol practice, phonics, reading fluency, number-fact drills). A vote there adds friction and breaks the drill.
- **The educator's or school leaders' own administrative tasks** (lesson reviews, approvals, record-keeping). These can't be handed to a student ballot without distorting who is accountable.
- **Decisions that aren't the class's to make** (school rules, staffing, timetables, grades), except as a clearly labelled mock exercise.
- **A step where a vote would replace sustained individual work**, such as writing, proof or reasoning.

## PB-01 Knapsack Ledger
**Category:** practical math and data (contextualised numeracy).

**Targets:** arithmetic, percentages, data-handling or optimisation practice that uses generic word problems.

**Allowed edits:**
- Give pairs a paper mock ledger: one fixed, made-up budget and 5 to 8 options, each with a made-up cost. Pairs choose a set whose total fits under the cap, writing the sum, and say what they gave up.
- Have pairs compare two different sets that both fit, using the unit's own skill (e.g. percentages of the budget used).

**Never:** use real money, a real school budget, or students' or families' own spending; use one locality's real prices; let the ledger replace the unit's skill practice. The ledger **is** the practice.

**Already present when:** the unit already has students choose among costed options under a fixed total.

## PB-02 Civic Decision Cycle
**Category:** civics, history and social studies.

**Targets:** a lecture on governance, decision-making or community needs that has no decision activity of its own.

**Allowed edits:**
- Map the unit's existing steps onto the cycle **Idea Collection → Feasibility Vetting → Ballot → Report-back**, for a mock decision about class-level or simulated community priorities. Vetting checks each idea against criteria stated in advance (cost, time, who it helps).
- Fold one stage of the cycle into an existing step, or add one step for a missing stage.

**Never:** make real decisions about school policy, staff or other students; have students lobby or petition real officials; ask students to campaign against each other.

**Already present when:** the unit already collects ideas, vets them and decides among them.

## PB-03 Counting Methods
**Category:** civics, and math on fairness.

**Targets:** a step with a vote, or a lesson about elections, representation or fairness.

**Allowed edits:**
- Count a mock vote by approval voting (each student marks every option they accept) and compare the result with a single-choice count.
- In a High grade band, work through the Method of Equal Shares on paper with small made-up numbers, and compare it with approval voting.

**Never:** count votes about individual students; show who voted for what; use a method the unit doesn't then explain.

**Already present when:** the unit already compares two ways of counting.

## PB-04 Shared Resource Round
**Category:** managing a shared resource.

**Targets:** a step where the class shares a real non-monetary resource (donated books, project materials, time slots, a space) and the educator would otherwise decide alone.

**Allowed edits:**
- Run a structured show-of-hands priority round on the options, or a turn order where every group gets one pick before any group gets a second.

**Never:** distribute money or anything bought for the purpose; distribute a resource that isn't the class's; let a round favour popular students or let the same group always choose first.

**Already present when:** the unit already shares the resource by a stated, fair rule.

## PB-05 Report-back
**Category:** every PB step.

**Targets:** any step with a vote, ballot, ranking or allocation.

**Allowed edits:**
- After the count, the educator writes on chart paper the result, the counting rule used, and what happens next. Fold this into the counting step where it fits.

**Never:** report individual votes or who chose what.

**Already present when:** the unit already announces or displays the result and the rule.

## Guardrails (every card)
- **PB-G1 Numbers only.** Students never handle, collect, count or track real money. Budgets are made-up figures on paper.
- **PB-G2 Rule first.** The counting or allocation rule is announced before anyone votes or chooses.
- **PB-G3 The class's own decisions.** A ballot decides only something that is genuinely the class's to decide, or is clearly labelled a mock exercise.
- **PB-G4 Options, not people.** Votes are on options, never on students. Tallies are class totals, with no names attached.
- **PB-G5 Learning unchanged.** A vote or ledger never replaces the unit's skill practice or sustained individual work, and the milestone and `macro_alignment` stay the same.
- **PB-G6 No outcome claims.** Don't claim PB raises scores or learning. Its purpose is civic agency, mathematical reasoning and student voice.
- **PB-G7 Offline, generic, zero-cost.** Ledgers, ballots and tallies are on paper or chalk, with made-up generic figures, not one locality's prices.
- **PB-G8 Everyone takes part.** Every student can vote or choose, by show of hands, tally marks or a paper slip, and nobody is required to speak or campaign.
