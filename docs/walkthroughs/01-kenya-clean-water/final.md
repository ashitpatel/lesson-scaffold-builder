==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 6 Science and Technology - Strand 3: Clean Water and
Health
==============================================================================
Goal: Learners explain how water becomes unsafe and describe and demonstrate
safe ways to treat and store drinking water.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-WATER-01          How Germs Get into Water (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no
                      earlier unit needed).
MLS-WATER-02          Filtering and Disinfecting Water (3 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-03          Clean Hands and Covered Containers (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-04          Writing a Safe-Water Guide for a Younger Child (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-01: How Germs Get into Water
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives learners the explanation of how water becomes unsafe, and shows
them that clear-looking water can still carry germs, as a class chart that
later units build on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain to the class that water can look clear and still
                      carry germs that cause diarrhoea, typhoid and cholera.
                      Germs get into water from human and animal waste, from
                      dirty containers and from hands that touch the water.
                      Water from rivers, lakes, open wells and uncovered roof
                      tanks is most at risk. Write these three ways on chart
                      paper under the heading 'How germs get into water'.

2. Lead Educator      Explain that safely collected water can become unsafe
[changed: EB-04]      again in an open bucket or when scooped out with a cup
                      hands have touched. Ask if scooping with a cup is safe:
                      all learners at once show one finger for yes, two for
                      no. Look at every hand; if many show one, explain again.
                      Add a second chart column, 'How to stop germs': wash
                      hands, cover containers, and pour or use a long-handled
                      ladle instead of scooping with a cup.

3. Lead Educator      With the class chart covered, each learner writes from
[changed: EB-01]      memory three ways germs get into drinking water and
                      three ways to stop them in their exercise book. Then
                      uncover the chart so each learner checks their own list
                      against it. Nothing is collected or marked.

4. Lead Educator      Before the next lesson, collect water from two or three
                      different water sources you can reach near the school
                      (for example a tap, a stored container, a well, or a
                      stream, pond, river or lake) in clean jars with lids, so
                      each group gets two or three jars. Wipe the outside of
                      each jar dry and close the lids tightly. Mark each lid
                      with where the water came from.

5. Peer Facilitator   In groups, learners compare jars by colour and smell,
[changed: CL-02]      swapping roles for each jar: the Holder holds it to the
                      light, the Smeller opens the lid only to smell, and the
                      Reporter tells the results, which the educator writes in
                      a class table on chart paper. Learners do not taste the
                      water or put their fingers in it. Afterwards, every
                      learner washes their hands with soap and water, and the
                      educator closes the jars and pours the water away.

6. Lead Educator      Use the class table to explain that colour and smell
                      cannot show germs: water can look clear and smell clean
                      and still make us ill. Add this line to the 'How germs
                      get into water' chart. Keep the chart for the next
                      units.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: The educator can safely reach the lake shore to collect water before
  the lesson.
  If not: Collect the jars from any open water source you can reach safely,
  such as a river, an open well or an uncovered roof tank.
- Needs: There are enough clean jars with lids to give each group two or
  three.
  If not: Use clear plastic bottles with caps instead, or give each group one
  jar and have the groups swap jars during the same lesson.
- Needs: Chart paper is available, and the chart can be kept for the later
  units.
  If not: Write the chart on the back of a used calendar or a flattened cement
  or flour bag, and keep it on the wall.
- Needs: Two weeks of lessons run without a break.
  If not: If a week is lost, do steps 1 and 2 in one period and steps 5 and 6
  in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: during the explanation of
  how stored water becomes unsafe, every learner answers one question with
  fingers at the same time, and the educator looks at every hand and explains
  again if many are wrong; the chart column and its three ways are unchanged
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: learners write the three ways in
  and three ways to stop germs from memory with the chart covered, then
  uncover it and check their own list; nothing is collected or marked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: the single fixed group
  reporter becomes three named roles (Holder, Smeller, Reporter) that swap
  with each jar. The no-taste, no-fingers and lid-only-to-smell wording is
  kept, the educator still writes the class table, and the step now ends with
  every learner washing their hands with soap and water while the educator
  closes the jars and pours the untreated water away.

MLS-WATER-02: Filtering and Disinfecting Water
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Has learners build a filter and watch boiling and sunlight disinfection,
so they can describe and demonstrate how to treat water and say why filtering
alone is not enough.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: point to the class chart
[changed: EB-04]      showing how germs get into water and that clear water
                      can still carry germs. Explain that filtering removes
                      mud and some dirt but not all germs. Then every learner
                      answers at once by holding up fingers: one if filtered
                      water is safe to drink, two if it still needs more
                      treatment. Look at all the hands, and explain again if
                      many are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Before the lesson, cut the bottom off a plastic bottle
                      for each group and one for yourself, out of the
                      learners' reach. Turn your bottle upside down and fill
                      it with layers of cloth, fine sand, coarse sand and
                      small stones. Pour muddy water through it into a jar,
                      then hold up the water from before and after for the
                      class to compare.

3. Peer Facilitator   In groups, learners build their own filter in the bottle
                      the educator cut, layering cloth, fine sand, coarse sand
                      and small stones. Learners do not touch the blade or the
                      cut edge of the bottle. Each group pours muddy water
                      through its filter and compares the water before and
                      after.

4. Lead Educator      Explain that filtered water still needs to be
                      disinfected before drinking, and that boiling water for
                      one minute kills germs. Take the whole class together to
                      the stove and stay with all the groups. Boil water in a
                      kettle for one minute and pour it into a cup yourself so
                      learners see the steam. Learners stand back and do not
                      touch the kettle, stove, cup or hot water.

5. Lead Educator      Explain that sunlight also kills germs when clear
                      bottles of filtered water lie in full sun for at least
                      six hours. Early in the school day, take the whole class
                      outside together and stay with all the groups. Each
                      group lays a clear bottle of its filtered water on a
                      sheet of iron on the ground in full sun. Learners stay
                      off the roof and do not touch the iron sheet.

6. Lead Educator      After at least six hours of full sun, take the whole
                      class back outside together and stay with all the
                      groups. Lift the bottles off the sheet yourself, while
                      learners stand back and do not touch the hot iron sheet.
                      Back in class, add 'filter, then boil or leave in full
                      sun' to the 'How to stop germs' column of the chart.

7. Lead Educator      In week 3, open two lessons with three questions
[changed: EB-01]      answered from memory on scrap paper, with the chart
                      covered and exercise books closed: what a filter
                      removes, why filtered water is not yet safe to drink,
                      and how long to boil water. Then show the answers so
                      each learner checks their own. Nothing is collected or
                      marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Used plastic bottles, cloth, fine sand, coarse sand, small stones and
  muddy water are available for each group.
  If not: If there are fewer bottles than groups, two groups share one filter
  and take turns layering and pouring.
- Needs: There is a kettle, a stove and fuel at the school for one boiling
  demonstration.
  If not: Boil the water in a pot on the school kitchen fire, with the same
  rules: the educator alone handles the pot and the hot water, and learners
  stand back. If there is no heat source, describe boiling for one minute and
  do the sunlight method as the demonstration.
- Needs: A day with at least six hours of full sun, and a sheet of iron,
  during the unit's weeks.
  If not: If there is no iron sheet, use a clean, flat surface at ground level
  in full sun. If no full-sun day comes, show the prepared bottles in class
  and describe the method, keep boiling as the disinfection demonstration, and
  end the unit on time.
- Needs: Three weeks of lessons, which leaves slack for a lost week or a
  cloudy spell.
  If not: If a week is lost, do steps 1 and 2 in one period and step 3 in the
  next, and do steps 5 and 6 on the same day.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after explaining that
  filtering does not remove all germs, every learner answers at once with
  fingers, and the educator re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall in week 3 of
  what filtering does, why it is not enough and how long to boil, answered
  from memory and self-checked, nothing collected


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-03: Clean Hands and Covered Containers
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the safe-storage half of the goal: learners practise handwashing
at a tippy-tap and demonstrate covered storage and pouring or ladling instead
of scooping.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: point to 'hands that touch the
                      water' on the class chart. Explain that most germs reach
                      water on hands. Teach the six steps of handwashing with
                      soap and running water, showing each step yourself.

2. Lead Educator      Before the lesson, make a small hole near the cap of a
                      clean jerrycan. Take the whole class together to a spot
                      in the school grounds and stay with all the groups. Hang
                      the jerrycan from a stick frame and tie on a string foot
                      pedal so it tips without hands touching it. Learners
                      watch and do not touch the tool, the poles or the full
                      jerrycan.

3. Peer Facilitator   Each day before break, the whole class goes together to
                      the tippy-tap with the educator, who stays with all the
                      groups. Two learners on a daily rota call the groups in
                      turn, and each learner tips the jerrycan with the foot
                      pedal and does the six steps with soap. Learners do not
                      lift, refill or move the jerrycan or the frame; the
                      educator refills it.

4. Lead Educator      Explain that drinking water should be kept covered and
[changed: EB-04]      poured out or taken with a long-handled ladle, never
                      scooped out with a cup. Use a covered bucket of water to
                      show both safe ways, putting the cover back each time.
                      Then every learner shows at once with fingers which way
                      is unsafe: 1 pour, 2 ladle, 3 scoop with a cup. Look at
                      all the hands and, if many are wrong, show both ways
                      again.

5. Lead Educator      Before the group practice, put the covered bucket in
                      place. For each group, fill a small covered container,
                      such as a capped plastic bottle, from the bucket using
                      the long-handled ladle, and put the cover back on the
                      bucket.

6. Peer Facilitator   In groups, learners take turns getting a cup of water
                      from the covered bucket only with the long-handled
                      ladle, putting the cover back each time. To practise
                      pouring, each learner pours water from the group's
                      capped bottle into their cup and puts the cap back. No
                      hand or cup goes into the bucket. The bucket stays where
                      the educator put it, and learners do not lift or tip it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: There is soap at the school for daily handwashing.
  If not: Use any soap the school already keeps. If there is none, practise
  the six steps with running water at the tippy-tap and tell the class that
  the six steps are done with soap and running water whenever soap is
  available.
- Needs: A used jerrycan, sticks, string, water for refilling and a spot in
  the school grounds are available.
  If not: If there is only one jerrycan for a large class, the rota calls
  fewer groups each day so that every learner gets turns during the week.
- Needs: There is a bucket with a cover and a long-handled ladle, for example
  from the school kitchen.
  If not: Cover the bucket with a flat board or a plate. The educator fills a
  small covered container (a capped bottle, or a jug covered with a plate)
  from the bucket, and learners practise pouring from it into a cup. Learners
  do not lift or tip the bucket.
- Needs: School is open every day for the daily practice before break.
  If not: Practise on the days school is open, and do steps 4 to 6 in one
  period if a week is lost. Do not extend the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: the storage explanation
  and demonstration now end with a finger check that every learner answers at
  once; the educator looks at all hands and shows both safe ways again if many
  are wrong; nothing is recorded


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-04: Writing a Safe-Water Guide for a Younger Child
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Has each learner explain in writing, in their own one-page guide, how
water becomes unsafe, two ways to make it safe and why filtering alone is not
enough.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-02: point to the groups' filters,
                      recall the boiling and sunlight demonstrations, and show
                      the MLS-WATER-01 chart of how germs get into water.
                      Explain the task: each learner writes a one-page guide
                      for a younger child, 'How to make water safe to drink'.
                      Write its four parts on chart paper: how germs get in,
                      two ways to make water safe, why filtering alone is not
                      enough, and one labelled drawing.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each learner
                      drafts the one-page guide in their exercise book with
                      all four parts, including one labelled drawing.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, learners read each other's draft and check it
                      against the four parts on the chart. Each partner writes
                      one note on what is missing or wrong, then the partners
                      swap roles.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each learner to correct
                      the draft using the partner's note and write the final
                      copy on a clean page.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with a younger class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading their final guides aloud to a small group of
                      younger children and showing the labelled drawing.
                      Reading is a choice: a learner may lay the guide on the
                      desk for the children to look at, or keep it and just
                      listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each learner has an exercise book and a pencil for the draft, the
  drawing and the final copy.
  If not: Learners draft on a used sheet of paper and write the final copy in
  their exercise book.
- Needs: There are two full lessons for writing.
  If not: If a week is lost, do the partner check (step 3) in the first part
  of the period for the final copy (step 4), and give the rest of the period
  to writing.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: finished guides are read
  or shown to a younger class, the audience they were written for, in the
  classroom with the educator present; showing the guide is each learner's
  choice
