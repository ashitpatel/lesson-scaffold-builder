==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 6 Science and Technology - Strand 3: Clean Water and
Health
==============================================================================
Goal: Learners explain how water becomes unsafe and describe and demonstrate
safe ways to treat and store drinking water.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-WATER-01          How Water Becomes Unsafe (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no
                      earlier unit needed).
MLS-WATER-02          Filtering Water and Seeing Its Limits (2 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-03          Making Filtered Water Safe by Heat and Sunlight (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02.
MLS-WATER-04          Clean Hands, Safe Storage and a Guide for Others (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02,
                      MLS-WATER-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-01: How Water Becomes Unsafe
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the explanation of the three germ entry routes and three stops
that every later treatment and storage step relies on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that clear water can still carry germs that
[changed: EB-04]      cause diarrhoea, and name the three routes from the
                      chart: waste from people and animals, dirty
                      containers,like the shared rope-bucket at the baoli, and
                      hands touching water. Name the three stops from the
                      chart: covered containers, taking water with a
                      long-handled ladle(kalchhi) not a shared cup, and clean
                      hands. Show the three routes and three stops on chart
                      paper with chalk.

2. Lead Educator      Show two reused jars with lids like small matkas, one
                      covered and one left open. Pour clean water into both
                      with a long-handled ladle and leave them side by side on
                      the desk for the class to observe.

3. Lead Educator      Ask every learner to write the three ways germs get into
[changed: EB-04]      drinking water and three ways to stop them on slates at
                      the same time and hold them up. Look at all the slates
                      and re-explain any route or stop many miss before moving
                      on. Keep only a class-level tally on chart paper with
                      chalk.

4. Lead Educator      Ask each learner to copy the three entry routes and
                      three stops from the chart into their notebook. Check
                      notebooks during the lesson and correct errors on the
                      spot.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, ask three short questions on the three entry
[changed: EB-01]      routes and three stops, answered from memory on scrap
                      paper with the chart and notebooks put away. Show the
                      answers on the chart straight after so each learner
                      checks their own. Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper and chalk are available for the routes chart.
  If not: Use the existing board and learner notebooks instead; if a week is
  lost, combine steps 2 and 3 in one period and drop the tally.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: names and shows the three
  stops on the chart so the slate check asks only taught content
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: volunteer call-out becomes
  every learner answering at once on slates, with the educator looking at all
  answers and re-explaining misses
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: week-2 low-stakes recall of routes
  and stops from memory, self-checked, nothing collected


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-02: Filtering Water and Seeing Its Limits
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Teaches filtering with reused materials and proves filtered water still
needs disinfection, giving the first treatment method for the final guide.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: waste, containers and hands
[changed: EB-04]      leave germs that stay even when water looks clear.
                      Explain that filtering removes mud and some dirt but not
                      all germs, so filtered water must still be disinfected
                      before drinking. Then ask what stays after filtering,
                      with every learner holding up one finger meaning germs
                      from all three routes stay at the same time. Look at all
                      the answers and re-explain that filtering removes mud
                      only if many are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Show a filter the educator prepared before the lesson by
                      cutting a reused plastic bottle and layering cloth,such
                      as a folded cotton gamchha, fine sand, coarse sand and
                      small stones. Pour muddy water through it into a reused
                      jar and hold up the before and after jars side by side.
                      Learners watch from their desks and must not touch the
                      cut bottle edge or pick up the wet sand and stones.

3. Peer Facilitator   In small groups, fill pre-cut reused bottles already cut
                      by the educator with layers of cloth, fine sand, coarse
                      sand and small stones. Pour muddy water through the
                      group filter into a reused jar and compare it with
                      unfiltered water. Group members must not touch any
                      cutting tool or pick up more stones than the bowl
                      provides.

4. Lead Educator      Ask why filtered water alone is still not safe to drink,
[changed: EB-09]      pointing to the before and after jars, and have every
                      learner write their answer from the lesson on scrap
                      paper. Then state the precise answer: filtering removes
                      mud and some dirt but leaves germs that must still be
                      killed by disinfection. Each learner writes one sentence
                      in their notebook: filtered water still needs
                      disinfection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Reused plastic bottles, cloth, fine sand, coarse sand, small stones
  and muddy water are available.
  If not: Use soil from the school grounds and extra cloth layers if sand is
  missing; if a week is lost, combine the educator demo and group build in one
  period and shorten the notebook sentence.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: added every-learner finger
  check that germs from all three routes stay after filtering, with educator
  look and re-explain before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: turned telling into
  asking-then-telling: every learner writes why filtered water is unsafe, then
  educator states the precise answer


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-03: Making Filtered Water Safe by Heat and Sunlight
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Adds the two germ-killing steps, boiling and sunlight in clear bottles,
that turn filtered water into safe drinking water.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: clear water can still carry
[changed: EB-04]      germs from waste, containers and hands. Explain that
                      boiling for one minute and sunlight in clear bottles
                      both kill germs in filtered water. Every learner holds
                      up scrap paper naming both killing steps at the same
                      time, you look at all answers and re-explain any step
                      most miss before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Boil water in a kettle over dry khejri twigs on the
[changed: EB-08]      school stove yourself and hold up the steaming cup from
                      a safe distance for the class to see. Each learner tells
                      a neighbour why boiling the filtered water makes it
                      safe, then state that boiling kills the germs that
                      filtering leaves. Learners stay seated at their desks
                      and must not touch the stove, kettle or hot cup or pick
                      up the fuel.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to a sunny spot in the
                      school grounds on the open aangan in full dhoop and stay
                      with all the groups. Lay clear reused bottles of
                      filtered water flat on an iron sheet in full sun, and
                      show the six-hour rule on chart paper. Learners must not
                      touch the hot iron sheet or pick up stones and tools
                      lying nearby.

4. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together back to the sunny spot
                      after at least six hours in sun and stay with all the
                      groups. Lift the bottles yourself and hold them up for
                      the class to see. Learners must not touch the iron sheet
                      or pick up the bottles themselves.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: A school stove with fuel and at least one sunny day for bottles on an
  iron sheet.
  If not: If cloudy, keep bottles until the next sunny day within the two
  weeks and teach the six-hour rule on chart paper; if fuel is missing,
  demonstrate with the stove cold and describe boiling on the chart; if a week
  is lost, combine the boiling demo and bottle-laying in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every learner answers at
  once on scrap paper naming both killing steps, educator looks and
  re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-08 Self-Explanation: each learner explains to a neighbour
  why boiling makes filtered water safe, then educator confirms the precise
  reasoning


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-04: Clean Hands, Safe Storage and a Guide for Others
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02, MLS-WATER-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Keeps treated water safe through handwashing and covered storage, then
synthesises all methods into each learner's own tested one-page guide.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01, MLS-WATER-02 and MLS-WATER-03:
[changed: EB-04]      germs enter water through hands and containers,
                      filtering alone leaves germs, and heat and sunlight kill
                      them. Explain that clean hands and covered containers
                      keep treated water safe until drinking. Ask every
                      learner to write one safe and one unsafe storage act on
                      scrap paper and hold it up at the same time, look at all
                      the answers, and re-explain the unsafe ones if many are
                      wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Show the six handwashing steps with soap at a tippy-tap
                      the educator built before the lesson from a jerrycan,
                      sticks and string. Take the whole class together to the
                      tap and stay with all the groups while each learner
                      practises in turn. Learners must not touch the frame
                      joints or pick up sticks and stones nearby.

3. Lead Educator      Show a covered reused container and pour water out with
                      a long-handled ladle, then show an open bucket and a
                      shared cup as unsafe. Learners practise pouring in turn
                      while the whole class stays together and you stay with
                      all the groups. Learners must not touch the shared cup
                      or pick up the bucket to drink from it.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time for each
                      learner to draft a one-page guide for a younger child on
                      making water safe to drink. The draft must explain how
                      germs get into water, describe filtering plus one
                      killing step, say why filtering alone is not enough, and
                      add one labelled drawing.

5. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft against the three
                      required points and the labelled drawing. Each partner
                      writes one note on what is missing or unclear, then
                      partners swap roles.

6. Lead Educator      Give writing time for each learner to correct the draft
                      using the partner's note and write a final copy in their
                      notebook. Collect the final copies by stacking them on
                      the desk, with each learner's name only on their own
                      work.

7. Lead Educator      Stack the final copies together into one class book of
[changed: CL-04]      safe-water guides kept in the classroom, with the
                      educator present. Each learner places their own page or
                      keeps it, and a name stays only on its own page and may
                      be left off.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Soap, water, a jerrycan with sticks and string, covered containers
  and a long-handled ladle are available.
  If not: Practise the six steps with water only and mark the soap point on
  the chart; if a week is lost, combine handwashing and container steps in one
  period and keep the full drafting lesson unchanged.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every learner writes one
  safe and one unsafe storage act and holds it up at the same time, the
  educator looks at all answers and re-explains if many are wrong
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: final guides combined
  into one classroom book as a shared product
