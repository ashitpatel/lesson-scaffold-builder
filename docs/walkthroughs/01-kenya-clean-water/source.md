# Grade 6 Science and Technology - Strand 3: Clean Water and Health

*Teacher's guide for the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), Upper Primary. Suggested length: 6 weeks of 35-minute lessons.*

## 3.1 Why water can make us ill

Water can look clear and still carry germs that cause diarrhoea, typhoid and cholera. Germs get into water from human and animal waste, from dirty containers, and from hands that touch the water. Water from rivers, lakes, open wells and roof tanks with no cover is most at risk. Water that is collected safely can become unsafe again if it is stored in an open bucket or scooped out with a cup that hands have touched.

Learners should be able to name three ways germs get into drinking water and three ways to stop them.

## 3.2 The story of cholera in our county

Begin with a lecture on the history of cholera outbreaks in Kisumu County and around Lake Victoria, from the colonial period to the most recent outbreak, and on the work of the county health department. Learners copy the dates of the major outbreaks into their exercise books. Allow two lessons for this lecture.

## 3.3 Making water cleaner: filtering

Filtering removes mud and some dirt, but not all germs. Demonstrate a simple filter: cut the bottom off a plastic bottle, turn it upside down, and fill it with layers of cloth, fine sand, coarse sand and small stones. Pour muddy water through it and compare the water before and after. Then let groups of learners cut their own bottles with a razor blade and build their own filters.

Explain that filtered water still needs to be disinfected before drinking.

## 3.4 Making water safe: boiling and sunlight

Boiling water for one minute kills germs. Demonstrate with a kettle on a stove and let each learner take a turn pouring the boiling water into a cup to see the steam.

Solar disinfection (SODIS) uses sunlight: clear plastic bottles of filtered water are laid on a roof or a sheet of iron in full sun for at least six hours. Set up SODIS bottles on the school roof and ask two learners to climb up each afternoon to turn them.

For homework, each learner boils water at home and brings a bottle of it to school. Record in the class register which families treat their water and which do not, so the teacher can follow up with the chief at the next baraza.

## 3.5 Keeping hands and containers clean

Most germs reach water on hands. Teach the six steps of handwashing with soap and running water. Build a tippy-tap: a jerrycan with a small hole near the cap, hung from a stick frame and tipped with a foot pedal made of string, so hands never touch the container. Learners practise handwashing at the tippy-tap before break each day.

Containers should be covered, and water should be poured out or taken with a long-handled ladle, never scooped with a cup.

## 3.6 Testing water

Each group should walk to the lake shore after school to collect water samples in jars and bring them back for testing. The school should buy a water-testing kit for each group (about KSh 2,000 each). Groups compare the samples by colour and smell and record the results.

## 3.7 Culminating task

Each learner writes a one-page guide for a younger child: "How to make water safe to drink". The guide must explain how germs get into water, describe two ways of making water safe and say why filtering alone is not enough, and include one labelled drawing. Learners check their guide with a partner and correct it before writing the final copy.

The goal of the strand is for learners to explain how water becomes unsafe and to describe and demonstrate safe ways to treat and store drinking water.
