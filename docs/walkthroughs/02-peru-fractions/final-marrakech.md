==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Mathematics Grade 5 - Unit 4: Fractions in the Market and the
Garden
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils represent, compare and add fractions and use them to describe how
a whole is shared.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FRAC-01           What Fractions Are and Folding Equivalent Fractions (2
                      weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FRAC-02           Comparing and Adding Fractions (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-FRAC-01.
MLS-FRAC-03           Sharing the Garden Bed and Explaining the Shares (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-FRAC-01, MLS-FRAC-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-01: What Fractions Are and Folding Equivalent Fractions
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the meaning of numerator and denominator and the paper-strip
model of equivalent fractions that later comparing, adding and sharing work
uses.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a fraction names equal parts of a whole,
[changed: EB-04]      where the bottom number says how many equal parts and
                      the top number says how many are meant. Cut a root
                      vegetable drawing on the board into equal and unequal
                      pieces to show parts must be equal. Show 3/4 as three of
                      four equal parts. Every pupil holds up fingers for the
                      whole in two drawings, you look at all answers and
                      re-explain if many are wrong or move on.

2. Lead Educator      Each pupil folds one paper strip into halves, then
                      quarters, then eighths,as a baker cuts round khobz (pain
                      rond) for sharing and labels each part. Lay the folded
                      strips one above another to see that 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8.
                      Keep the strips for later lessons.

3. Lead Educator      Each pupil folds a second paper strip into thirds and
                      then sixths and labels each part. Compare the two strips
                      like quartered zellige squares seen around the medina,
                      to see equivalent parts across halves, quarters,
                      eighths, thirds and sixths. Keep both strips in
                      notebooks.

4. Lead Educator      Work four examples on the board of finding equivalent
[changed: EB-08]      fractions by multiplying the top and bottom numbers by
                      the same number. After each example every pupil turns to
                      a neighbour and says why multiplying top and bottom by
                      the same number keeps the same amount. State the precise
                      reason straight after each pair explains, then pupils
                      copy each example into their notebooks using the folded
                      strips to check.

5. Lead Educator      Pupils complete twelve practice exercises from the
                      textbook on equivalent fractions in their notebooks.
                      Check answers against the folded strips and the board
                      examples. Correct mistakes in class.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Paper strips, chalk, board and notebooks are available.
  If not: Reuse scrap paper cut into strips; if a week is lost, combine steps
  2 and 3 in one period and drop half the practice exercises.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers with
  fingers during the fraction explanation, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains or moves on
- Evidence-Based, EB-08 Self-Explanation: after each worked example every
  pupil explains to a neighbour why the step works, then educator states the
  precise reason


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-02: Comparing and Adding Fractions
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRAC-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the strip model into comparing fractions with the same top or
bottom number and adding fractions with the same bottom number.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRAC-01: use the folded paper strips.
                      Compare 3/8 and 5/8 with the same bottom number using
                      the strips to check which is larger. Record the
                      comparison in notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Compare 2/3 and 2/5 with the same top number using the
[changed: EB-09]      folded strips to check. Ask why two parts of thirds are
                      larger than two parts of fifths,as with a third of khobz
                      against a fifth pupils answer from the strips, then
                      state that the same number of parts is larger when the
                      whole is cut into fewer equal parts. Record the result
                      in notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      Explain how to add fractions with the same bottom number
[changed: EB-04]      by adding the top numbers and keeping the bottom number.
                      Show two examples on the board using the folded strips.
                      Every pupil answers one adding question on slates held
                      up at the same time, the educator looks at all slates
                      and re-explains if many are wrong, otherwise moves on.
                      Pupils copy the examples into notebooks.

4. Lead Educator      Pupils complete the twenty textbook exercises on
[changed: CL-04]      comparing and adding fractions in their notebooks. Check
                      answers with the folded strips. Correct mistakes in
                      class. Each pupil adds one tick on chart paper for each
                      correct answer to make one class total with no names.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Pupils kept their folded strips from MLS-FRAC-01.
  If not: Refold one strip quickly in class; if a week is lost, do half the
  textbook exercises and drop the rest.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: vague discuss-why becomes
  precise why-question answered from strips first, then the precise
  cutting-into-fewer-parts answer is stated
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers one
  adding question on slates during teaching, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains or moves on
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: added a nameless class
  total of correct answers on chart paper to the practice check


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-03: Sharing the Garden Bed and Explaining the Shares
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRAC-01, MLS-FRAC-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Uses fractions to describe how the 8-square garden bed is shared,
writing each share two ways and adding shares in a finished poster.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRAC-01 and MLS-FRAC-02: use the folded
                      strips and the method for adding shares with the same
                      bottom number. Look at the board drawing of the bed as a
                      grid of 8 squares, each 1 square metre. Name the minimum
                      shares: 2 squares for potatoes, 1 for beans, 3 for
                      maize.

2. Lead Educator      Whole class goes together to the bed behind the
                      classroom like those in medina house gardens, and the
                      educator stays with all groups. Look at the rectangle
                      bed and count its 8 square metres without picking
                      plants, touching tools, or lifting heavy objects. Return
                      together to the classroom.

3. Lead Educator      Rule first: vote by show of hands twice, once for each
[changed: EB-04,      spare square, most votes wins that square among the
PB-04]                three crops. Take both votes, keep the minimum shares,
                      and write the final 8-square totals on the board to sum
                      to 8/8. Each pupil writes each share as a fraction of
                      the bed on a slate, holds it up together, and checks the
                      total is the whole bed. The educator checks slates and
                      re-explains if many are wrong.

4. Lead Educator      The educator writes the result, the rule used and what
[changed: PB-05]      happens next on chart paper as class totals with no
                      names.

5. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet work time. Each pupil makes
                      a poster with drawings and numbers showing how the bed
                      was shared, writes each crop's share in two equivalent
                      ways, and adds two of the shares together.

6. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, take turns as Explainer and Checker to explain
[changed: CL-02]      the poster and check the two equivalent forms and the
                      added sum. Each partner writes one note on what is
                      missing or wrong, then partners swap roles and repeat.
                      Correct mistakes before the final copy, with each
                      pupil's name only on their own poster.

7. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      showing their final posters to a small group of
                      listeners. Showing is a choice: a student may lay the
                      poster on the desk for listeners to read, or keep it and
                      just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Access to the garden bed behind the classroom in dry weather.
  If not: Stay in the classroom and use only the board grid of 8 squares; if a
  week is lost, shorten poster time and combine steps 3 and 4, keeping the
  final copy.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: pupils show share
  fractions on slates at the same time and the educator re-explains if many
  are wrong before moving on
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added Explainer and Checker
  roles with swap in poster pair check
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final posters shown to
  another class as a real audience
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-04 Shared Resource Round: teacher decides alone
  replaced by rule-first show-of-hands round voting each of the 2 spare
  squares separately and writing the final 8-square totals summing to 8/8
  before slate fraction writing
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-05 Report-back: educator writes the result, the
  rule and next steps on chart paper as class totals with no names
