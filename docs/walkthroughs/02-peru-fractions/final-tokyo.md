==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Mathematics Grade 5 - Unit 4: Fractions in the Market and the
Garden
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils represent, compare and add fractions and use them to describe how
a whole is shared.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FRACT-01          What Fractions Mean and Equivalent Folds (2 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FRACT-02          Comparing and Adding Fractions with Market Shares (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-FRACT-01.
MLS-FRACT-03          Sharing the Garden Bed and Explaining the Shares (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-FRACT-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FRACT-01: What Fractions Mean and Equivalent Folds
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the meaning of numerator and denominator and the folding basis
for equivalent fractions used in all later sharing work.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a fraction names equal parts of one whole
[changed: EB-04]      strip of paper. Show that the bottom number counts the
                      equal parts and the top number counts the parts taken.
                      Show one strip cut into unequal pieces and state it is
                      not quarters because the parts are not equal. Each pupil
                      holds up a slate showing how many equal parts make
                      quarters, you look at all slates and re-explain if many
                      are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Give each pupil one paper strip of origami paper
[changed: CL-04]      familiar from craft time for folding. Each pupil folds
                      it into halves, then quarters, then eighths, and labels
                      each part. Pupils lay the folded strips in rows on one
                      class sheet to make one class chart showing that one
                      half equals two quarters equals four eighths.

3. Lead Educator      Give each pupil a second paper strip cut like a roasted
                      nori sheet for onigiri. Each pupil folds it into thirds
                      and then sixths and labels each part. Lay both sets of
                      strips out to compare the sizes of thirds and sixths.

4. Lead Educator      Work four examples on the board showing equivalent
[changed: EB-08,      fractions made by multiplying the top and bottom numbers
CL-02]                by the same number. After each example, in pairs one
                      partner explains why multiplying the top and bottom
                      keeps the amount the same while the other checks with
                      the folded strips, then they swap roles each example and
                      you state the reason. Pupils copy the examples and
                      complete practice exercises from the textbook in their
                      notebooks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Paper strips, notebooks, chalk and chart paper are available.
  If not: Reuse scrap paper cut into strips and copy the charts into
  notebooks; if a week is lost, combine thirds and sixths folding with the
  board examples in one period and drop half the practice exercises.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers on
  slates during the fraction-meaning explanation, answers are looked at and
  re-explained if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-08 Self-Explanation: after each equivalent-fraction
  example pupils explain to a neighbour why the step keeps the value, then the
  reason is confirmed
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: individual folded
  strips laid as one class chart of equivalent fractions
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: neighbour talk given as
  explainer and checker roles that swap each example


==============================================================================
MLS-FRACT-02: Comparing and Adding Fractions with Market Shares
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRACT-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds comparison and same-denominator addition so pupils can judge and
combine fractional shares of a whole.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRACT-01: bring the folded paper strips.
[changed: CL-03]      Compare three eighths and five eighths by laying the
                      strips side by side as with two kyushoku school-lunch
                      portions. Record on chart paper that the larger top
                      number gives the larger share when parts are equal. Say
                      that three squares and five squares of the eight-square
                      garden bed behind the classroom show the same
                      comparison.

2. Lead Educator      Compare two thirds and two fifths using paper strips and
[changed: EB-09]      the class chart. Ask why the two thirds piece is larger
                      when both show two parts taken, and have each pupil
                      answer from the strips before telling. State that with
                      the same top number the smaller bottom number means
                      fewer equal parts so each piece is larger, and mark it
                      on chart paper. Pupils copy the two comparisons into
                      their notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      Explain that fractions with the same bottom number add
[changed: EB-04,      by adding the top numbers, working two eighths examples
PB-01]                on the board. Each pupil holds up a slate showing one
                      sum, you look at all slates and re-explain if many are
                      wrong. In pairs, pupils use a made-up paper ledger of 8
                      points with six made-up options costed in eighths,
                      choosing a set fitting under 8 points, writing the sum,
                      and saying what they gave up.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Classroom board, chart paper and textbooks or notebooks for practice
  exercises.
  If not: Use copied board exercises instead of textbook pages; if a week is
  lost, teach both comparisons in one period and shorten practice to six
  exercises.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: same-numerator rule becomes
  asking-then-telling: pupils answer why from strips, then educator states the
  precise fewer-parts-larger-piece answer
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: addition examples add
  simultaneous scrap-paper answers checked during the lesson with
  re-explain-or-move-on before textbook practice
- Connected Learning, CL-03 Real-World Context Mapping: linked the
  three-eighths and five-eighths comparison to squares of the eight-square
  garden bed
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-01 Knapsack Ledger: textbook exercises replaced
  by made-up 8-point paper ledger with six options costed in eighths; pairs
  choose a fitting set, write the sum, and state what was given up, retaining
  the every-pupil slate check


==============================================================================
MLS-FRACT-03: Sharing the Garden Bed and Explaining the Shares
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRACT-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Applies representation, equivalence and addition to divide the garden
whole into crop shares and explain the division in each pupil's own poster.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRACT-02: use the class chart for adding
[changed: EB-04]      fractions with the same bottom number. Draw the garden
                      bed on the board as a grid of eight squares, each one
                      square metre. State that potatoes need at least two
                      squares, broad beans at least one, and maize at least
                      three. Each pupil holds up a slate showing the share for
                      two squares, look at all slates and re-explain if many
                      are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Decide the extra two squares beyond the minimum amounts
[changed: PB-04]      by structured show-of-hands: state first that the option
                      with most hands wins and each pupil gets one hand. Count
                      hands for each crop option with the class, then write
                      the winning shares, the rule used, and the planting
                      order on chart paper. Pupils copy the grid into their
                      notebooks and label each crop area. Each pupil writes
                      each crop share as a fraction of the eight squares.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the bed behind the
                      classroom and stay with all groups at the bed. Point out
                      the rectangle matching the board grid. Pupils must not
                      pick up tools, lift heavy objects, or pick plants.
                      Pupils confirm which part of the bed matches each crop
                      square on their copied grid.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet work time. Each pupil writes
                      each crop share as a fraction in two equivalent ways
                      using the folding rule. Each pupil adds two of the
                      shares with the same bottom number and checks the sum
                      with the grid drawn from the rooftop planters in trays.

5. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, each pupil explains their garden drawing and
                      numbers to a partner. Each partner checks the two
                      equivalent forms and the added sum against the grid.
                      Each pupil corrects mistakes and makes a final copy with
                      drawings and numbers.

6. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with three short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      writing equivalent shares and adding same-bottom shares,
                      answered from memory on scrap paper with the chart and
                      notebooks put away. Show the answers so each pupil
                      checks their own. Nothing is collected or marked.

7. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      showing their final posters and explaining one share to
                      a small group of listeners. Showing is a choice: a
                      student may lay the poster on the desk for listeners to
                      read, or keep it and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Access to the garden bed behind the classroom in the growing season.
  If not: Use a chalk grid of eight squares on the school ground or classroom
  floor instead of the bed; if a week is lost, do one garden visit instead of
  two and combine the equivalent-forms and addition work into one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil shows the
  share on a slate during garden teaching, answers checked before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of equivalent
  shares and same-bottom addition from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: finished garden posters
  shown to another class as a real audience, with showing optional
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-04 Shared Resource Round: educator-alone
  decision replaced by structured show-of-hands priority round with one hand
  per pupil for the extra squares, with PB-05 report-back folded into the same
  step: winning shares, rule used and planting order written on chart paper
