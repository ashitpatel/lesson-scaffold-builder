==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Mathematics Grade 5 - Unit 4: Fractions in the Market and the
Garden
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils represent, compare and add fractions and use them to describe how
a whole is shared.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FRAC-01           What a Fraction Is and Folding Equivalent Fractions (2
                      weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FRAC-02           Comparing and Adding Fractions (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-FRAC-01.
MLS-FRAC-03           Sharing the Garden Bed and the Fraction Poster (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-FRAC-01, MLS-FRAC-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-01: What a Fraction Is and Folding Equivalent Fractions
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives pupils a set of labelled paper strips and the multiplying rule, so
they can represent any fraction and write it in equivalent ways.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a fraction names equal parts of a whole.
                      The denominator says how many equal parts the whole is
                      cut into, and the numerator says how many of those parts
                      we mean. Draw a shape cut into four equal parts on the
                      board and shade three to show 3/4. Draw a potato cut
                      into one big piece and three small pieces, and explain
                      why it is not cut into quarters.

2. Lead Educator      Give each pupil three strips of reused paper of the same
                      length. Each pupil folds one strip into halves, one into
                      quarters and one into eighths. Pupils write the fraction
                      on each part, such as 1/2, 1/4 or 1/8.

3. Lead Educator      Pupils lay their three strips one above the other and
                      write 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8 in their notebook. Explain that
                      fractions covering the same length of the strip are
                      equivalent fractions.

4. Lead Educator      Each pupil folds two more strips of the same length, one
                      into thirds and one into sixths, and labels each part.
                      Pupils lay them one above the other and write the
                      equivalent fractions they see, such as 1/3 = 2/6.

5. Lead Educator      Work four examples on the board of finding an equivalent
[changed: EB-04]      fraction by multiplying the numerator and the
                      denominator by the same number, for example 2/3 =
                      (2×2)/(3×2) = 4/6. Then every pupil writes a fraction
                      equivalent to 3/5 on scrap paper and holds it up at the
                      same time. Look at all the answers, and re-explain the
                      rule with one more example if many are wrong before
                      moving on. Answers are not recorded and no pupil is
                      named.

6. Lead Educator      Pupils work in their notebooks through twelve
                      equivalent-fraction exercises that the educator writes
                      on the board, or takes from the class textbook if it has
                      them, such as "write two fractions equivalent to 1/2,
                      2/3, 3/4 and 1/5". Each pupil keeps their labelled
                      strips inside their notebook for the next unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Enough reused paper to give each pupil five strips of the same
  length.
  If not: Cut strips from the backs of old worksheets or newspaper margins, or
  have pupils draw and divide strips of equal length in their notebooks
  instead of folding.
- Needs: Pupils have the textbook with the twelve equivalent-fraction
  exercises.
  If not: Copy the twelve exercises onto the board in chalk for pupils to copy
  into their notebooks.
- Needs: Two full school weeks are available without a holiday or closure.
  If not: If a week is lost, do steps 4 and 5 in one period and set six of the
  twelve exercises, so the unit still ends within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the four worked
  examples, every pupil writes and holds up a fraction equivalent to 3/5 on
  scrap paper; the educator looks at all answers and re-explains with one more
  example if many are wrong, with nothing recorded and no one named


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-02: Comparing and Adding Fractions
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRAC-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Uses the folded strips to let pupils compare fractions and add fractions
with the same denominator, the skill the final poster needs to add two crop
shares.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRAC-01: each pupil takes out their
                      labelled strips for halves, quarters, eighths, thirds
                      and sixths. Pupils compare 3/8 and 5/8 on the eighths
                      strip and write 3/8 < 5/8 in their notebook. Explain
                      that when the denominator is the same, the fraction with
                      the bigger numerator is bigger.

2. Lead Educator      Pupils compare 2/3 and 2/5, which have the same
                      numerator. Each pupil folds one more strip of the same
                      length into fifths and lays it under the thirds strip to
                      check. Pupils write 2/3 > 2/5 in their notebook. Explain
                      that when the numerator is the same, the fraction with
                      the smaller denominator is bigger because its parts are
                      larger.

3. Lead Educator      Explain how to add fractions with the same denominator:
[changed: EB-04]      add the numerators and keep the denominator. Show 3/8 +
                      2/8 = 5/8 on the board. Then every pupil writes the
                      answer to 1/6 + 4/6 on scrap paper and holds it up at
                      the same time. Look at all the answers without naming
                      anyone, and explain the rule again with a new example if
                      many are wrong before moving on.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson for pupils to complete in their
                      notebooks twenty exercises the educator writes on the
                      board, or takes from the class textbook if it has them:
                      ten comparing fractions with the same denominator or the
                      same numerator (e.g. 2/7 and 5/7, 3/4 and 3/8), and ten
                      adding fractions with the same denominator (e.g. 2/9 +
                      5/9).

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open two lessons with three questions
[changed: EB-01]      answered from memory on scrap paper, with strips,
                      notebooks and textbooks put away: one comparing
                      fractions with the same denominator, one comparing
                      fractions with the same numerator, and one adding
                      fractions with the same denominator. Then show the
                      answers so each pupil checks their own. Nothing is
                      collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Pupils still have their labelled strips from MLS-FRAC-01.
  If not: Pupils refold the eighths and thirds strips from reused paper at the
  start of step 1.
- Needs: Pupils have the textbook with the twenty exercises on page 84.
  If not: Copy the exercises onto the board in chalk for pupils to copy into
  their notebooks.
- Needs: Two full school weeks are available without a holiday or closure.
  If not: If a week is lost, do steps 1 and 2 in one period and set ten of the
  twenty exercises.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the adding example,
  every pupil answers one addition on scrap paper at the same time; the
  educator looks at all answers and re-explains if many are wrong, with no
  names recorded or called out
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of the two
  comparing rules and the adding rule from memory in week 2, answers shown
  straight after and self-checked, nothing collected


==============================================================================
MLS-FRAC-03: Sharing the Garden Bed and the Fraction Poster
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FRAC-01, MLS-FRAC-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Applies representing, equivalent fractions and adding to describe how
the class garden bed is shared, ending in each pupil's own corrected poster.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FRAC-01: with notebooks and strips put
[changed: EB-01]      away, pupils write from memory on scrap paper an
                      equivalent fraction for 3/4, then, from MLS-FRAC-02, the
                      bigger of 2/3 and 2/5, and 2/8 + 3/8. Show the answers
                      and the rules behind them, and each pupil checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected or marked.

2. Lead Educator      Draw the garden bed on the board as a grid of 8
                      one-metre squares, 4 long and 2 wide. Write on the board
                      that this term the class can plant potatoes (at least 2
                      squares), broad beans (at least 1) and maize (at least
                      3), and the other 2 can go to any crop. If the class has
                      no garden bed, say it is an imagined bed and a practice
                      decision; the shares and poster stay the same.

3. Lead Educator      Say the rule before voting: each pupil raises one hand
[changed: PB-04]      for one crop; most hands gets the first free square,
                      second-most gets the second, and ties are re-voted.
                      Count class totals, with no names. Write the totals, the
                      rule and each crop's squares for planting on chart
                      paper, and label the grid. Pupils write each crop's
                      share as a fraction of the bed in their notebook, for
                      example 3/8 for 3 squares of maize.

4. Lead Educator      Give two full lessons for each pupil to make a draft
                      poster on chart paper or reused paper, with their name
                      on it. The poster explains with drawings and numbers how
                      the bed was shared. It writes each crop's share as a
                      fraction in two equivalent ways and adds two of the
                      shares together.

5. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, each pupil explains their draft poster to
                      their partner. The partner checks that each share is out
                      of 8 squares, that the two equivalent fractions match,
                      and that the sum is correct, and points out any mistake.
                      Then partners swap roles.

6. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson for each pupil to correct the
                      mistakes their partner found and make the final copy of
                      their poster.

7. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute visit by a younger class to the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pupils lay their
                      final posters on their desks, and pairs take turns
                      explaining to small groups of visitors how the class
                      garden bed was shared. Showing a poster is each pupil's
                      choice: a pupil may keep it put away and walk round with
                      the visitors instead, and any pupil may fold their name
                      out of sight.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Enough chart paper or large reused sheets for one poster per pupil.
  If not: Pupils make the poster on a double page of their notebook or on the
  back of an old sheet of paper.
- Needs: The garden bed is the size the source describes (4 metres by 2
  metres).
  If not: The activity runs on the board grid, so keep the 8-square grid as
  described; no visit to the bed is needed.
- Needs: Two full school weeks are available without a holiday or closure.
  If not: If a week is lost, do steps 1 to 3 in one period and give one lesson
  instead of two for the draft poster in step 4.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: the spoken reminder of earlier
  rules becomes three questions answered from memory with notebooks and strips
  put away, followed straight away by the answers for self-checking, with
  nothing collected or marked; the garden-bed grid drawing is kept unchanged
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: finished posters shown
  and explained to a visiting younger class in the classroom, with the
  educator present; showing a poster is optional
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-04 Shared Resource Round: the educator deciding
  the 2 free squares alone is replaced by a show-of-hands priority round on
  the crops, with the rule said before voting and class totals only; the PB-05
  report-back is folded into the same counting step (totals, rule and each
  crop's squares on chart paper, with no individual votes); pupils still write
  each crop's share as a fraction
