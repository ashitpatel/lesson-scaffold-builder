# Matemática 5.º grado - Unit 4: Fractions in the Market and the Garden

*Teacher's guide for primary mathematics, MINEDU curriculum, UGEL Cusco edition. Suggested length: 6 weeks of 45-minute sessions.*

## 4.1 What a fraction is

A fraction names equal parts of a whole. The bottom number (the denominator) says how many equal parts the whole is cut into; the top number (the numerator) says how many of those parts we mean. In 3/4 the whole is cut into four equal parts and we take three. The parts must be equal: a potato cut into one big piece and three small pieces is not cut into quarters.

## 4.2 A short history of measuring

Open the unit with a lecture on how the Inca measured land and kept accounts on knotted cords (quipus), and how the metric system arrived later. Pupils copy the drawing of a quipu from the board. Spend two sessions on this lecture.

## 4.3 Folding fractions

Each pupil folds a strip of paper into halves, then quarters, then eighths, labelling each part. Pupils lay the strips one above the other to see that 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8. This is the basis for equivalent fractions. Pupils then fold a second strip into thirds and sixths. Work four examples on the board of finding equivalent fractions by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same number, then give pupils twelve practice exercises from the textbook.

## 4.4 Comparing and adding fractions

Pupils compare fractions with the same denominator (3/8 and 5/8) and then with the same numerator (2/3 and 2/5), using their folded strips to check. Explain how to add fractions with the same denominator: add the numerators and keep the denominator. Pupils complete the twenty exercises on page 84.

## 4.5 Fractions in the market

Tell pupils to bring S/ 5 from home on Friday. The class walks to the San Pedro market with the teacher, and each pupil buys a quarter kilo or a half kilo of potatoes or quinoa and checks the weight. Back at school, pupils write the price they paid as a fraction of S/ 5.

For homework, pupils ask their parents how much money the family earned from the last harvest and what fraction of it went on food, and bring the answer to class.

## 4.6 Sharing the school garden bed

The class has its own garden bed, a rectangle 4 metres long and 2 metres wide, behind the classroom. This term the class can plant three crops: potatoes, broad beans and maize, but the bed is not big enough for as much of each as pupils would like. Draw the bed on the board as a grid of 8 squares, each 1 square metre. Explain that potatoes need at least 2 squares, broad beans at least 1 and maize at least 3, and the rest can go to any crop. The teacher decides how many squares each crop gets and pupils write each crop's share as a fraction of the bed.

## 4.7 Checking progress

At the end of each week, give a ten-question test and write each pupil's score on the class wall chart next to their name, so that pupils can see who is ahead.

## 4.8 Culminating task

Each pupil makes a poster that explains, with drawings and numbers, how the garden bed was shared, writes each crop's share as a fraction in two equivalent ways, and adds two of the shares together. Pupils explain their poster to a partner and correct any mistakes before the final copy.

The goal of the unit is for pupils to represent, compare and add fractions and to use them to describe how a whole is shared.
