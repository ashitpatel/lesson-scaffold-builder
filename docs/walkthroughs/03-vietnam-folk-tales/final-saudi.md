==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Literature Grade 6 - Unit 2: Folk Tales and Retelling
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils recognise the structure and character types of folk tales, retell
a tale in their own words, and write a tale of their own.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FOLK-01           Reading and Mapping Two Folk Tales (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FOLK-02           Retelling a Folk Tale Aloud (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-FOLK-01.
MLS-FOLK-03           Collecting and Writing a Folk Tale (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-FOLK-01.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-01: Reading and Mapping Two Folk Tales
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds recognition of the three-part structure and character types
through mapped reading of the two tales, grounding later retelling and
writing.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a folk tale has a beginning with a
[changed: EB-04]      character and a problem, a middle with three events, and
                      an ending where good is rewarded, with type characters
                      and sometimes a helper. Ask two questions on the parts
                      that every pupil answers at the same time on slates,
                      look at all the slates, and re-explain any part many
                      miss. Read aloud the tale of the fisherman with the
                      magic pot as mere invention while pupils follow in
                      textbooks.

2. Lead Educator      Pupils read the fisherman tale silently. Each pupil
                      fills a story map in their notebook: characters,
                      problem, three main events, and ending.

3. Lead Educator      Read aloud the tale of the clever rabbit escaping the
                      tiger. Pupils then read it silently in their textbooks.

4. Lead Educator      Each pupil fills a second story map in their notebook
                      for the rabbit tale: characters, problem, three main
                      events, and ending. Keep both maps for later use.

5. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, within their class group pupils discuss what
                      kind of person each main character is, using what the
                      character says and does, as in Kalila wa Dimna tales, as
                      evidence. Pairs compare the fisherman and the rabbit
                      tales.

6. Lead Educator      Each pupil writes two sentences in their notebook about
                      the lesson each story teaches, with one quotation from
                      the text to support it.

7. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      the three parts and character types, answered from
                      memory on scrap paper with books and maps put away. Show
                      the answers straight after so each pupil checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Textbooks with both tales, notebooks, and chart paper or chalkboard
  are available.
  If not: Copy passages on the chalkboard and use notebooks only; combine the
  two story maps into one period and drop the class table if a week is lost.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers two
  questions on slates during the structure explanation, educator looks at all
  answers and re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: week-2 recall of parts and
  character types from memory on scrap paper, answers shown straight after,
  nothing collected or marked


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-02: Retelling a Folk Tale Aloud
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns mapped reading into fluent oral retelling in pupils' own words,
meeting the retelling half of the macro objective.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: use the two story maps of the
                      fisherman and rabbit tales. Each pupil chooses one tale
                      and plans a retelling in their own words in under three
                      minutes, using only the map without the book.

2. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils take turns practising the retelling
                      aloud from memory, keeping to under three minutes. Each
                      partner listens while the other retells.

3. Peer Facilitator   Each pupil retells the chosen tale to the class in their
                      own words in under three minutes, without the book and
                      using only the story map.

4. Lead Educator      The whole class goes together to the celebration space
                      with the educator staying with all groups; chosen pupils
                      retell their tale again at the seasonal school
                      celebration while the class listens as audience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Classroom space for retelling and a slot at the seasonal school
  celebration are available.
  If not: Retell in the classroom only; combine practice and class retelling
  into one period and drop the celebration repeat if a week is lost.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-03: Collecting and Writing a Folk Tale
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns structure, character types, and a collected home tale into each
pupil's own written folk tale, meeting the writing half of the macro
objective.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: use the two story maps of the
                      fisherman and rabbit tales and the three-part structure.
                      Each pupil asks a grandparent or older family member at
                      home to tell a folk tale, such as one shared on a
                      Ramadan evening, and writes it down in their notebook.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each pupil
                      starts a folk tale of 400 to 600 words with a beginning,
                      a middle with three events, and an ending, using at
                      least one character type in their notebook.

3. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson of quiet writing time. Each
                      pupil finishes the draft in their notebook, checking the
                      three parts and three middle events are present.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils read each other's draft and suggest one
                      improvement about structure, character type, or ending.
                      Partners swap roles so each draft gets one note.

5. Lead Educator      Give writing time for each pupil to revise the draft
                      using the partner's note and write a final copy in their
                      notebook. Collect the final copies into a handwritten
                      class book kept in the classroom.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: A family storyteller is reachable at home and pupils have notebooks
  and writing time.
  If not: Use one of the two class tales instead of a home tale; combine the
  two drafting lessons into one longer session and shorten revision if a week
  is lost.
