==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Literature Grade 6 - Unit 2: Folk Tales and Retelling
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils recognise the structure and character types of folk tales, retell
a tale in their own words, and write a tale of their own.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FOLK-01           Reading and Mapping Two Folk Tales (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FOLK-02           Character, Lesson and Retelling Aloud (2 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-FOLK-01.
MLS-FOLK-03           Collecting and Writing a Folk Tale (3 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-FOLK-01.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-01: Reading and Mapping Two Folk Tales
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the structural foundation for the objective by giving every pupil
two completed story maps showing characters, problem, three events and ending
in both tales.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain briefly what a folk tale is: a story passed down
[changed: EB-04]      by word of mouth, with a beginning, a middle with three
                      events, and an ending, plus character types and a
                      magical helper or object. Ask two questions every pupil
                      answers at once on slates, then look at all slates and
                      re-explain any part many missed before moving on. Pupils
                      copy the three parts and two example character types
                      into notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Read aloud the fisherman tale from the textbook using
[changed: EB-03]      Icelandic names for the characters while pupils follow,
                      then pupils read it silently. Show the completed story
                      map on the board step by step, saying each part aloud:
                      characters, problem, three events, ending. Pupils copy
                      the completed map into notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      Read aloud the rabbit tale from the textbook while
                      pupils follow. Pupils then read it silently and fill the
                      same story map: characters, problem, three events,
                      ending.

4. Lead Educator      Lead pupils to complete both story maps on chart paper
                      as a class table. Check that each map lists characters,
                      problem, three events and ending.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each pupil has the textbook with both tales for silent reading.
  If not: Teacher reads each tale aloud and copies key passages on the board;
  pupils share books in pairs and complete one map per tale in the same weeks.
- Needs: Chart paper and chalk or board markers are available for the class
  story maps.
  If not: Keep the maps in notebooks only and drop the chart-paper copy; use
  one combined checking lesson for both tales.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers two
  questions on slates during the explanation and the educator re-explains
  missed parts before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: the fisherman tale
  story map is shown step by step as a complete worked example before pupils
  fill the rabbit tale map alone


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-02: Character, Lesson and Retelling Aloud
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Advances the objective from recognition to interpretation and speech by
turning the story maps into evidence-based character judgements and an unaided
three-minute retelling.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: use the class story maps of the
[changed: EB-04]      two tales. Ask what kind of person each main character
                      is with one piece of evidence, with every pupil writing
                      the answer on scrap paper and holding it up at the same
                      time. Look at all the answers and re-explain using the
                      story map if many are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Give writing time for each pupil to write two sentences
                      about the lesson each story teaches. Each pupil copies
                      one short quotation from the text to support the lesson.

3. Lead Educator      Give practice time for each pupil to retell one tale in
                      their own words in under three minutes without the book,
                      using only the story map. Use a rota so every pupil
                      practises aloud in class as practice toward a midwinter
                      storytelling evening for parents.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, take turns retelling the chosen tale aloud
                      without the book. The listener follows the story map and
                      names one part that was missing or unclear, then
                      partners swap roles.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      the two tales answered from memory on scrap paper, with
                      story maps and books put away. Show the answers on the
                      board so each pupil checks their own. Nothing is
                      collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Class periods allow a rota so every pupil practises a three-minute
  retelling.
  If not: Run paired retelling in one period instead of individual turns; drop
  the second tale and retell one tale only.
- Needs: Pupils can speak aloud in class without a special hall or event.
  If not: Keep all retelling inside normal lessons; shorten practice to two
  minutes per pupil and combine steps 3 and 4 in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers the
  character question at the same time on scrap paper, educator looks at all
  answers and re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: short week-2 recall of both tales
  from memory on scrap paper, answers shown for self-check, nothing collected


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-03: Collecting and Writing a Folk Tale
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Completes the objective by turning the three-part structure and
character types into each pupil's own drafted, peer-checked and revised tale
of 400 to 600 words.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: use the three-part structure
                      and character types. Explain the home task: ask a family
                      elder at home to tell a tale such as a saga episode or a
                      hidden-people account told with respect and write it
                      down in notebooks, without visiting strangers and
                      without writing personal details.

2. Lead Educator      Ask each pupil to plan their own tale of 400 to 600
                      words with a beginning, a middle with three events, and
                      an ending, using at least one character type. Pupils
                      write the plan as three event lines in notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      Give two full lessons of quiet writing time for each
                      pupil to draft the tale in notebooks. The draft must
                      follow the plan and include the chosen character type.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, take turns as reader and checker: one reads
[changed: CL-02]      the other's draft and suggests one improvement about
                      structure, events or character type, then partners swap
                      roles. Each partner writes the suggestion on the draft.

5. Lead Educator      Give writing time for each pupil to revise the draft
                      using the partner's suggestion and write a final copy in
                      notebooks. The pupil writes their name on their own
                      work.

6. Lead Educator      Place each final tale in one class book made of reused
[changed: CL-04]      paper covers tied with thread, with the educator present
                      in the classroom. Including a tale is a choice: a pupil
                      may keep the tale in the notebook instead, and a name
                      may be left off. Keep the book in the classroom for
                      pupils to read.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: A family elder at home can tell the pupil a tale.
  If not: Pupil uses one of the two textbook tales or a tale retold by the
  teacher; no pupil visits unfamiliar households.
- Needs: Two full lessons are available for drafting within the 3-week run.
  If not: If a week is lost, combine planning and start of drafting in one
  period and shorten revision to one period; drop the second draft lesson
  rather than extending the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: named reader and checker
  roles with swap for the existing pair check
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: combined final tales
  into one voluntary classroom book
