==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Literature Grade 6 - Unit 2: Folk Tales and Retelling
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils recognise the structure and character types of folk tales, retell
a tale in their own words, and write a folk tale of their own.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-FOLK-01           Folk Tale Structure and Story Maps (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-FOLK-02           Character and Lesson (2 weeks). Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
MLS-FOLK-03           Retelling Aloud and Collecting a Tale by Word of Mouth
                      (3 weeks). Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
MLS-FOLK-04           Writing and Revising Our Own Folk Tale (3 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-FOLK-01.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-01: Folk Tale Structure and Story Maps
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives pupils the three-part structure and the character types, then has
them apply both by mapping the two textbook tales.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a folk tale is a story passed down by word
                      of mouth for many generations before anyone wrote it
                      down. On chart paper, write its three parts: beginning
                      (a character and a problem), middle (a series of events,
                      often in threes) and ending (the problem is solved and
                      good is rewarded). Below the parts, list the character
                      types (kind youngest child, greedy elder brother, clever
                      animal) and the magical helper or object. Keep the chart
                      for later units.

2. Lead Educator      Ask two questions every pupil answers at once: which
[changed: EB-04]      part 'and the kind brother lived happily ever after'
                      belongs to, shown with one, two or three fingers for
                      beginning, middle or ending; and the character type of a
                      younger sister who shares her bread with a stranger,
                      written on scrap paper and held up. Look at all the
                      answers. If many are wrong, re-explain from the chart
                      before the reading; otherwise move on. Nothing is
                      recorded.

3. Lead Educator      Read aloud the textbook tale of the poor fisherman who
                      is given a magic pot and loses it through greed. Then
                      each pupil reads the same tale silently in the textbook.

4. Lead Educator      Each pupil draws a story map in their notebook with four
                      boxes: characters, problem, three main events, and
                      ending. Pupils fill it in for the fisherman tale,
                      looking back at the text.

5. Lead Educator      Read aloud the textbook tale of the clever rabbit who
                      escapes a tiger by trickery. Then each pupil reads the
                      same tale silently in the textbook.

6. Lead Educator      Each pupil draws a second four-box story map in their
                      notebook (characters, problem, three main events,
                      ending) and fills it in for the rabbit tale, looking
                      back at the text.

7. Lead Educator      With the class chart covered and textbooks closed, each
[changed: EB-01]      pupil marks the beginning, middle and ending on each of
                      their two story maps and writes the character type next
                      to each main character, from memory. Then uncover the
                      chart so each pupil checks and corrects their own maps.
                      Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Enough textbooks for pupils to read both tales silently.
  If not: Pupils share one textbook between two or three for the silent
  reading, and the educator reads each tale aloud a second time.
- Needs: Chart paper is available for the class chart of parts and character
  types.
  If not: Write the chart on one corner of the chalkboard and have each pupil
  copy it into the back of their notebook to use in later units.
- Needs: Two full weeks of lessons (a holiday or school event may take some of
  them).
  If not: Do steps 4 and 5 in one period, and do step 6 as the last part of
  that period, so the unit still ends within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: right after the structure
  and character types are explained, every pupil answers two questions at once
  (fingers, then scrap paper); the teacher looks at all the answers and either
  re-explains from the chart or moves on, and nothing is recorded
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: pupils name the parts and
  character types on their story maps from memory, with the chart covered and
  textbooks closed, then uncover the chart and check their own maps; nothing
  is collected or marked


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-02: Character and Lesson
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds on the character types by having pupils judge each main character
from what it says and does, then write the lesson each tale teaches, supported
by a quotation.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: pupils open their story maps
[changed: EB-04]      for both tales. Lead a class discussion on what kind of
                      person the fisherman is, using evidence from the story:
                      what he says and what he does. Then each pupil writes on
                      scrap paper one word for his character and key words
                      from a line showing it, and all hold them up together.
                      Look at every paper without collecting it; if many lack
                      evidence, reread a key line together.

2. Lead Educator      Lead the same class discussion for the rabbit: what kind
                      of character it is, using what it says and does in the
                      story. Pupils find the lines in the textbook that show
                      it.

3. Lead Educator      Give quiet writing time. Each pupil writes two sentences
                      in their notebook about the lesson the fisherman tale
                      teaches and copies one quotation from the text that
                      supports it.

4. Lead Educator      Give quiet writing time. Each pupil writes two sentences
                      in their notebook about the lesson the rabbit tale
                      teaches and copies one quotation from the text that
                      supports it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two full weeks of lessons.
  If not: Do steps 1 and 2 in one discussion period, and steps 3 and 4 in one
  writing period, so the unit still ends within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: After the fisherman
  discussion, every pupil writes a character trait and a supporting line on
  scrap paper and holds it up at the same time. The educator looks at all the
  answers without collecting them and rereads a key line with the class if
  many lack evidence.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-03: Retelling Aloud and Collecting a Tale by Word of Mouth
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns each pupil's story map into an oral retelling of one tale in their
own words, and has them write down a tale told to them by word of mouth.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: with the class chart covered
[changed: EB-02]      and notebooks closed, each pupil writes the three parts
                      of a folk tale and two character types from memory on
                      scrap paper, then the chart is uncovered so each pupil
                      checks their own; nothing is collected. Each pupil then
                      chooses one of the two tales and takes out its story
                      map. Pupils put the textbook away. From now on they
                      retell from the story map only.

2. Lead Educator      In a lesson early in the week, the Lead_Educator tells
                      one folk tale aloud from memory to the whole class, and
                      every pupil writes it in their notebook. Each pupil then
                      chooses which tale to keep: this one, one they have
                      heard told before, or one from an older person at home.
                      Pupils never visit neighbours or others outside their
                      household and write only the tale. Don't ask who lives
                      at home, why, or which option was used.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils take turns retelling their chosen tale
                      in their own words, using only the story map. The
                      listening partner watches the classroom clock and says
                      when three minutes are up. Pairs practise this way over
                      several lessons until each pupil finishes in under three
                      minutes.

4. Lead Educator      Each pupil in turn retells their tale aloud in their own
                      words in under three minutes, without the book, holding
                      only the story map. Spread the retellings over as many
                      lessons as the class size needs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each pupil has a grandparent or another older person at home who can
  tell a folk tale.
  If not: In class, the educator tells one folk tale aloud from memory to the
  whole class, and every pupil writes it down in their notebook. Any pupil may
  use either this tale or a tale collected at home.
- Needs: A classroom clock or the educator's watch to time three minutes.
  If not: The educator calls out when three minutes are up for the whole class
  during pair practice.
- Needs: Enough lessons for every pupil to give a final retelling (a large
  class needs several periods).
  If not: If a week is lost, cut pair practice to one lesson and start the
  final retellings sooner, so the unit still ends within 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-02 Spaced Practice: the 'Recall from' step now opens with
  a brief written review, from memory, of the three parts and character types
  taught at least a week earlier in MLS-FOLK-01. The chart is uncovered
  straight after for self-checking, and nothing is collected.


==============================================================================
MLS-FOLK-04: Writing and Revising Our Own Folk Tale
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-FOLK-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Has each pupil use the three parts and a character type to write, draft,
revise and finish a folk tale of their own of 400 to 600 words.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-FOLK-01: use the class chart of the
                      three parts and the character types. Each pupil chooses
                      at least one character type for their own folk tale. In
                      their notebook they write one line each for the
                      beginning, the three events of the middle, and the
                      ending.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each pupil
                      drafts their own folk tale on paper, following their
                      plan: a beginning that introduces a character and a
                      problem, then a middle with three events.

3. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson of quiet writing time. Each
                      pupil finishes the draft with an ending in which the
                      problem is solved, aiming for 400 to 600 words in total.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils read each other's draft and check it
                      against the class chart: three parts, three events in
                      the middle, and at least one character type. Each
                      partner writes one suggested improvement on a slip of
                      paper, then partners swap roles.

5. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson for each pupil to revise the draft
                      using the partner's suggestion and write a final copy of
                      400 to 600 words.

6. Lead Educator      Gather the pupils' final copies into one class book of
[changed: CL-04]      folk tales, with no choosing of best tales, no order of
                      quality, and no marks or comments on any page. Fold a
                      sheet of chart paper as the cover and tie the pages in
                      with reused string. A pupil may leave their name off
                      their tale or keep it out of the book. Keep the book in
                      the classroom for pupils to read.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Loose paper for drafts and final copies.
  If not: Pupils draft and write the final copy in their notebooks, and the
  partner writes the suggestion at the bottom of the draft page.
- Needs: Three weeks of lessons, including two full drafting lessons and one
  revision lesson.
  If not: If a week is lost, keep both drafting lessons and the revision
  lesson. Do step 1 in the last ten minutes of the lesson before the first
  drafting lesson, and do step 4 in the first fifteen minutes of the revision
  lesson, before pupils start revising, so the unit still ends within 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: all final copies bound
  by hand into one class book kept in the classroom, unranked and unmarked,
  with name and inclusion left to each pupil
