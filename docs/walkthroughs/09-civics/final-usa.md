==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Chapter 7 - Making Decisions Together: Local Government and
Community Priorities
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how communities decide among competing needs with
limited resources, and judge whether a decision process is fair.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-DECIDE-01         Why Communities Need Ways to Decide (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DECIDE-02         Collecting and Costing School-Grounds Ideas (2 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DECIDE-03         Voting Fairly and Arguing for a Process (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01, MLS-DECIDE-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-01: Why Communities Need Ways to Decide
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared language of competing needs, limited resources and
accepted decisions that later budget, vote and fairness work relies on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that every community has more needs than money,
[changed: EB-09]      time or space, using one school-grounds example. Explain
                      that councils choose which need comes first each year.
                      Ask why a decision people have had a say in is more
                      understood and accepted, with each student answering
                      from the lesson on scrap paper, then state the precise
                      answer that people understand a decision they joined and
                      accept it even when they wanted something else.

2. Lead Educator      Explain that residents elect council members for a fixed
[changed: EB-04]      term to a City Council. Explain that the elected council
                      makes decisions and sets a yearly budget while officials
                      carry it out. Explain that the council reports back and
                      holds open meetings where residents raise needs, which
                      pupils may observe. Ask one question with every student
                      holding up the answer on scrap paper at the same time,
                      look at all answers, and re-explain if many are wrong
                      before moving on.

3. Peer Facilitator   Draw the council-structure diagram on the board from
[changed: CL-02]      section 7.2. Each student copies the diagram into their
                      notebook using pencil and paper. In pairs, take Reader
                      and Checker roles: one partner reads the labels aloud
                      while the other checks the copy for the council,
                      officials and budget labels including the city manager
                      who carries out council policy, then partners swap roles
                      and check the second copy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board with chalk and student notebooks are available for the
  council-structure diagram.
  If not: Use chart paper and have students copy in pencil; combine
  explanation and copying into one period and use the second period for
  review, with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: third telling becomes a
  precise why question answered from the lesson with the precise answer stated
  straight after
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: whole-class simultaneous
  answer on scrap paper with educator review and conditional re-explanation
  before moving on
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: checking of diagram copies
  changed to rotating peer Reader and Checker roles


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-02: Collecting and Costing School-Grounds Ideas
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces three classroom ideas and tested point-cost combinations that
give the later vote and fairness judgement concrete material to decide among.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Ask each student to think of one change that would
                      improve the school grounds for everyone, such as shade,
                      seating, a garden bed or a notice board.In this city
                      that often means a bottle-filling station, bike racks,
                      or shaded pick-up benches. Each student writes the idea
                      on a slip of paper. Collect the slips in the classroom.

2. Lead Educator      Read all slips aloud in class and lead a short
                      discussion of the ideas. Select the three ideas that
                      best serve the school grounds to go forward. Write the
                      three ideas on chart paper with chalk.

3. Lead Educator      Explain that councils check each proposal against limits
[changed: EB-04]      of cost, time, space and who benefits. Explain that a
                      large idea that helps many may lose to a smaller one
                      that fits this year, with one example using two
                      school-grounds ideas. Ask one question with two choices
                      that every student answers at once on scrap paper held
                      up, look at all answers, and re-explain if many are
                      wrong before moving on.

4. Lead Educator      Write a price list on the board in points for the three
[changed: EB-03]      ideas within a made-up budget of 1,000 points. Show one
                      full combination that fits, adding its costs step by
                      step on the board. Each student then works in their
                      notebook to find other combinations that fit within
                      1,000 points. Collect no money and handle no purchases;
                      points stay a numeric exercise.

5. Lead Educator      Build one class table on chart paper of combinations
[changed: CL-04]      that fit within 1,000 points, with no names. Each
                      student may offer one fitting combination from their
                      notebook and you add each new one to the table. Keep all
                      work in the classroom on paper.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Paper slips, board space for the points price list and notebooks for
  combinations work are available.
  If not: Use reused paper scraps for slips; if a week is lost, drop one
  practice combination round and keep only the three-idea costing, with no
  extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: added every-student
  simultaneous answer on scrap paper with educator review and
  re-explain-or-move-on to the limits explanation
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: added one complete
  step-by-step budget combination on the board before students solve other
  combinations alone
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: combined individual
  fitting combinations into one class table with no names


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-03: Voting Fairly and Arguing for a Process
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01, MLS-DECIDE-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the ideas and budget work into a majority vote, an approval-voting
comparison and each student's own revised argument about which decision
process is fair.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-02: use the three selected ideas
[changed: PB-05]      and their point costs on the class chart, and from
                      MLS-DECIDE-01 use the council decision role. State the
                      single-choice rule first: one vote per student, most
                      votes wins, and the mock result buys nothing. Each
                      student votes once by hand raise for one idea, then
                      count class totals on chart paper with no names
                      recorded. Write the rule, the totals and the most-voted
                      winner on chart paper.

2. Lead Educator      Discuss if majority vote was fair to losers. Explain
[changed: EB-04,      approval voting where each votes for every acceptable
PB-03]                option and most-approved wins, then ask one check every
                      student answers at once on scrap held up, look at all
                      answers and re-explain if many are wrong before voting.
                      State the approval rule first, then each student raises
                      a hand for every option they accept while counting mock
                      totals on chart paper with no names and compare winners.
                      Nothing is collected or marked.

3. Lead Educator      Each student makes one poster section on chart paper
                      about the winning idea using words and drawings.
                      Assemble the sections into one class poster kept in the
                      classroom. Post no photo and no names online; keep all
                      work offline on paper.

4. Lead Educator      In week 3, open the writing lesson with four short
[changed: EB-01]      questions on scrap paper answered from memory on
                      majority vote and approval voting, with charts,
                      notebooks and books put away, taking a few minutes. Show
                      the answers straight after so each student checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected or marked.

5. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time for the
                      culminating argument. Each student states a position on
                      majority vote, approval voting, or the head of the
                      school alone,as with the live uniform-rule question with
                      at least two reasons using evidence from the class vote
                      and budget work, and answers one objection. Each draft
                      stays two pages in the student's own notebook.

6. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check it against
                      position, two reasons with evidence, and one answered
                      objection. Each partner writes one note on what is
                      missing or unclear, then partners swap roles.

7. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note. Each student writes a
                      final two-page copy in their notebook.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper for vote totals and the class poster, plus two full
  lessons for drafting and revising, are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, combine the approval-voting discussion and poster
  work into one period and shorten the poster to one section per pair; keep
  both writing lessons and drop the poster assembly, with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  scrap paper whether approval voting would change the winner, educator looks
  at all answers and re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: brief week-3 recall of majority
  and approval voting from memory with answers shown for self-check
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-05 Report-back: states single-choice rule before
  the hand-raise, then votes, counts class totals with no names, and writes
  rule, totals and winner
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-03 Counting Methods: hypothetical approval guess
  replaced by EB-04 check plus real approval hand-raise count compared with
  single-choice winner
