==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Chapter 7 - Making Decisions Together: Local Government and
Community Priorities
==============================================================================
Goal: Explain how communities decide among competing needs with limited
resources, and judge whether a decision process is fair.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-DECIDE-01         Needs That Compete and How the Local Council Decides (2
                      weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DECIDE-02         Weighing What Is Possible Within Limits (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
MLS-DECIDE-03         Voting, Fairness and Judging the Decision (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-01: Needs That Compete and How the Local Council Decides
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared list of three school-ground needs and the
elected-council picture that later budget and voting work decides among.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that communities have more needs than money,
[changed: EB-04]      time or space, so Grama Panchayat chooses what comes
                      first and officials carry it out, and that having a say
                      helps acceptance. Students copy the council-structure
                      diagram from the board into their notebooks. Then every
                      student answers at the same time on slates which body
                      chooses and which carries out, holds them up together,
                      the educator looks at all slates and re-explains if many
                      are wrong before moving on.

2. Peer Facilitator   Give each student a slip of paper. Each student writes
[changed: CL-02]      one change that would improve the school grounds for
                      everyone, such as shade, seating, a garden bed, a notice
                      board or a covered waiting area. Collect the slips, then
                      in mixed pairs the Reader reads two slips aloud while
                      the Recorder lists them on chart paper, then they swap
                      roles for the next two slips until every idea is listed,
                      with the educator present.

3. Lead Educator      Lead a class discussion of the listed ideas. Check each
[changed: PB-02]      idea against criteria stated in advance: cost, time,
                      space and who benefits, as a mock feasibility vet for
                      class priorities. Select three ideas that pass to go
                      forward and write them on chart paper for the next unit.

4. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      scrap paper from memory with notebooks, charts and books
                      put away: why needs compete, which body chooses, which
                      carries out, and why having a say matters. Then show the
                      answers on the board so each student checks their own.
                      Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board space, slips of paper, notebooks and chart paper are available.
  If not: Copy lists into notebooks instead of chart paper; combine listing
  and selecting into one period and drop re-copying the diagram, staying
  within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers at
  the same time on slates which body chooses and which carries out, educator
  checks all slates and re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: week-2 low-stakes recall of
  scarcity reason and council roles from memory, self-checked, nothing
  collected
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: teacher-only slip reading
  and listing converted to mixed pairs with rotating Reader and Recorder roles
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-02 Civic Decision Cycle: discussion/selection
  folded with mock feasibility vetting against cost, time, space and who
  benefits stated in advance


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-02: Weighing What Is Possible Within Limits
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives students the cost, time, space and benefit check and the
1,000-points combinations that show which needs can be done this year.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-01: use the chart of the three
[changed: EB-04]      forward ideas. Explain that councils check each proposal
                      against cost, time, space and who benefits, and that a
                      proposal helping many may lose to a smaller one that can
                      be done this year. Each student holds up fingers for
                      which check would block a costly idea with no space,
                      then look at all answers and re-explain the checks if
                      many are wrong, else move on.

2. Lead Educator      Write a price list on the board in points for the three
[changed: EB-03]      ideas, keeping the 1,000-point total, for example
                      benches 400, shade cloth 350, garden bed 200, notice
                      board 150, covered waiting area 600. Work one
                      combination step by step on the board, adding its points
                      and checking it against 1,000. Each student then works
                      out in their notebook which other combinations of ideas
                      fit within 1,000 points.

3. Lead Educator      List the fitting combinations on chart paper as class
                      totals. Discuss which combination helps the most people
                      and which can be done this year.A gram sabha likewise
                      reports back its choice with reasons.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board space for the points price list and notebooks for combinations
  work are available.
  If not: Read prices aloud and work in sharing pairs on one sheet; keep only
  the fitting combinations for the three forward ideas and drop the full-class
  comparison, staying within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers with
  fingers during the check explanation, answers are looked at, and
  re-explanation or moving on depends on them
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one points combination
  is worked step by step on the board before students work out the other
  combinations alone


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-03: Voting, Fairness and Judging the Decision
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the three ideas and budget combinations into a majority vote, an
approval-vote comparison, and each student's own reasoned judgement of which
process is fair.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-02: use the chart of the three
                      ideas and their fitting points combinations. Hold a
                      class vote by raised hands for one of the three ideas
                      and record only the class total for each idea on chart
                      paper.

2. Lead Educator      Discuss whether the vote was fair to everyone, using the
[changed: EB-04]      case of 11 for benches and 10 for shade leaving the 10
                      with nothing. Explain approval voting: each person votes
                      for every option they would accept, and the option
                      approved by the most people wins. Then ask every student
                      to show on fingers how many options a person may vote
                      for, look at every hand, and re-explain if many are
                      wrong before moving on.

3. Lead Educator      Vote again on the same three ideas in a mock decision
[changed: PB-05]      with approval voting, letting each student raise a hand
                      for every idea they would accept.As in a gram sabha open
                      meeting where all may accept any option. Record only
                      class totals on chart paper and compare the approval
                      winner with the earlier majority winner. Write on chart
                      paper the winners, the counting rules used, and what
                      happens next with the mock choice.

4. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with three short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      majority and approval voting answered from memory on
                      scrap paper, with charts and notebooks put away. Show
                      the answers straight after so each student checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected or marked.

5. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
[changed: CL-05]      drafts a two-page argument on whether the school grounds
                      decision should have been made by majority vote, by
                      approval voting, or by the head of the school alone,
                      stating a position, giving two reasons with evidence
                      from the class vote and budget work, and answering one
                      objection. Write it so a student from another class who
                      missed the votes can follow it.

6. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check it against
                      position, two reasons with evidence, and one objection.
                      Each partner writes one note on what is missing or
                      unclear, then partners swap roles.This lets quieter
                      members be heard, as Kudumbashree groups aim to do.

7. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note and write a final copy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two full quiet periods are available for drafting and revising the
  two-page argument.
  If not: If a week is lost, shorten the approval re-vote comparison to one
  period and drop the second tally discussion, protecting both writing lessons
  within 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows
  approval-voting rule on fingers, educator looks and re-explains if many are
  wrong before voting
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of majority and
  approval voting from memory in week 2, self-checked, nothing collected
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: draft written for
  another class as a real audience, without adding time
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-05 Report-back: folded report-back into
  approval-vote count: write winners, counting rules used, and what happens
  next for the mock choice, keeping class totals only
