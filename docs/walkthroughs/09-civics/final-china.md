==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Chapter 7 - Making Decisions Together: Local Government and
Community Priorities
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how communities decide among competing needs with
limited resources, and judge whether a decision process is fair.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-DECIDE-01         Community Needs and School-Ground Ideas (2 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DECIDE-02         Weighing What Is Possible Within Limits (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
MLS-DECIDE-03         Voting and Judging Fairness (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-DECIDE-01.
MLS-DECIDE-04         Arguing Which Decision Process Is Fairest (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02, MLS-DECIDE-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-01: Community Needs and School-Ground Ideas
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the starting point for the macro objective by explaining
competing needs and council decision-making and producing three school-ground
ideas to decide among.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that every community has more needs than money,
[changed: EB-04]      time or space, so the the Residents' Committee must
                      choose which need comes first. Explain the council makes
                      decisions and officials carry them out, and it sets a
                      yearly budget and reports back. Every student holds up a
                      slate showing which body decides and which carries out,
                      you look at slates and re-explain if many are wrong,
                      otherwise move on. Students copy the council structure
                      diagram from the board into notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Give each student a slip of paper. Each student writes
                      one change that would improve the school grounds for
                      everyone, such as shade, seating, a garden bed, a notice
                      board, or a covered waiting area like wutong shade,
                      stone stools, and a hallway bulletin board. Collect the
                      slips, read them out, and lead a class discussion of the
                      ideas.

3. Lead Educator      State three vetting rules on the board before choosing:
[changed: PB-02]      cost, time, and who it helps. Pick the three ideas that
                      pass those rules and best serve everyone for further
                      discussion. Write the titles of the three chosen ideas
                      on chart paper for display inside the classroom only (no
                      student names). Keep all other slips for reference.

4. Lead Educator      Make a class chart on chart paper listing the three
                      chosen ideas. Keep the chart for later budget and voting
                      work. Tell the class these three ideas will be priced
                      and voted on next.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      council decisions and budget answered from memory on
                      scrap paper, with charts, notebooks and books put away.
                      Show the answers straight after so each student checks
                      their own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board, chalk, chart paper, slips of paper and notebooks are available
  for diagrams and idea collection.
  If not: Use reused paper and one board list instead of chart paper; combine
  steps 3 and 4 in one period and drop copying of extra slips.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  slates which body decides and which carries out, educator looks and
  re-explains or moves on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall in week 2 of
  council content from memory on scrap paper, self-checked, nothing collected
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-02 Civic Decision Cycle: added feasibility
  vetting against stated cost, time and who-it-helps rules before picking the
  three ideas


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-02: Weighing What Is Possible Within Limits
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Contributes the limited-resources half of the macro objective by having
students test which combinations of ideas fit a fixed budget.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-01: use the class chart of three
[changed: EB-04]      chosen school-ground ideas. Explain that councils check
                      each proposal against limits of cost, time, space, and
                      who benefits, with one example of a larger idea losing
                      to a smaller one that can be done this year. Ask one
                      question and have every student hold up an answer on
                      scrap paper at the same time, look at all answers, and
                      re-explain the limits if many are wrong before moving
                      on.

2. Lead Educator      Write the three chosen ideas on the board with their
[changed: EB-03]      point costs for a made-up budget of 1,000 points, using
                      the source costs such as benches 400, shade cloth 350,
                      garden bed 200, notice board 150, covered waiting area
                      600. Work one combination aloud, showing 400 plus 200
                      plus 150 is 750 and fits. Leave the last addition of a
                      second combination for students to finish in notebooks,
                      then show the answer. Each student copies the list into
                      a notebook.

3. Lead Educator      Give lesson time for each student to work out in a
                      notebook which combinations of ideas fit within 1,000
                      points. Each student writes at least two different
                      combinations and their totals. Keep work in notebooks
                      for the final argument.

4. Peer Facilitator   In groups, assign Counter, Recorder and Presenter,
[changed: CL-02]      rotating roles for each combination as with classroom
                      duty teams. Groups report which combinations fit within
                      1,000 points and their totals for class-level totals on
                      chart paper. Discuss which combinations help the most
                      people within the limit. Keep the chart with the budget
                      totals for later voting and writing.

5. Lead Educator      Write on chart paper which combinations fit under the
[changed: PB-05]      made-up 1,000-point cap, the rule that a set fits when
                      its total is 1,000 points or less, and that the chart
                      will be used for the vote in MLS-DECIDE-03. Read the
                      totals aloud while students check their notebooks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board and notebooks available for price list and budget totals;
  normal class time across two weeks.
  If not: Keep one shared price list on the board instead of individual
  copies; if a week is lost, do one combination per student instead of two and
  shorten the totals discussion.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers one
  question on scrap paper at the same time, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one budget combination
  worked step by step plus a second left for students to finish before solo
  practice
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: class totals tally done
  with rotating Counter, Recorder and Presenter roles
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-05 Report-back: added educator report-back
  writing the fitting combinations, the fits-under-cap rule, and next use for
  voting


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-03: Voting and Judging Fairness
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Contributes the judging-fairness half of the macro objective by running
majority and approval votes on the same ideas and comparing who gains and who
gets nothing.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-01: use the class chart of the
[changed: CL-02]      three chosen ideas. Hold a class vote where each student
                      votes for one idea by raising a hand in a classroom
                      simulation that decides nothing real. Two students take
                      rotating roles, one counts hands aloud while the other
                      writes class-level totals on the board, and they swap
                      roles for each idea counted, then name the idea with the
                      most votes as winner.

2. Lead Educator      Explain approval voting where each person votes for
[changed: EB-04]      every option they would accept, and the option approved
                      by the most people wins. Explain other methods share a
                      budget out so each group of voters gets something in
                      proportion to its size. Ask every student to write on
                      scrap paper whether the 11-10 winner changes under
                      approval, hold it up at once, look at all answers, and
                      re-explain if many are wrong before moving on. Nothing
                      is collected or marked.

3. Lead Educator      Hold a second vote where each student raises a hand for
                      every option they would accept. Count class-level totals
                      for each idea on chart paper. Compare the approval
                      winner with the majority winner from step 1.

4. Lead Educator      Discuss with the class whether the first vote was fair
                      to everyone, using the example of 11 wanting benches and
                      10 wanting shade framed as who was heard, not only who
                      won. Ask what changed under approval voting and who
                      still gets nothing. Keep both totals charts for the
                      final writing.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with three short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      majority and approval voting answered from memory on
                      scrap paper, with the charts and notebooks put away.
                      Then show the answers so each student checks their own.
                      Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Full class present for hand-raise votes; board and chart paper
  available for class-level totals.
  If not: Vote with present students only and record totals on the board; if
  time is short, combine steps 2 and 3 in one period and shorten the fairness
  discussion.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  scrap paper at once during approval-voting explanation, educator looks and
  re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of majority and
  approval methods from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: teacher-only count becomes
  rotating counter and recorder pair swapping for each idea counted


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-04: Arguing Which Decision Process Is Fairest
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02, MLS-DECIDE-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Completes the macro objective by turning the class budget work and both
vote results into each student's own evidenced judgement about the fairest
decision process.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-02: use the budget totals chart
                      and the vote totals charts from MLS-DECIDE-03. Explain
                      the question whether the school grounds decision should
                      have been made by majority vote, by approval voting, or
                      by the head of the school alone. Each student writes a
                      one-line position in a notebook.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      writes a two-page argument that states a position, gives
                      at least two reasons with evidence from the class vote
                      and budget work, and answers one objection.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check it states a
                      position, gives two reasons with evidence, and answers
                      one objection. Each partner writes one note on what is
                      missing or unclear, then partners swap roles.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note and write a final copy.
                      Keep drafts and final copies in notebooks.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading their final arguments aloud to a small group of
                      listeners. Showing is a choice: a student may lay the
                      argument on the desk for listeners to read, or keep it
                      and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Quiet classroom time for two full writing lessons plus notebooks and
  prior budget and vote charts kept.
  If not: If a week is lost, shorten each writing lesson and require one page
  instead of two, but keep both partner feedback and revision; do not extend
  the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final arguments read to
  another class as a real audience
