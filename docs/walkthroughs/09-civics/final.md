==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Chapter 7 - Making Decisions Together: Local Government and
Community Priorities
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how communities decide among competing needs with
limited resources, and judge whether a decision process is fair.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-DECIDE-01         Why Communities Decide and Collecting School-Ground
                      Ideas (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DECIDE-02         Weighing Ideas Against a Points Budget (2 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-DECIDE-01.
MLS-DECIDE-03         Voting and Judging Whether the Vote Was Fair (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
MLS-DECIDE-04         Writing and Revising an Argument on Fair Decision-Making
                      (2 weeks). Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02, MLS-DECIDE-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-01: Why Communities Decide and Collecting School-Ground Ideas
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Sets up the problem of more needs than resources and how a local council
decides, and produces the class's own set of competing ideas to decide among.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that every community has more needs than money,
                      time or space, such as a town that needs a well, a road
                      crossing and a library but can pay for only one this
                      year. Explain that decisions people have a say in are
                      more often understood and accepted. Then explain that an
                      elected local council sets a yearly budget and decides
                      which projects to fund, while officials carry the
                      decisions out.

2. Lead Educator      Draw a simple diagram of the local council on the board:
[changed: EB-04]      residents elect council members for a fixed term, the
                      council decides and reports back to residents, and
                      officials carry out the decisions. Mention that some
                      councils hold open meetings where residents raise needs
                      directly. Ask who decides which projects get funded: all
                      students together show one, two or three fingers for
                      residents, council or officials. Scan every hand,
                      re-explain if many are wrong, then students copy it.

3. Lead Educator      Give each student a slip of paper. Each student writes
                      one change that would improve the school grounds for
                      everyone, such as shade, seating, a garden bed, a notice
                      board or a covered waiting area.

4. Lead Educator      Collect the slips, read them out and lead a class
[changed: PB-02]      discussion of the ideas. Before voting, state the rule:
                      each student raises a hand once, for the idea they most
                      want, the three with the most hands go forward, and ties
                      go to a second show of hands. Count the hands and write
                      on chart paper for the class to keep the three ideas,
                      the class totals only, the rule used, and that costing
                      comes next.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with four questions answered
[changed: EB-01]      from memory on scrap paper, with notebooks closed and
                      the board cleared: why a town cannot fund every need,
                      who elects the council, who decides what is funded, and
                      who carries decisions out. Then show the answers so each
                      student checks their own. Nothing is collected or
                      marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Slips of paper are available for every student.
  If not: Students write their idea on a strip torn from reused paper or the
  last page of their notebook.
- Needs: A sheet of chart paper is available to keep the three picked ideas
  for later units.
  If not: Write the three ideas on the back of an old poster or reused card,
  or have students copy them into their notebooks.
- Needs: Two full weeks of class periods are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, hold steps 1 and 2 in one period and steps 3 and
  4 in the next.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the council diagram
  is explained, every student answers one question at the same time with
  fingers; the educator scans all hands and re-explains if many are wrong
  before students copy the diagram
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall in week 2 of the
  scarcity idea and the council roles, from memory with notebooks closed,
  self-checked straight after, nothing collected or marked
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-02 Civic Decision Cycle: The educator's own pick
  of the three best ideas becomes a class show-of-hands ballot on the
  shortlist (Ballot stage). The one-vote rule and the tie rule are stated
  before voting. The PB-05 report-back is folded into the same counting step:
  the chart paper shows the three ideas, class totals only (no names), the
  rule used, and that costing comes next.


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-02: Weighing Ideas Against a Points Budget
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Shows students how limited resources force a choice by having them test
which combinations of their own ideas fit within a fixed budget.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-01: use the chart paper with the
                      three picked ideas and the slip ideas the class
                      discussed. Explain that councils check each proposal
                      against limits: how much it costs, how long it takes,
                      whether there is space, and who would benefit. Explain
                      that a proposal that helps many people but costs too
                      much may lose to a smaller one that can be done this
                      year.

2. Lead Educator      Tell the class they have a made-up budget of 1,000
[changed: EB-03]      points for school-ground improvements. Write a price
                      list on the board giving each idea a cost in points, for
                      example benches 400, shade cloth 350, a garden bed 200,
                      a notice board 150 and a covered waiting area 600. Work
                      one combination aloud step by step: add benches, shade
                      cloth and a notice board to get 900 points, then compare
                      900 with 1,000 to show it fits.

3. Lead Educator      Write one more combination on the board: the covered
[changed: EB-04]      waiting area, shade cloth and a notice board. Every
                      student writes the total and whether it fits within
                      1,000 points on scrap paper and holds it up. Look at all
                      the answers, and if many are wrong, work the sum again
                      on the board before students start. Nothing is collected
                      or recorded.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of notebook work. Each student works
                      out in their notebook which combinations of ideas fit
                      within 1,000 points.

5. Lead Educator      Take about 10 minutes after the notebook work to build
[changed: CL-04]      one class chart on chart paper listing every combination
                      that fits within 1,000 points. Students call out
                      combinations from their notebooks that are not yet
                      listed, the class adds each one aloud to confirm it
                      fits, and the educator writes it with its total. Calling
                      out is a choice, and no names go next to any
                      combination. Keep the chart for later units.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: MLS-DECIDE-01 has produced the class's ideas before this unit starts.
  If not: Start with the source's example price list (benches, shade cloth,
  garden bed, notice board, covered waiting area) and add the class's ideas
  when they are ready.
- Needs: A board and chalk are available for the price list.
  If not: Write the price list on reused chart paper or card, or read it aloud
  for students to copy into their notebooks.
- Needs: Two full weeks of class periods are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, hold steps 1 and 2 in one period and keep the
  full lesson of notebook work.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: after writing the
  price list, the educator works one complete combination aloud, step by step,
  before students test combinations alone in step 4
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student writes the
  total and fit of a second combination on scrap paper and holds it up; the
  educator looks at all answers and works the sum again if many are wrong;
  nothing collected or recorded
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: students' fitting
  budget combinations pooled into one class chart without names, after the
  individual notebook practice


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-03: Voting and Judging Whether the Vote Was Fair
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Has the class make its own decision among its three ideas by majority
vote, then compare that result with approval voting to judge whether the
process was fair.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-01: read out the three ideas on
                      the chart paper. Hold a class vote where each student
                      raises a hand for one idea. Count the hands for each
                      idea and write only the class totals on chart paper. The
                      idea with the most votes wins.

2. Lead Educator      Explain that majority voting is simple but can leave
                      large groups with nothing: if 11 students want benches
                      and 10 want shade, one vote gives benches to everyone
                      and nothing to the 10. Explain approval voting: each
                      person votes for every option they would accept, and the
                      option approved by the most people wins. Add that some
                      methods share a budget so each group gets something in
                      proportion to its size.

3. Lead Educator      Write on the board: of five made-up voters, two accept
[changed: EB-04]      only benches, and three accept both but prefer shade.
                      Every student writes on scrap paper which option wins
                      under a one-vote majority and which under approval
                      voting, and holds it up at once. Look at all the
                      answers; if many are wrong, re-explain with this example
                      before moving on. Nothing is collected or recorded.

4. Lead Educator      Lead a class discussion on whether the class vote was
                      fair to everyone, using the totals on the chart paper.
                      Discuss what might have happened if the class had used
                      approval voting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Most of the class is present on the day of the vote.
  If not: Hold the vote at the next period when most students are present,
  then run steps 2 and 3 in the same week.
- Needs: Chart paper is available for the class vote totals.
  If not: Write the totals in a corner of the board and have students copy
  them into their notebooks for MLS-DECIDE-04.
- Needs: Two full weeks of class periods are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, hold steps 2 and 3 in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the explanation of
  majority and approval voting, every student answers one two-part question on
  scrap paper held up at once (majority: shade 3 to 2; approval: benches 5 to
  3); the educator looks at every answer and re-explains if many are wrong;
  nothing is collected or recorded


==============================================================================
MLS-DECIDE-04: Writing and Revising an Argument on Fair Decision-Making
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DECIDE-02, MLS-DECIDE-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Has each student judge in writing which decision process is fairest,
using evidence from the class's own budget and vote work.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DECIDE-02: cover the combinations and
[changed: EB-01]      vote totals charts and clear the board. With notebooks
                      closed, students write on scrap paper one combination
                      within 1,000 points, what approval voting means, and
                      which idea won, by how many hands. Uncover the charts
                      and restate approval voting for self-checking; nothing
                      is collected or marked. Write on the board: "Should the
                      school grounds decision have been made by majority vote,
                      by approval voting, or by the head of the school alone?"

2. Lead Educator      Give two full lessons of quiet writing time. Each
                      student drafts a two-page argument that states a
                      position, gives at least two reasons with evidence from
                      the class's vote and budget work, and answers one
                      objection.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, students swap drafts and read their partner's
                      whole draft, then swap back.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson for each student to revise their own
                      argument after reading the partner's draft and to write
                      a final copy.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading aloud the position and strongest reason from
                      their final arguments to a small group of listeners.
                      Reading is a choice: a student may lay the argument on
                      the desk for listeners to read, or keep it and just
                      listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Students have enough paper for a two-page draft and a final copy.
  If not: Students write on both sides of reused sheets or in the back of
  their notebooks.
- Needs: Students are present in even numbers for partner reading.
  If not: Form one group of three in which each student reads one other draft.
- Needs: Two full weeks of class periods are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, hold the partner reading at the start of the
  revision lesson and keep the two full drafting lessons.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: the reread of notebooks becomes
  recall from memory on scrap paper (a budget combination and the meaning of
  approval voting), self-checked against the notebooks and the restated idea
  right after; nothing is collected or marked
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final arguments shared
  with another class in the classroom as a real audience, with showing work
  left to each student's choice
