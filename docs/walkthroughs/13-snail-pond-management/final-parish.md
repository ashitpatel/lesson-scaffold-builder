==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 9 Environmental Studies - Pond Management
==============================================================================
Goal: Study the ponds of the area, analyse how they are used and polluted, and
draw up and carry out a pond design and management plan.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-POND-01           Knowing Local Ponds and Their Value (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-POND-02           Sketching and Observing Pond Use (3 weeks). Starts fresh
                      (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-POND-03           Sharing and Analysing Findings (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-POND-01, MLS-POND-02.
MLS-POND-04           Pond Design and Action Plan (3 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-POND-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-01: Knowing Local Ponds and Their Value
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared map of where ponds are and how families use them, plus
the songs collection showing how the community values ponds.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a pond is a small still water body near
[changed: EB-04]      homes, wheat fields or roadsides, like the village green
                      or church pond. Revisit the class water source maps
                      together. Discuss why some areas have many ponds and
                      others have few. Then ask every student to hold up one
                      finger for pond and two for river to show two water
                      bodies on the map, look at all fingers, and re-explain
                      if many are wrong before moving on.

2. Peer Facilitator   Discuss what family members use ponds for each day:
[changed: CL-02]      bathing, washing, swimming, fishing, watering gardens
                      and duckery. In mixed pairs, one partner counts hands
                      for each use while the other records class-level totals
                      for each use on chart paper, then pairs swap roles for
                      the next use.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, collect poems, songs and sayings that mention
                      a pond from family members at home. Each pair writes the
                      words in notebooks at home and copies them onto chart
                      paper in class. Bring the collection back to class.

4. Lead Educator      Share the collected songs and sayings in class. Count
                      whether pond references are fewer than river or sea
                      references. Discuss what the collection shows about how
                      the community values ponds.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with three short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      pond uses and pond features from memory on scrap paper,
                      with the chart, notebooks and books put away. Then show
                      the answers on the board so each student checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Family members at home are available to share songs and sayings;
  chart paper and notebooks are available.
  If not: Use the educator's own examples and class discussion only; combine
  collection and sharing into one period and drop the river-versus-sea count.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows pond
  or river with fingers, educator looks at all answers and re-explains if many
  are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of pond uses and
  features from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added mixed rotating
  counter and recorder pairs for the class use totals


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-02: Sketching and Observing Pond Use
3 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces the sketches and field notes on pond condition, users, hygiene,
seasons and history that the analysis and design units build on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, sketch the pond's location, size, shape, trees
                      and plants such as willow, reeds and duckweed, and bank
                      condition on chart paper. The whole class goes together
                      and the educator stays with all the groups. Stay on the
                      bank, do not enter the water, pick plants or lift
                      stones.

2. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, ask pond users how many people use the pond,
[changed: CL-02]      for what purposes, with what materials, and how use
                      changes with seasons, with one partner asking and the
                      other recording in notebooks, then swapping roles for
                      the next interview. The whole class goes together and
                      the educator stays with all the groups. Do not touch
                      pumps, feed or washing materials, only look and ask.
                      Stay on the bank, do not enter the water.

3. Lead Educator      Look at hygiene and sanitation around the pond: dumped
                      garbage, plastics, waste and sewage inflow, and note the
                      animals, birds and insects on and around the pond, such
                      as frogs, newts, dragonflies and moorhens. The whole
                      class goes together and the educator stays with all the
                      groups. Do not touch garbage, waste or plastics, pick up
                      objects, or enter the water. Do not touch animals, birds
                      or insects, only look.

4. Lead Educator      Ask older users about the pond's history, whether water
                      is dirtier than earlier, and how quantity changes with
                      seasons. Record any social or religious activities held
                      at the pond. The whole class goes together and the
                      educator stays with all the groups. Do not enter the
                      water or pick plants and offerings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Safe daylight access to a nearby pond for the whole class; pond users
  and older residents are willing to answer questions.
  If not: Sketch from memory and the class water maps in the classroom;
  combine the hygiene and history steps into one period with shortened notes,
  with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: paired asking and recording
  roles with swap for the pond-use interviews


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-03: Sharing and Analysing Findings
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-POND-01, MLS-POND-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the field findings into class charts and agreed answers on pond
rules, design features, pollution causes and protection actions.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-POND-01 and MLS-POND-02: use the songs
                      collection, pond sketches and observation notes. Pool
                      all findings into class charts, graphs and diagrams on
                      chart paper. Keep totals at class level with no
                      individual records.

2. Lead Educator      Debate whether there are community rules for pond use,
                      such as parish council bylaws and planning-permission
                      rules and whether there are typical pond designs.
                      Discuss what makes a well-designed pond. Agree a short
                      class list of design features.

3. Lead Educator      Discuss what pollutes pond water: chemical fertilisers,
[changed: EB-04]      pesticides, detergent, oil and organic waste. Discuss
                      what follows from pollution for use and living things.
                      Relate this to how the community values clean ponds.
                      Each student writes one pollutant with one effect on a
                      slate and holds it up, the educator looks at all slates
                      and re-explains what many miss before moving on.

4. Lead Educator      Discuss what can protect the pond and improve its
[changed: EB-09]      surroundings. Ask how dumped waste and soap water reach
                      the pond and why they harm use and living things, each
                      pair answers from observation notes, then the educator
                      states the precise answer. Discuss whether it is true
                      that the pond causes too many mosquitoes. Agree a short
                      class list of protection actions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper, chalk and two weeks of class time for pooling and
  debate.
  If not: Pool findings on the chalkboard only; shorten the design debate and
  drop the mosquito question to a brief answer, with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers one
  pollutant with one effect on slates, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: precise how/why question on
  waste reaching the pond answered from notes first, then educator states the
  precise answer


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-04: Pond Design and Action Plan
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-POND-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the agreed design features and protection actions into a pond
design, practical bank work, posters and a class report.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-POND-03: use the class lists of design
[changed: EB-09]      features and protection actions. Ask why soap water must
                      go to a soak pit and not the pond, have pairs answer
                      from the lesson, then state that soap water pollutes
                      pond water for use and living things. Draw a pond design
                      and management plan with planted banks, blackberry and
                      hazel, a soak pit for soap water, and space kept for
                      people's use.

2. Lead Educator      Clear garbage near the pond and plant the banks as the
                      plan shows. The whole class goes together and the
                      educator stays with all the groups. Do not touch sharp
                      waste or glass with bare hands; leave all glass, sharp
                      waste and heavy sacks for the educator; students handle
                      only light dry litter with sticks or plant only. Stay on
                      the bank, do not enter the water, following wildlife
                      trust guidance on protected species.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, make awareness posters about protecting the
                      pond using the class protection list. Each pair writes
                      and draws on reused paper with chalk or charcoal. Keep
                      the work to one lesson.

4. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      writes their part of the class report: what was done and
                      what is expected to follow. Each student keeps their own
                      draft in their notebook.

5. Lead Educator      Combine the drafts into one class report on chart paper.
                      The educator sends the report to the village school
                      cluster network for other schools.

6. Lead Educator      In week 3, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      design features and protection actions answered from
                      memory on scrap paper, with charts and notebooks put
                      away. Then show the answers so each student checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Permission to clear and plant a pond bank; seedlings, reused tools
  and poster paper are available; the village school cluster network accepts
  the report.
  If not: If the bank is unreachable, do the design, posters and report in
  class only and drop planting; if a week is lost, do two report steps in one
  period, with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: telling of the soak pit
  becomes asking-then-telling with precise why question, pair attempt, then
  stated answer
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of design
  features and protection actions from memory in week 3, self-checked
