==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 9 Environmental Studies - Pond Management
==============================================================================
Goal: Study the ponds of the area and draw up a pond design and management
plan to recommend to the community.
Grade band: High. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-POND-01           Pond Uses and Community Values (2 weeks). Starts fresh
                      (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-POND-02           Sketching Ponds and Surveying Use (3 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-POND-03           Pooling Findings and Analysing Pond Health (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-POND-01, MLS-POND-02.
MLS-POND-04           Pond Design and Action Plan (3 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-POND-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-01: Pond Uses and Community Values
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared pond-use list and songs-and-sayings collection that
later observation and analysis use toward the management plan.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a pond is a small still water body common
[changed: EB-04]      in the area. Revisit the class water source maps and
                      discuss why some areas have many ponds and others have
                      few. List everyday uses on chart paper: bathing,
                      washing, swimming, fishing, watering gardens, and duck
                      keeping. Then each student holds up a slate listing two
                      uses from memory, look at all slates and re-explain
                      missing uses if many are wrong before moving on.

2. Peer Facilitator   In mixed groups, pool the pond poems, songs, or sayings
[changed: CL-02]      each student brings from family or neighbours. Groups
                      take turns as Reader, Writer, and Checker on chart paper
                      without names or home details, swapping roles for each
                      item. Read the combined collection aloud in class.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, sort the collected lines into pond, river, and
                      sea piles and count each pile. Add the pairs' counts
                      into one class table on chart paper. Discuss what the
                      collection says about how the community values ponds.

4. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      pond uses and the sayings collection, answered from
                      memory on slates with charts and notes out of sight.
                      Show the answers straight after so each student checks
                      their own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Students can recall pond songs or sayings and chart paper is
  available.
  If not: Use two educator-provided examples and hold one class discussion;
  combine steps 2 and 3 in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  slates during the uses explanation, answers are looked at and missing uses
  re-explained if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of uses and
  sayings from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: teacher copying changed to
  mixed groups with rotating Reader, Writer, and Checker roles


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-02: Sketching Ponds and Surveying Use
3 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces the pond sketches and class-level use, hygiene, and
seasonal-change records that the analysis unit pools into findings.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Assign each group a nearby pond from the class water
[changed: EB-04]      source maps for study. Explain the sketch task: position
                      type, size and shape, trees and plants around and on it,
                      and bank condition. Ask every student to hold up a slate
                      naming the four sketch items, look at all slates, and
                      re-explain any items many miss before moving on. Set the
                      visit rota and the rule to stay together.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the assigned ponds,
[changed: CL-02]      with the educator staying with all groups. In groups
                      with rotating roles of Measurer, Recorder, and Checker
                      swapping each visit, sketch the pond's size and shape,
                      position among houses, fields or roadside, trees and
                      plants, and bank cover. Near water, plants, and animals,
                      students must not touch water, pick plants, or lift
                      stones.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the ponds, with the
                      educator staying with all groups. In groups, note pond
                      users, purposes, materials used, and seasonal changes as
                      class totals on chart paper. Near water, waste, and
                      pumps, students must not touch waste or plastics, pick
                      up bottles, or lift pump equipment.

4. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the ponds, with the
                      educator staying with all groups. Note garbage,
                      plastics, waste, and inflows, watch birds, animals, and
                      insects, and ask users about pond history, water change,
                      and social or religious uses. Near garbage, water, and
                      animals, students must not touch garbage, pick up
                      plastics, or enter the water.

5. Peer Facilitator   In groups, copy findings into simple tables: users and
                      seasons, hygiene notes, creatures seen, and social or
                      religious uses. Use class totals with no names or home
                      details. Keep the sheets for the sharing unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Dry weather and access to nearby ponds for group visits.
  If not: Sketch from the bank in one combined visit for two ponds and drop
  the repeat visit; use the water source maps for missing details.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student holds up
  slate naming the four sketch items, educator looks at all slates and
  re-explains missed items before moving on
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating Measurer,
  Recorder, and Checker roles swapping each visit to the sketch task


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-03: Pooling Findings and Analysing Pond Health
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-POND-01, MLS-POND-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the sketches and survey sheets into agreed class charts and
protection points that the design unit applies in its plan.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-POND-01 and MLS-POND-02: use the class
[changed: EB-04]      pond-use chart and the group sketch and survey sheets.
                      Pool the findings into class charts, graphs, and
                      diagrams on chart paper. Explain that chemical
                      fertilisers, pesticides, detergent, oil, and organic
                      waste pollute pond water. Each student holds up a slate
                      naming one pollutant, you look at all slates and
                      re-explain what is unclear before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Debate whether the community has rules for pond use and
                      what they cover. Compare pond designs from the sketches
                      and agree what makes a well-designed pond. Record agreed
                      points on chart paper.

3. Lead Educator      Discuss what pollutes pond water in this area and what
                      follows for use, plants, and animals. Discuss what can
                      protect the pond and improve its surroundings. Record
                      agreed protections on chart paper.

4. Peer Facilitator   In groups, discuss whether the pond causes too many
                      mosquitoes using the pooled findings. Reach a group
                      answer with one reason from the charts. Share each
                      group's answer in a class tally on chart paper.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, ask three questions on pond pollutants and
[changed: EB-01]      protections answered from memory on scrap paper, with
                      charts and sheets put away. Show the correct answers
                      straight after so each student checks their own. Nothing
                      is collected, marked, or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper and chalk for pooling charts and graphs.
  If not: Reuse notebook pages for group tables and keep one class chart;
  combine the rules and pollution discussions in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  slates during the pollutant explanation, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of pollutants
  and protections from memory in week 2, self-checked


==============================================================================
MLS-POND-04: Pond Design and Action Plan
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-POND-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Converts the agreed analysis into each group's own pond design and
management plan plus feasible clearing, planting, and a shared report.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-POND-03: use the agreed charts and
[changed: EB-04]      protection points. Describe a well-managed village pond:
                      banks planted with multi-purpose trees, medicinal
                      plants, vegetables, and taro, a soak pit for soap water,
                      and space kept for people's use. Have every student show
                      the features on slates at the same time, look at all
                      slates, and re-explain any feature many miss before
                      moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Give sustained drawing and writing time for each group
                      to draw a pond design and write management rules to
                      recommend to the community. Require banks, planting, use
                      space, and protection steps in each plan. Collect the
                      plans on chart paper.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to a nearby pond, with the
                      educator staying with all groups. Clear loose garbage
                      near the pond using sticks and reused sacks, sorting
                      what can be reused. Near waste, water, and heavy
                      objects, students must not touch sharp items, pick up
                      waste with bare hands, or lift heavy objects.

4. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the pond bank, with the
                      educator staying with all groups. Plant the bank edges
                      with available seedlings, seeds, or cuttings and water
                      them. Near tools, plants, and water, students must not
                      touch sharp tools, pick wild plants, or enter the water.

5. Lead Educator      Make awareness posters on reused paper about agreed pond
                      protections using the class charts. Write a short report
                      of the activity and what is expected to follow. Send the
                      report through the educator to a school sharing network.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Access to a pond bank for clearing and planting, plus seedlings,
  seeds, or cuttings.
  If not: Clear only the school-edge pond area and plant fewer spots in one
  visit; shorten the poster task to one poster per group.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows the
  pond features on slates together, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains missed features before group design
