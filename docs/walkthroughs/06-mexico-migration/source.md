# Historia 2.º de secundaria - Block 3: Migration Stories, Past and Present

*Teacher's guide following the SEP programme for lower secondary history (Grade 8). Suggested length: 8 weeks of 50-minute sessions.*

## 3.1 Why people move

Migration is the movement of people from one place to live in another. Historians explain it with push factors, which drive people away from a place (war, drought, poverty, lack of land or work), and pull factors, which draw people to a place (work, land, safety, family already there). Most migrations have several push and pull factors at once. Migration can be internal (within a country, such as from the countryside to a city) or international.

Students should be able to define push and pull factors and give two examples of each from a historical migration.

## 3.2 Great migrations in history

Give a lecture covering the great migrations of history: the peopling of the Americas, the movement of people from Europe and Africa to the Americas from the sixteenth to the nineteenth century, migration to the cities of Mexico during the twentieth century, and migration from Oaxaca and other states to the north. Students copy the dates and the map of routes from the board. Spend three sessions on this lecture.

## 3.3 Reading historical sources

Students read three short primary sources from the textbook: a letter from a farm worker in 1945 writing home from the north, a newspaper report of 1960 on families arriving in the capital, and a song verse about leaving home. For each source students answer: who wrote it, when, why, and which push or pull factors it shows.

## 3.4 Building a timeline

Students make a timeline on a long strip of paper from 1900 to today, marking five migration events from the sources and the lecture, with one push and one pull factor written under each event.

## 3.5 Family migration stories

Each student interviews a parent or grandparent about whether anyone in the family has migrated, including whether they had legal documents to cross the border and whether any relatives are living abroad without papers. Students bring family photographs to school and pin them on a map in the classroom with their family name.

## 3.6 Visiting the bus terminal

Groups of students go to the central bus terminal on a Saturday on their own to count how many buses leave for the north and to ask travellers where they are going and why.

## 3.7 Printing the timelines

Each student should bring 50 pesos so the class timelines can be printed in colour at the stationery shop for display at the school's open day.

## 3.8 Culminating task

Each student writes a two-page explanation of one migration, past or present, that explains its push and pull factors using at least two of the primary sources from section 3.3 and the timeline, and considers one way the migration changed the place people left. Students spend two sessions drafting, read a partner's draft and suggest one improvement, then revise.

The goal of the block is for students to explain migrations with push and pull factors, using historical sources as evidence.
