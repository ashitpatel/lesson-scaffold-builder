```
==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: History Grade 8 - Block 3: Migration Stories, Past and
Present
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain migrations with push and pull factors, using historical
sources as evidence.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-MIGRATE-01        Push and Pull Factors and Great Migrations (2 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-MIGRATE-02        Reading Sources and Building a Timeline (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01.
MLS-MIGRATE-03        Explaining One Migration with Evidence (3 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-MIGRATE-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-01: Push and Pull Factors and Great Migrations
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the push and pull vocabulary and the dated map of past movements
that later source reading and explanation depend on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain push factors that drive people away and pull
[changed: EB-04]      factors that draw people to a place, using chalk and
                      board. Give the source examples of war, drought, poverty
                      and lack of work or land, and work, land, safety and
                      family already there. Ask two examples and have every
                      student show one finger for push or two for pull, look
                      at all hands and re-explain if many are wrong. Students
                      copy the two definitions into notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Ask each student to write two push examples and two pull
[changed: CL-04]      examples from past movements such as Huguenots in Berlin
                      and guest workers in West Germany in their notebook.
                      Check definitions against the board while students work.
                      Each student adds one push example and one pull example
                      to a class chart on chart paper with no names or scores.
                      Keep the notebooks and the chart for later use with
                      sources and the timeline.

3. Lead Educator      Present two early long-distance movements with dates and
                      routes drawn on the board. Students copy the dates and
                      the routes into their notebooks. Use chalk and reused
                      paper if the board map cannot stay up.

4. Lead Educator      Ask why families moved from rural areas to large cities,
[changed: EB-09]      have each student write one reason from the push and
                      pull factors, then state that lack of work and land
                      pushed them and work and family pulled them. Present two
                      later internal movements, from rural areas to large
                      cities and from one region to distant work regions, with
                      dates and routes. Students add the dates and routes to
                      their notes and map. Keep the same materials as the last
                      step.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chalk, board, notebooks and reused paper are available; no lost
  weeks.
  If not: If paper is short, copy only dates and one route line per movement.
  If one week is lost, combine steps 3 and 4 into one period and drop the
  second example list to stay within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows push
  or pull with fingers during teaching, educator looks and re-explains if many
  are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: precise why-question on
  rural to city movement answered by each student first, then precise push and
  pull answer stated
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: individual push/pull
  examples also pooled on a nameless class chart for later source and timeline
  use


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-02: Reading Sources and Building a Timeline
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the push and pull definitions into evidence work by reading three
primary sources and mapping five events with their factors on a timeline.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGRATE-01: use the push and pull
[changed: EB-03]      definitions and the copied dates and routes. Model the
                      four questions on a short example on the board, saying
                      each step aloud. Students read the farm-worker letter
                      from 1945 about writing home from a distant work region.
                      Each student writes who wrote it, when, why, and which
                      push or pull factors it shows.

2. Lead Educator      Students read the newspaper report from 1960 about
[changed: EB-09]      families arriving in the large city. Each student writes
                      who wrote it, when, why, and which push or pull factors
                      it shows in their notebook. Then state the precise
                      answers for who, when, why and the factors shown. Keep
                      this answer with the first answer for the timeline.

3. Lead Educator      Students read the song verse about leaving home with
                      Arabic, Turkish, and German verses provided. Each
                      student writes who wrote it, when, why, and which push
                      or pull factors it shows. Keep the three source answers
                      together for the timeline and final explanation.

4. Peer Facilitator   In mixed small groups at the long strip of joined paper
[changed: CL-02]      from 1900 to today, take roles of Measurer, Marker and
                      Checker and swap roles for each event. Mark five
                      migration events from the sources and the lecture
                      including Huguenot settlement and guest-worker
                      recruitment on the strip using chalk and pens. Work in
                      the classroom with notebooks open for dates.

5. Lead Educator      Write one push factor and one pull factor under each of
                      the five events on the timeline. Use the source answers
                      from the first three steps. Keep the timeline for the
                      final explanation unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Long paper for a joint timeline strip, pens, and classroom wall or
  desk space are available.
  If not: If no long strip exists, join notebook pages with string. If one
  week is lost, read the newspaper report and song verse in one period and
  shorten step 4 to three marked events, then add the other two as homework
  review without extending past 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: added a complete
  modelled answer to the four source questions on a short example before
  students analyse the three sources alone
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: students attempt the
  precise why and which-factor answers first, then the educator states the
  precise answers straight after
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: timeline marking done in
  mixed small groups with rotating Measurer, Marker and Checker roles


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-03: Explaining One Migration with Evidence
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces each student's own two-page explanation of one migration's push
and pull factors from sources and the timeline, including one change in the
place people left.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGRATE-02: use the timeline with push
                      and pull factors and the three source answers. Each
                      student chooses one migration, past or present, from the
                      timeline such as Huguenot settlement or guest-worker
                      recruitment. Each student writes a short plan naming its
                      push and pull factors and one change in the place people
                      left.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time with notebooks
                      and pens. Each student drafts a two-page explanation of
                      the chosen migration using at least two primary sources
                      and the timeline. The draft explains push and pull
                      factors and one change in the place people left.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check push and
                      pull factors, use of two sources and the timeline, and
                      the change in the place left. Each partner writes one
                      note on what is missing or unclear, then partners swap
                      roles.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note. Each student writes a
                      final two-page copy in the notebook. Keep the drafts and
                      finals in the classroom.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session during school hours with
[changed: CL-05]      another class in the classroom, with the educator
                      present. Pairs take turns reading their final
                      explanation aloud to a small group of listeners. Showing
                      is a choice: a student may lay the notebook open on the
                      desk for listeners to read, or keep it and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two full periods of quiet writing time plus notebooks and pens are
  available.
  If not: If writing time is cut, shorten planning to one claim plus factors
  and combine partner notes into the end of the first drafting lesson. Drop
  the second full copy and keep the revised draft as the final, without
  extending past 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final explanations read
  to another class as a real audience
```
