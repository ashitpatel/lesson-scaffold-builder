==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: History Grade 8 - Block 3: Migration Stories, Past and
Present
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain migrations with push and pull factors, using historical
sources as evidence.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 50 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-MIGRATE-01        Push and Pull Factors in Primary Sources (2 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-MIGRATE-02        Building a Migration Timeline from 1900 to Today (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01.
MLS-MIGRATE-03        Writing and Revising an Explanation of a Migration (3
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01, MLS-MIGRATE-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-01: Push and Pull Factors in Primary Sources
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives students the push and pull vocabulary and their own written
analysis of three primary sources, the evidence base the later timeline and
explanation draw on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that migration is the movement of people from
                      one place to live in another. It can happen within a
                      country (internal) or between countries (international).
                      Explain push factors (war, drought, poverty, lack of
                      land or work) and pull factors (work, land, safety,
                      family already there), and that most migrations have
                      several of each. Write both lists on chart paper as the
                      class chart, and have students copy them into their
                      notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Before students read the sources, read out three short
[changed: EB-04]      reasons for moving, such as "the rains failed for three
                      years" or "an uncle has work waiting in the city". Every
                      student shows one finger for a push factor or two for a
                      pull factor at the same time. Look at all the hands, and
                      if many are wrong, re-explain using the class chart
                      before moving on. Nothing is recorded and no one is
                      named.

3. Lead Educator      Students read the textbook letter from a farm worker
                      writing home in 1945. Each student answers in their
                      notebook: who wrote it, when, why, and which push or
                      pull factors it shows, using the class chart.

4. Lead Educator      Students read the textbook newspaper report from 1960 on
                      families arriving in the capital. Each student answers
                      in their notebook: who wrote it, when, why, and which
                      push or pull factors it shows.

5. Lead Educator      Students read the textbook song verse about leaving
                      home. Each student answers in their notebook: who wrote
                      it, when, why, and which push or pull factors it shows.

6. Lead Educator      Each student writes in their notebook a definition of
                      push factors and a definition of pull factors in their
                      own words. Under each definition, they give two examples
                      from the migrations in the three sources.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each student or pair has a textbook with the three primary sources
  (1945 letter, 1960 newspaper report, song verse).
  If not: Read each source aloud and write its key lines on the board.
  Students then answer the four questions from the board text.
- Needs: Chart paper is available for the class chart of push and pull
  factors.
  If not: Write the two lists on the board. Students rely on the copy in their
  notebooks in the later units.
- Needs: Two full weeks of sessions are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, read two of the sources in one session and do
  step 5 as the closing part of the last reading session.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the explanation,
  every student classifies three reasons for moving as push or pull at the
  same time with fingers; the educator looks at all the answers and
  re-explains if many are wrong; nothing is recorded


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-02: Building a Migration Timeline from 1900 to Today
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Places migrations from the sources and the overview talk in time, with a
push and a pull factor under each, giving each student a timeline to use as
evidence in the final explanation.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGRATE-01: use the class chart of push
[changed: EB-04]      and pull factors and each student's notebook answers on
                      the three sources. Give a short talk on the great
                      migrations in the textbook chapter, with key dates on
                      the board and a map of the routes. After each migration,
                      every student holds up scrap paper naming one of its
                      push factors; look at all answers and re-explain if many
                      are wrong. Students copy the dates and map into their
                      notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Each student glues or tapes sheets of used paper end to
                      end to make a long strip. They draw a line along it and
                      mark the years from 1900 to today.

3. Lead Educator      Each student chooses five migration events from the
                      three sources and their notes from the talk. They mark
                      each event on the strip with its date and a short label.

4. Lead Educator      Under each of the five events, each student writes one
                      push factor and one pull factor. They check each factor
                      against the class chart.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open two lessons with three questions on push
[changed: EB-01]      and pull factors and the migrations from the talk,
                      answered from memory on scrap paper with the chart and
                      notebooks put away. Then show the answers so each
                      student checks their own. Nothing is collected or
                      marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Used paper and glue or tape are available to make a long strip for
  each student.
  If not: Students draw the timeline across a double page of their notebook,
  spacing the decades evenly.
- Needs: Two full weeks of sessions are available.
  If not: If a week is lost, keep the talk short and do steps 3 and 4 in the
  same session.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after each migration in
  the talk, every student holds up a push factor on scrap paper and the
  educator re-explains if many are wrong; the Recall wording, map and copying
  are kept
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall in week 2 of
  push and pull factors and the talk's migrations from memory, with chart and
  notebooks put away, self-checked and not collected


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGRATE-03: Writing and Revising an Explanation of a Migration
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGRATE-01, MLS-MIGRATE-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns each student's source answers and timeline into their own two-page
explanation of one migration's push and pull factors, backed by source
evidence and revised after partner feedback.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGRATE-02: each student uses their
                      timeline, and their notebook answers on the three
                      sources from MLS-MIGRATE-01. Each student chooses one
                      migration, past or present, to explain. They note which
                      two or more sources and which timeline events they will
                      use as evidence.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full session of quiet writing time. Each student
                      drafts an explanation of their chosen migration's push
                      and pull factors. They use at least two of the primary
                      sources and the timeline as evidence.

3. Lead Educator      Give a second full session of drafting time. Each
                      student completes the two-page draft and adds one way
                      the migration changed the place people left.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, students read each other's draft. Each partner
                      writes one suggested improvement on a slip of paper and
                      gives it to the writer, then partners swap roles.

5. Lead Educator      Give a full session for each student to revise the draft
                      using the partner's suggestion and write a final copy.

6. Lead Educator      Collect every final copy and, with the class, tie the
[changed: CL-04]      pages together with string inside a cover made from used
                      paper to make one class book of migration explanations.
                      Each student may write their name on their own pages or
                      leave it off. Add no marks or comments, and keep the
                      book in the classroom for students to read.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Five sessions across three weeks are available for choosing, drafting
  twice, partner reading and revising.
  If not: If a week is lost, do step 1 at the start of the first drafting
  session and step 4 at the start of the revision session. Keep both full
  drafting sessions.
- Needs: Each student has enough writing paper for a two-page draft and a
  final copy.
  If not: Students write the draft in their notebooks and the final copy on
  the backs of used sheets.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: final explanations
  combined into one class book made from string and used paper, with names
  optional and no marks shown
