```
==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: History Grade 8 - Block 3: Migration Stories, Past and
Present
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain migrations with push and pull factors, using historical
sources as evidence.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-MIGR-01           Push and Pull Factors in Migration (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-MIGR-02           Reading Sources and Building a Timeline (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-MIGR-01.
MLS-MIGR-03           Explaining One Migration (3 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-MIGR-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGR-01: Push and Pull Factors in Migration
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the push-factor and pull-factor vocabulary and map of example
movements that later units use to read sources and explain one migration.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain migration as movement to live in another place.
                      Define push factors with two examples and pull factors
                      with two examples, noting internal and international
                      migration. Give a short overview of Harappan settlements
                      in Punjab and later Amritsar and Delhi and towns of
                      northern Indian Punjab, drawing routes with chalk on the
                      board. Students copy definitions and the sketch map into
                      notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Draw a two-column chart on chart paper for push and pull
[changed: EB-04]      factors. Have every student write one push and one pull
                      example on scrap paper and hold it up at the same time,
                      look at all answers, and re-explain the difference if
                      many are wrong, then write the corrected totals on the
                      chart. Each student copies the chart into their
                      notebook.

3. Lead Educator      Ask each student to write definitions of push and pull
                      factors in their notebook with two examples of each.
                      Check notebooks against the class chart during the
                      lesson. Keep the chart for later units.

4. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      push and pull factors answered from memory on scrap
                      paper, with notebooks and the chart put away. Then show
                      the answers on the board so each student checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board, chalk, chart paper, and student notebooks are available for
  definitions, map sketch, and class chart.
  If not: If chart paper is missing, keep the chart on the board and in
  notebooks. If a week is lost, combine steps 2 and 3 in one period and drop
  the in-lesson notebook recheck.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers at
  the same time on scrap paper, educator looks at all answers and re-explains
  if many are wrong before writing chart totals
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: week 2 recall of push and pull
  factors from memory, self-checked with answers shown, nothing collected


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGR-02: Reading Sources and Building a Timeline
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGR-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the factor vocabulary into source evidence and a dated timeline of
five events that the final explanation draws on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGR-01: use the class chart of push and
[changed: EB-03]      pull factors. Think aloud through one chart example step
                      by step, naming who, when, why and its factors. Read the
                      1945 farm worker letter together aloud. Each student
                      writes in their notebook who wrote it, when, why, and
                      which push or pull factors it shows.

2. Lead Educator      Read the 1960 newspaper report on families arriving in
                      the capital together aloud. Each student writes in their
                      notebook who wrote it, when, why, and which push or pull
                      factors it shows. Keep answers in notebooks for the
                      timeline.

3. Lead Educator      Read the song verse about leaving home together aloud,
                      choosing a Punjabi folk boli already in the textbook.
                      Each student writes in their notebook who created it,
                      when, why, and which push or pull factors it shows. Keep
                      answers in notebooks for the timeline.

4. Lead Educator      Make a timeline on a long strip of paper from 1900 to
                      today using chalk and reused paper. Mark five migration
                      events from the sources and the lesson, including 1947
                      Partition movements, writing one push and one pull
                      factor under each event. Work in the classroom with the
                      educator present throughout.

5. Lead Educator      In week 3, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      push and pull factors and the three sources, answered
                      from memory on scrap paper with the chart and notebooks
                      put away. Then show the answers so each student checks
                      their own. Nothing is collected or marked.

6. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session in the classroom with
[changed: CL-05]      another class, with the educator present throughout. In
                      mixed pairs, take turns standing by the timeline and
                      explaining one event and its factors to small groups of
                      listeners. Taking part is a choice: a student may
                      explain, hold the strip, or stay with the group and
                      listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Textbook copies of the three short sources and a long strip of paper
  for the timeline are available.
  If not: If strip paper is missing, join reused pages into one strip. If a
  week is lost, read two sources in one period and shorten timeline work to
  the minimum five events in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one chart example is
  worked step by step before students analyse the letter and the next two
  sources alone
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of factors and
  source details from memory in week 3, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: finished class timeline
  shown and explained to another class as a real audience


==============================================================================
MLS-MIGR-03: Explaining One Migration
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-MIGR-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces each student's own two-page explanation of one migration's push
and pull factors from at least two sources, including one change to the place
left.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-MIGR-02: use the class timeline of five
                      migration events with push and pull factors. Each
                      student chooses one migration, past or present,with the
                      1947 Partition as one respectful option among others and
                      writes a short plan naming the migration and two sources
                      to use. Keep the timeline visible during the lesson.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      drafts a two-page explanation of the chosen migration
                      that explains its push and pull factors using at least
                      two primary sources and the timeline. Each draft states
                      one way the migration changed the place people left,
                      such as a village home or mohalla.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check it against
                      the push and pull factors and the two sources used. Each
                      partner writes one note on what is missing or unclear in
                      the other's notebook. Then partners swap roles.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note. Each student writes a
                      final copy in their notebook with push and pull factors,
                      source evidence, and the change to the place left.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom during school hours, with the educator
                      present. Pairs take turns reading their final
                      explanations aloud to a small group of listeners.
                      Sharing is a choice: a student may lay the explanation
                      on the desk for listeners to read, or keep it and just
                      listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Student notebooks and the class timeline remain available for
  drafting and revision.
  If not: If the timeline is damaged, rebuild a five-event list on the board
  from notebooks. If a week is lost, combine planning with the start of
  drafting and drop the second quiet-writing half period, keeping one revision
  pass.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final explanations read
  to another class as a real audience
```
