==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Class 1 Language - Chapter 3: Letters, Sounds and First Words
==============================================================================
Goal: Children know the sounds of the first eight letters in the textbook and
read short words made from them by blending the sounds.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 40 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-LETTERS-01        Sounds of the First Four Letters (2 weeks). Starts fresh
                      (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-LETTERS-02        Sounds of Letters Five to Eight (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-LETTERS-01.
MLS-LETTERS-03        Blending Sounds into Short Words (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-LETTERS-01.
MLS-LETTERS-04        Reading Words and a Sentence Aloud (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-LETTERS-02, MLS-LETTERS-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-LETTERS-01: Sounds of the First Four Letters
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives children the sounds of the first four letters in textbook order.
The first blended words are built from these letters.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Tell the class that each letter stands for one sound and
                      that they will learn two new letters a week, in the
                      order of the textbook. Show the first letter on a card
                      made from reused cardboard. Say its sound clearly, and
                      have the whole class say the sound back three times.

2. Lead Educator      Children trace the letter in the air with a finger, then
                      trace it on their slates with a finger.

3. Lead Educator      Children write the letter five times with chalk on their
                      slates.

4. Lead Educator      Show two pictures of things whose names start with the
[changed: EB-04]      letter's sound. Draw them on the board or on reused
                      card. Then say two more words, one that starts with the
                      sound and one that does not, and every child shows
                      thumbs up or thumbs down for each at the same time. Look
                      at all the thumbs without naming anyone, and if many are
                      wrong, say the sound with the pictures again before
                      moving on.

5. Lead Educator      Teach each new letter the same way, two letters a week:
                      the card, the sound said back three times, the tracing,
                      the chalk writing and the two pictures. Stop when the
                      class has learned the first four letters in textbook
                      order.

6. Lead Educator      In week 2, open two lessons with a few minutes of recall
[changed: EB-01]      of the two week-1 letters, with the letter cards out of
                      sight. Say their sounds four times in mixed order
                      (first, second, second, first), and children write each
                      letter on their slates from memory. After each one, show
                      its card so every child checks their own slate. Nothing
                      is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each child has a slate and chalk.
  If not: Children write the letter five times with a finger on the desk top
  or in a reused tray of sand.
- Needs: Reused cardboard is available for letter cards and pictures.
  If not: Write the letter large on the board and draw the two pictures on the
  board.
- Needs: Two full school weeks of daily lessons.
  If not: If a week is lost, teach that week's two letters in the other week
  alongside its own pair, one letter per lesson, and show one picture per
  letter instead of two.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: after the two pictures,
  every child shows thumbs up or down at the same time on whether two spoken
  words start with the sound. The educator looks at all the thumbs and
  re-explains with the pictures if many are wrong, without naming anyone
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall in week 2: four
  short items, the two week-1 sounds said in mixed order (first, second,
  second, first). Children write each letter from memory with the cards out of
  sight, then check their own slate against the card shown straight after each
  item. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded


==============================================================================
MLS-LETTERS-02: Sounds of Letters Five to Eight
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-LETTERS-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Teaches letters five to eight, which completes the set of eight letter
sounds named in the chapter goal.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-LETTERS-01: with the four letter cards
[changed: EB-01]      hidden, say each card's sound while children write its
                      letter on their slates from memory, then show that card
                      so each child checks their own slate. No results are
                      collected or recorded. Put the four cards on the board
                      in textbook order, then show the fifth letter on a new
                      card made from reused cardboard. Say its sound clearly,
                      and have the whole class say the sound back three times.

2. Lead Educator      Children trace the letter in the air with a finger, then
                      trace it on their slates with a finger.

3. Lead Educator      Children write the letter five times with chalk on their
                      slates.

4. Lead Educator      Show two pictures of things whose names start with the
                      letter's sound. Draw them on the board or on reused
                      card.

5. Lead Educator      Teach each new letter the same way, two letters a week:
                      the card, the sound said back three times, the tracing,
                      the chalk writing and the two pictures. Stop when the
                      class has learned all eight letters in textbook order.

6. Lead Educator      In the last two lessons of week 2, open each lesson by
[changed: EB-02]      saying the sounds of letters five and six one at a time,
                      with their cards turned face down. Children write each
                      letter on their slates from memory, then show its card
                      so each child checks their own slate. No results are
                      collected or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each child has a slate and chalk.
  If not: Children write the letter five times with a finger on the desk top
  or in a reused tray of sand.
- Needs: Reused cardboard is available for letter cards and pictures.
  If not: Write the letter large on the board and draw the two pictures on the
  board.
- Needs: Two full school weeks of daily lessons.
  If not: If a week is lost, teach that week's two letters in the other week
  alongside its own pair, one letter per lesson, and show one picture per
  letter instead of two.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: the recall of the four earlier
  letters is now done from memory on slates with the cards hidden, each card
  is shown straight after, and nothing is collected; the introduction of the
  fifth letter is unchanged
- Evidence-Based, EB-02 Spaced Practice: a few minutes of review of letters
  five and six in the last two lessons of week 2, at least a week after they
  were first taught, with children writing each letter from memory and
  checking it themselves


==============================================================================
MLS-LETTERS-03: Blending Sounds into Short Words
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-LETTERS-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the blending skill the goal requires. Children turn the sounds of
letters they know into spoken short words.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-LETTERS-01: put the four letter cards on
                      the board. Tell the class that each letter stands for
                      one sound, and that saying the sounds one after another,
                      joined together, makes a word.

2. Lead Educator      Write a short word on the board, made only from letters
                      the class knows. Point to each letter while the class
                      says its sound. Then sweep your finger under the word
                      while the class blends the sounds together and says the
                      word.

3. Lead Educator      Blend five words this way each day. Have children read
[changed: EB-04]      the fifth word silently, then say two words aloud, one
                      of them the word on the board, and all children at once
                      hold up one finger for the first or two for the second.
                      Look at all the hands; if many are wrong, blend it again
                      before moving on, without naming anyone. When the class
                      learns a new letter, words from the next day on may use
                      it.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, children read the same five words to a
                      partner. Then partners swap, so each child reads.

5. Lead Educator      End every lesson with ten minutes of reading practice
                      from the textbook word list, using words made from
                      letters the class knows. All children read from the
                      list.

6. Lead Educator      At the end of each week, during the reading practice,
                      listen to each child read that week's words. Do not
                      write down names or results.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Children can be put in pairs.
  If not: With an odd number of children, make one group of three, in which
  each child reads in turn.
- Needs: Children have textbooks with the word list.
  If not: Copy the list words made from known letters onto the board or chart
  paper for the whole class to read.
- Needs: Two full school weeks of daily lessons.
  If not: If a week is lost, keep five words a day in the remaining week and
  do the weekly listening once, at the end of that week.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: the fifth daily word
  becomes a check where every child answers at once: children read it silently
  and all show one or two fingers for which of two spoken words it is. The
  teacher looks at every hand and blends the word again if many are wrong.
  Nothing is recorded and no one is named.


==============================================================================
MLS-LETTERS-04: Reading Words and a Sentence Aloud
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-LETTERS-02, MLS-LETTERS-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Brings all eight letter sounds and blending together. Each child ends by
reading aloud a sentence of short words they have learned.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-LETTERS-03: use the blending routine,
                      with the eight letter cards from MLS-LETTERS-02 on the
                      board. Each day, write five short words made from the
                      eight letters. Point to each letter while the class says
                      its sound, then sweep your finger under the word while
                      the class blends it and says the word.

2. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, children read the same five words to a
                      partner. Then partners swap, so each child reads.

3. Lead Educator      End every lesson with ten minutes of reading practice
                      from the textbook word list. All children read from the
                      list.

4. Lead Educator      At the end of each week, during the reading practice,
                      listen to each child read that week's words. Do not
                      write down names or results.

5. Lead Educator      Before the last lesson, write short sentences on strips
                      of reused paper or card, or on the board, using only
                      words the class has learned. Write one sentence for each
                      child.

6. Lead Educator      In the last lesson, each child reads their sentence
                      aloud, standing at the front or from their seat, as the
                      child chooses; with a large class, spread the readings
                      over the last two lessons. Stand beside the child. If
                      the child stops at a word, point to each letter and
                      blend the sounds together with them, so every child
                      finishes the sentence. Don't correct or comment on any
                      child's reading in front of the class.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper is available for the sentences.
  If not: Write the sentences on the board before the last lesson.
- Needs: Two full school weeks of daily lessons.
  If not: If a week is lost, run the daily blending, partner reading and
  reading practice in the remaining week, and hold the sentence reading in its
  last lesson.
- Needs: Every child is present for the last lesson.
  If not: A child who is absent reads their sentence aloud on the next day
  they attend.
