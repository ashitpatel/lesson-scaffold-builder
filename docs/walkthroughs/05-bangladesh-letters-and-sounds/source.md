# Class 1 Bangla - Chapter 3: Letters, Sounds and First Words

*Teacher's guide for the NCTB primary curriculum. Suggested length: 6 weeks of daily 40-minute lessons.*

## 3.1 Why sounds come first

Children learn to read faster when they are taught directly which sound each letter stands for and how to blend sounds into words. In this chapter children learn the first eight letters in the order given in the textbook, two letters a week, and begin to read short words made only from letters they know.

## 3.2 The history of our script

Begin with a lecture on the history of the Bengali script, from ancient Brahmi inscriptions to the printed letters of today, and on the Language Movement of 1952. Children copy the date 21 February into their exercise books. Allow two lessons.

## 3.3 Teaching a new letter

For each new letter: show the letter on a card, say its sound clearly, and have the whole class say the sound back three times. Children trace the letter in the air, then on their slates with a finger, then write it with chalk five times. Show two pictures of things whose names start with the sound.

## 3.4 Blending sounds into words

Once children know at least three letters, write a short word made from them on the board. Point to each letter while the class says its sound, then sweep your finger under the word while the class blends the sounds together and says the word. Do this with five words each day. Children then read the same five words to a partner.

## 3.5 Daily reading practice

Every lesson ends with ten minutes of reading practice from the word list in the textbook. Children who cannot yet read the list should be moved to the back benches and given colouring sheets so that they don't slow down the others.

## 3.6 Checking who can read

At the end of each week, the teacher listens to each child read the week's words and writes the names of children who cannot read them on a "slow readers" chart on the classroom wall, to encourage them to work harder.

Parents should buy the companion workbook from the bazaar (120 taka) so that children can practise at home. Parents with smartphones should send the teacher a video of their child reading every evening.

## 3.7 Reading to the class

At the end of the chapter, each child reads aloud a short sentence made of the words they have learned, standing in front of the class.

The goal of the chapter is for children to know the sounds of the first eight letters and to read short words made from them by blending.
