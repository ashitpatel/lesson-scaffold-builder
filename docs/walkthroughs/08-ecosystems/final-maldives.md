==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Unit 5 - Ecosystems: Energy Flow and Food Webs
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how energy flows through an ecosystem and predict how a
change to one population affects the others.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-ECO-01            Ecosystem Ideas and Sorting Organisms (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-ECO-02            Food Chains, Food Webs and Energy Pyramids (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
MLS-ECO-03            Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats (4 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
MLS-ECO-04            Human Changes and Species-Loss Explanation (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-01: Ecosystem Ideas and Sorting Organisms
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared vocabulary of ecosystem, biotic, abiotic, producer,
consumer and decomposer plus the class organism chart used by every later
unit.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain ecosystem as living things plus water, soil, air
[changed: EB-04]      and sunlight interacting as a system, with biotic
                      meaning living and abiotic meaning non-living. Give two
                      local examples of each such as coral and rabbitfish,
                      using Dhivehi names. Students copy both definitions into
                      notebooks. Then each student holds up a slate showing
                      one biotic and one abiotic example, you look at all
                      slates and re-explain anything unclear before moving on.

2. Peer Facilitator   Work in small groups on the school grounds. The whole
[changed: CL-02]      class goes together and the educator stays with all
                      groups, keeping school dress and grouping norms. In each
                      group take turns as spotter, recorder and checker,
                      swapping roles at each new spot, to list organisms seen
                      and sort each as producer, consumer or decomposer. Look
                      only and do not touch, pick up or lift animals, insects
                      or unknown plants.

3. Lead Educator      Back in class, combine the group lists into one class
                      chart on chart paper with three columns for producers,
                      consumers and decomposers. Keep the chart for later
                      units.

4. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      ecosystem, biotic, abiotic and producer, consumer and
                      decomposer, answered from memory on slates with
                      notebooks, chart and books put away. Show the answers
                      straight after so each student checks their own. Nothing
                      is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: School grounds accessible for one class visit with chart paper, chalk
  and notebooks available.
  If not: Use the classroom and yard edge for listing, or educator-supplied
  local examples, and combine two short listing periods into one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows biotic
  and abiotic examples on slates during teaching, educator looks at all and
  re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of key terms
  from memory in week 2, self-checked, nothing collected
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating spotter,
  recorder and checker roles to the existing grounds listing task


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-02: Food Chains, Food Webs and Energy Pyramids
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the class organism chart into connected feeding relationships and
shows why only about one tenth of energy passes to each higher level.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-01: use the class chart of
[changed: EB-04]      producers, consumers and decomposers. Review one food
                      chain example such as grass to grasshopper to frog to
                      snake, and a reef chain such as seaweed to rabbitfish to
                      grouper, then ask one question every student answers at
                      the same time on scrap paper held up, look at all the
                      answers and re-explain if many are wrong before moving
                      on. Give each student a card naming one organism from
                      the class chart.

2. Lead Educator      Stand in a circle and pass a ball of string from each
                      organism to the organisms that eat it, including reef
                      fish eaten by local families until the strings form a
                      web. Keep the cards visible so the feeding links stay
                      clear.

3. Lead Educator      Ask one student to let go of the string and discuss
                      which organisms are affected when that species
                      disappears. Repeat once with a different organism if
                      time allows.

4. Lead Educator      Work one example on the board step by step from the
[changed: EB-03,      producer amount to each higher level using one tenth per
CL-01]                level, saying each step aloud. Write a second example
                      with its last step left blank for students to finish in
                      notebooks, then show the answer. Each student then
                      chooses one of two four-link chains from the web and,
                      from the same starting amount, finds the energy at each
                      level and draws the pyramid to scale on squared paper.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Cards, ball of string and squared paper available for the web and
  pyramid drawings.
  If not: Reuse cardboard slips and twine, and draw pyramids on plain paper
  with hand-ruled squares; drop the second disappearance round.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers one
  question at the same time on scrap paper, educator looks at all answers and
  re-explains if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: complete step-by-step
  energy example shown first, then a second example with last step left for
  students before independent calculation and pyramid
- Connected Learning, CL-01 Interest Integration: student chooses one of two
  web chains for the same one-tenth energy calculation


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-03: Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats
4 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces the class quadrat totals that give later predictions about
species loss real evidence from the school grounds.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-01: each student at the same time
[changed: EB-04]      holds up a slate sorting one shown organism as producer,
                      consumer or decomposer for the tally, the educator looks
                      at all slates and re-explains any confused part before
                      marking quadrats, with nothing recorded. The whole class
                      goes together to the school grounds and the educator
                      stays with all groups. Mark fixed quadrats with sticks
                      and string. Do not touch, pick up or lift insects,
                      unknown plants or heavy stones.

2. Peer Facilitator   Visit the same quadrats once each week for four weeks
[changed: CL-02]      and tally the plants and animals seen inside, working in
                      threes as Counter, Recorder and Checker who swap roles
                      each visit. The whole class goes together and the
                      educator stays with all groups. Look only and do not
                      touch, pick up or lift animals, insects or unknown
                      plants, never touching coral.

3. Lead Educator      In week 3, open the quadrat visit with three sorting
[changed: EB-01]      questions answered from memory on slates, with charts
                      and notebooks put away. Then show the answers so each
                      student checks their own. Nothing is collected, marked
                      or recorded.

4. Lead Educator      After the visits, each group compares its weekly tallies
                      to describe what changed in its quadrat. Combine the
                      group tallies into class totals on chart paper. Keep the
                      class totals for the final explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Same quadrat spots stay marked and reachable once a week for four
  weeks.
  If not: If one week is lost, use three weekly visits instead of four and
  shorten the comparison to three tallies; combine marking and first tally in
  one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student sorts one
  organism on slates at the same time, the educator looks and re-explains
  before marking
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes week-3 recall of
  sorting from memory on slates, self-checked, nothing collected
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating Counter,
  Recorder and Checker roles to the existing weekly quadrat tally


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-04: Human Changes and Species-Loss Explanation
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Uses the food web, quadrat totals and human-change cases so each student
writes and revises a full explanation of what happens if one species
disappears.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-02 and MLS-ECO-03: use the class
[changed: EB-02]      food web strings and the class quadrat totals. Each
                      student writes on scrap paper one feeding link from the
                      web and one change seen in the quadrat totals, then
                      holds it up while the educator reviews the links.

2. Lead Educator      Read aloud two short case studies, one about an
[changed: EB-04]      introduced island species with no predators and one
                      about a restored drained wetland. In small groups,
                      discuss how each change moved through the food web,
                      noting what it means for fisher families. Then each
                      student holds up a slate naming one organism that
                      increases and one that decreases for one case, the
                      educator scans all slates and re-explains the links if
                      many are wrong before moving on.

3. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      chooses one organism from the class food web and drafts
                      a two-page explanation of what happens if it disappears,
                      tracing at least three feeding links, using quadrat
                      totals and naming one uncertain effect.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft against the
                      three-link, evidence and uncertainty checks. Each
                      partner writes one note on what is missing or unclear,
                      then partners swap roles.

5. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note and write a final copy in
                      their notebook with their name on their own work.

6. Lead Educator      Place each student's final explanation page together
[changed: CL-04]      into one class book of species-loss explanations kept in
                      the classroom. Any student may keep their page in their
                      own notebook instead. Look through the book together as
                      a class without ranking or comparing pages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Copies of the two case studies and notebooks available for drafting
  and revision.
  If not: Educator reads the cases aloud and students discuss from the board;
  shorten drafting to one sustained period and revision to the next period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-02 Spaced Practice: Recall step becomes brief practice of
  upstream web and quadrat content weeks after first teaching
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers on
  slates after case discussion, educator scans and re-explains if many are
  wrong
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: combined final
  explanations into one class book kept in the classroom
