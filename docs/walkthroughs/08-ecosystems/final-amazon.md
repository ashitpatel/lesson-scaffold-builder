==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Unit 5 - Ecosystems: Energy Flow and Food Webs
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how energy flows through an ecosystem and predict how a
change to one population affects the others.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-ECO-01            Ecosystem Parts and Feeding Groups on the School Grounds
                      (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-ECO-02            Food Webs and Energy Pyramids (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-ECO-01.
MLS-ECO-03            Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats (4 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
MLS-ECO-04            People, Change and a Disappearance Explanation (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-01: Ecosystem Parts and Feeding Groups on the School Grounds
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared vocabulary of ecosystem, biotic, abiotic, producer,
consumer and decomposer, plus the class organism list that later food webs and
sampling rely on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that an ecosystem is living things together with
                      water, soil, air and sunlight in the várzea floodplain
                      around the community interacting as a system. Explain
                      that biotic means living and abiotic means non-living.
                      Students copy both definitions into their notebooks.

2. Lead Educator      Explain that green plants such as açaizeiro, buriti and
[changed: EB-04]      samaúma seedlings capture sun energy and store it in
                      sugars they make. Explain that herbivores eat plants,
                      carnivores eat other animals, omnivores eat both, and
                      decomposers return nutrients from dead material to soil.
                      Students hold up a scrap-paper answer together naming
                      the group for two examples, the educator looks at all
                      answers and re-explains any group many miss. Students
                      copy each term with one example into their notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the school grounds with
                      notebooks and pencils. In small groups, students list
                      organisms they see and sort each one as producer,
                      consumer or decomposer. The educator stays with all
                      groups.Another adult watches near any water or forest
                      edge. Students must not touch insects, pick plants, or
                      lift stones or logs.

4. Lead Educator      Back in class, combine the group lists into one class
[changed: CL-02]      list on chart paper with each organism marked as
                      producer, consumer or decomposer, using mixed groups
                      with Reader, Writer and Checker rotating each list. Keep
                      the chart for later units. Each student copies the class
                      list into their notebook.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      ecosystem, biotic, abiotic and feeding groups answered
                      from memory on scrap paper with notebooks and chart put
                      away. Show the answers straight after so each student
                      checks their own. Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: School grounds are accessible for one outing with notebooks, chart
  paper and chalk available.
  If not: If the grounds are unusable, observe from the yard edge in one
  period and shorten listing to visible plants and animals, keeping the class
  chart in week 2.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers two
  feeding-group items together on scrap paper and the educator re-explains
  groups many miss before copying
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of ecosystem and
  feeding-group terms from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: class list built with
  rotating Reader, Writer and Checker roles instead of teacher recording alone


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-02: Food Webs and Energy Pyramids
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the class organism list into feeding relationships and energy
amounts, showing one energy path, the full web, and why energy limits chain
length.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-01: use the class chart of
[changed: EB-04]      producers, consumers and decomposers. Explain that a
                      food chain shows one energy path and a food web shows
                      all feeding relationships together. The educator names
                      two feeding examples and each student writes chain or
                      web on scrap paper and holds it up, the educator looks
                      at all answers and re-explains if many are wrong, with
                      nothing collected. Give each student a card naming one
                      organism from the class list.

2. Lead Educator      Stand in a circle and pass a ball of string from each
                      organism to the organisms that eat it, as with cutia
                      eating açaí fruit or tambaqui eating seeds until the
                      string forms a web. Ask one student to let go of the
                      string and discuss which organisms are affected. Each
                      student copies two feeding paths from the web into their
                      notebook.

3. Lead Educator      Explain that only about a tenth of the energy at one
[changed: EB-03]      level is stored in the next, the rest used for living or
                      lost as heat, so chains rarely have more than four or
                      five links. Work one energy example step by step on the
                      board from a producer amount through two levels, saying
                      each step aloud. Start a second example and leave its
                      last step for students to finish in notebooks, then show
                      the answer.

4. Lead Educator      Offer two starting energy amounts at the producer level,
[changed: CL-01]      each linked to a different organism on the class list,
                      and squared paper, each student chooses one amount. Each
                      student calculates the energy at each level and draws an
                      energy pyramid to scale. Keep the pyramids with the
                      class food web materials.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Cards, a ball of string and squared paper are available for reuse in
  the classroom.
  If not: If squared paper is short, reuse plain paper with hand-drawn grids
  and combine pyramid drawing with the energy calculation in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student answers
  chain-or-web on scrap at once, educator looks and re-explains if many are
  wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one complete
  step-by-step energy example plus a second left for students to finish before
  solo pyramids
- Connected Learning, CL-01 Interest Integration: isolated pyramid calculation
  keeps the same skill and check but each student chooses one of two
  class-list-linked starting amounts


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-03: Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats
4 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces four weeks of class quadrat totals from the school grounds that
the final disappearance explanation uses as evidence.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-01: name what to count using the
[changed: EB-04]      producer, consumer and decomposer groups. Explain
                      counting inside squares to estimate the whole area, then
                      have every student show on slates what is counted and
                      why several squares help, look at all slates, and
                      re-explain if many are wrong, else continue. Take the
                      class together to mark quadrats with sticks and string,
                      with the educator staying with all groups. Students must
                      not pick plants, touch insects, or lift sticks or
                      stones.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the quadrats once a
[changed: CL-02]      week for four weeks with notebooks and pencils. Each
                      mixed group tallies the plants and animals inside its
                      quadrat on chart paper using Counter, Recorder and
                      Checker roles that rotate each week. The educator stays
                      with all groups on every visit. Students must not touch
                      animals, pick flowers or leaves, or lift quadrat sticks.

3. Peer Facilitator   In groups, compare the four weekly tallies and note
                      which plants and animals increased, decreased or stayed
                      the same. Write only class-level totals on chart paper,
                      with no student names or marks. Each group reads its
                      totals aloud for the class table.

4. Lead Educator      Combine the group totals into one class table of quadrat
                      results on chart paper. Discuss how the quadrats differ
                      and what might explain the differences. Each student
                      copies the class table into their notebook.

5. Lead Educator      In week 4, open with four short questions on producer,
[changed: EB-01]      consumer and decomposer groups and quadrat counting
                      answered from memory on scrap paper, with charts,
                      notebooks and books put away. Show the answers straight
                      after so each student checks their own. Nothing is
                      collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Fixed quadrat spots on the school grounds stay accessible once a week
  for four weeks, with sticks and string available.
  If not: If one week is lost, run three weekly visits instead of four and
  combine group and class comparison into one period; drop the earliest visit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student shows
  understanding on slates, educator looks at all answers and re-explains or
  continues
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes week-4 recall of groups
  and quadrat method from memory, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: group quadrat tallies use
  rotating Counter, Recorder and Checker roles


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-04: People, Change and a Disappearance Explanation
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Uses the food web, energy rule and quadrat totals to explain how human
changes move through webs and to predict what follows if one population
disappears.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-02 and MLS-ECO-03: use the class
                      food web and the quadrat totals. Read the two case
                      studies of a species introduced to an island and a
                      restored wetland like a várzea lake managed for pirarucu
                      and fishing families. In groups, discuss how each change
                      moved through the food web.

2. Lead Educator      Each student chooses one organism from the class food
                      web for a disappearance explanation. Each student lists
                      at least three feeding relationships the organism
                      belongs to and one effect that is uncertain. Each
                      student notes which quadrat totals will serve as
                      evidence.

3. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      drafts a two-page explanation in their own words of what
                      would happen on the school grounds if the chosen
                      organism disappeared. The draft must trace at least
                      three feeding relationships, use quadrat evidence and
                      name one uncertain effect.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft against the three
                      feeding relationships, the quadrat evidence and the
                      uncertain effect. Each partner writes one note on what
                      is missing or unclear, then partners swap roles.
                      Partners return the drafts with notes.

5. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note. Each student writes a
                      final copy in their notebook. Keep the final copies with
                      the class materials.

6. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading their final explanations aloud to a small group
                      of listeners. Reading is a choice: a student may lay the
                      explanation on the desk for listeners to read, or keep
                      it and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two full writing periods are available for drafting and revising,
  with case-study copies readable aloud.
  If not: If time is lost, shorten the case-study discussion to one period and
  drop the planning list, keeping both full writing lessons.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final explanations read
  to another class as a real audience
