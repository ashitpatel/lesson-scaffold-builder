==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Unit 5 - Ecosystems: Energy Flow and Food Webs
==============================================================================
Goal: Students explain how energy flows through an ecosystem and predict how a
change to one population affects the others.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-ECO-01            Ecosystem Terms and Sorting School-Grounds Organisms (2
                      weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-ECO-03            Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats (4 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-ECO-02            Food Webs and Energy Pyramids (2 weeks). Builds on
                      MLS-ECO-01.
MLS-ECO-04            Human Changes and Explaining a Disappearance (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-01: Ecosystem Terms and Sorting School-Grounds Organisms
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared vocabulary of ecosystem, biotic, abiotic, producer,
consumer and decomposer plus the class organism list used to trace energy flow
later.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that an ecosystem is living things with water,
[changed: EB-04]      soil, air and sunlight interacting as a system,from
                      pigeons and plane trees to moss in pavement cracks and
                      that biotic means living and abiotic means non-living.
                      Students copy both definitions into notebooks using
                      chalkboard notes. Ask one question about biotic and
                      abiotic that every student answers at the same time on
                      slates, look at all slates, and re-explain if many are
                      wrong, else move on.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together onto the school grounds
[changed: EB-03,      and stay with all groups. Show one organism such as a
CL-02]                pigeon, plane-tree leaf or patch of moss and work
                      through sorting it as producer, consumer or decomposer
                      step by step aloud. In small groups with Spotter,
                      Recorder and Checker roles rotating each organism,
                      students list organisms they see and sort each the same
                      way without touching insects, picking plants or lifting
                      stones.

3. Lead Educator      Back in class, combine group lists into one class list
                      on chart paper. Keep the list for later food web work.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Access to school grounds for one listing visit plus chart paper and
  notebooks.
  If not: List organisms from the yard edge or from memory on chart paper, and
  combine sorting and class-list steps in one period to stay within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: added every-student slate
  answer on biotic and abiotic with educator look and re-explain-or-move-on
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: added complete
  step-by-step sorting example before groups sort organisms themselves
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating Spotter,
  Recorder and Checker roles to existing group listing and sorting


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-03: Sampling the School Grounds with Quadrats
4 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Produces four weeks of class quadrat tallies that give the evidence of
local populations used in the final disappearance explanation.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the school grounds and
[changed: EB-03,      stay with all groups. Show step by step how to mark one
CL-02]                quadrat with sticks and string and tally the plants and
                      small animals inside it. Groups then mark their fixed
                      quadrats along pavement cracks, moss walls and planters
                      and tally their own in the same way with Measurer,
                      Recorder and Checker swapping roles each visit without
                      touching insects, picking plants or lifting heavy
                      objects.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the same quadrats and
                      stay with all groups. Groups repeat the tally on chart
                      paper without touching insects, picking plants or
                      lifting heavy objects.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the same quadrats and
                      stay with all groups. Groups repeat the tally on chart
                      paper without touching insects, picking plants or
                      lifting heavy objects.

4. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to the same quadrats and
                      stay with all groups. Groups make the final weekly tally
                      on chart paper without touching insects, picking plants
                      or lifting heavy objects.

5. Lead Educator      In class, each group compares its weekly tallies and the
                      class compares results across quadrats on the board.
                      Record class-level totals only on chart paper.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START

==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-02: Food Webs and Energy Pyramids
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the class organism list into feeding paths and a scaled energy
pyramid showing why only about a tenth of energy reaches each higher level.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-01: use the class list of producers,
[changed: EB-04]      consumers and decomposers. Explain one food chain path
                      and that a food web shows all feeding relationships
                      together. Each student writes one food chain path from
                      the list on a slate and holds it up, you look at all
                      slates and re-explain any link many miss before moving
                      on.

2. Lead Educator      Give each student a card naming one organism from the
                      class list. Students stand in a circle and pass string
                      from each organism to what eats it to form a web, then
                      one student lets go while the class discusses who is
                      affected.

3. Lead Educator      Explain that only about a tenth of energy passes to the
[changed: EB-03,      next level and chains rarely exceed four or five links,
CL-01]                working one pyramid calculation step by step on the
                      board. Students choose one of two class-list paths of
                      the same length for the same calculation, then finish
                      the last level of a second example in notebooks,
                      calculate energy at each level from the same producer
                      amount on the board, and draw a scaled pyramid on
                      squared paper.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Organism cards, a ball of string and squared paper are available.
  If not: Use notebook slips and chalk lines for links, and draw the pyramid
  on plain paper; combine the web discussion and pyramid start in one period
  to stay within 2 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every student writes one
  food chain path on a slate at the same time and the educator looks and
  re-explains before moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one pyramid
  calculation worked step by step plus a second example finished by students
  before independent calculation
- Connected Learning, CL-01 Interest Integration: students choose one of two
  class-list paths of equal length for the same pyramid calculation


==============================================================================
MLS-ECO-04: Human Changes and Explaining a Disappearance
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-ECO-02, MLS-ECO-03.
==============================================================================
Goal: Uses the food web and quadrat evidence to explain how human-introduced
and restored changes move through feeding links, ending in each student's own
predicted ecosystem effects.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-ECO-02 and MLS-ECO-03: use the class
[changed: EB-09]      food web string and quadrat tallies. Read the two case
                      studies on an introduced island species and a restored
                      wetland like Walthamstow Wetlands. In pairs, answer from
                      the case studies why each change reached the next
                      feeding link, then the educator states the precise path
                      for each case.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each student
                      chooses one organism from the class food web and drafts
                      a two-page explanation of what would happen if it
                      disappeared, tracing at least three feeding links with
                      quadrat evidence and naming one uncertain effect.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check for three
                      feeding links, quadrat evidence and one uncertain
                      effect. Each partner writes one note on what is missing
                      or unclear,using drawings alongside words where helpful
                      then partners swap roles.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each student to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note and write a final copy in
                      their own words.

5. Lead Educator      Collect the final copies into one bound class book of
[changed: CL-04]      disappearance explanations kept on the classroom shelf.
                      Each student may put their name on their page or leave
                      it off, and any student may keep their own copy instead.
                      The educator binds the pages with string during the
                      lesson with the whole class present.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Class food web chart and quadrat tallies are kept, plus notebooks for
  drafting.
  If not: Rebuild a small web on the board from the class list, and if a week
  is lost combine case discussion with draft start in one period while keeping
  both full writing lessons within 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: pairs first answer why each
  case-study change reached the next feeding link, then the educator states
  each precise path
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: final copies combined
  into one bound class book kept in the classroom
