==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: JHS 2 Social Studies - Section 4: Community Development and
Local Government
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils explain how a district decides among community needs with limited
funds and make a reasoned case for one project.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-DIST-01           How the District Council Works and What the Community
                      Needs (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-DIST-02           Costs and Fair Choices Within a Limited Budget (2
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-DIST-01.
MLS-DIST-03           Explaining Decisions and Recommending a Project (3
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-DIST-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-DIST-01: How the District Council Works and What the Community Needs
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the shared facts for the whole sequence: who decides, what a
development plan is, and the class chart of community needs ordered by
urgency.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain the Gemeinderat / conseil communal (municipal
[changed: EB-04]      council), the Gemeinderatsmitglied / conseiller communal
                      (municipal councillor), the Gemeindeversammlung /
                      assemblée communale (citizens' assembly), and what a
                      development plan is, using a chart on the board. Pupils
                      copy three short definitions into their notebooks. Then
                      ask two questions every pupil answers at the same time
                      on slates held up, look at all the answers, and
                      re-explain any part many miss before moving on.

2. Peer Facilitator   In small groups, list the needs of the community around
[changed: CL-02]      the school, such as a village fountain (Brunnen /
                      fontaine), a footbridge, streetlights,a shelter for the
                      Postauto / CarPostal stop, a clinic extension, a library
                      room, and a covered stall at the weekly market
                      (Marktstand / stand de marché). Name a speaker to read
                      ideas aloud, a writer to write the group list on paper,
                      and a checker to check nothing is missed, swapping roles
                      in the next group task.

3. Lead Educator      Each group reads its list aloud. Copy all the needs onto
                      one class chart on the board.

4. Lead Educator      Lead a class discussion on which listed needs are most
                      urgent and why. Agree a short urgency order on the class
                      chart.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      the step 1 definitions answered from memory on scrap
                      paper, with notebooks and the chart out of sight. Show
                      the answers on the board straight after so each pupil
                      checks their own. Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Board or wall space plus chart paper and notebooks are available for
  the class chart.
  If not: Keep the chart in exercise books and on the chalkboard; if a week is
  lost, combine steps 3 and 4 in one period and drop the second group listing
  round.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers two
  questions on slates during the explanation and the educator re-explains from
  the answers
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of the
  definitions from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating speaker,
  writer and checker roles to the existing group listing task


==============================================================================
MLS-DIST-02: Costs and Fair Choices Within a Limited Budget
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-DIST-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Teaches the limited-funds half of the objective by fitting project
combinations inside a fixed total and testing the vote result for fairness.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DIST-01: use the class chart of
[changed: EB-03]      community needs. Show the total of 500000 and the six
                      costs on the board: village fountain (Brunnen /
                      fontaine) 120000, footbridge 200000, streetlights 90000,
                      clinic extension 300000, library room 150000, covered
                      stall at the weekly market (Marktstand / stand de
                      marché) 80000, using numbers on paper with no real
                      money. Work one full set that fits within the total,
                      adding each cost step by step.

2. Peer Facilitator   In small groups, work out two different sets of projects
[changed: EB-03]      that fit within the 500000 total, using the six costs on
                      the board. Show each addition sum in notebooks.

3. Lead Educator      State the rules first: one vote for one option, then one
[changed: CL-02,      approval vote accepting more than one option, both by
PB-03]                show of hands. With the educator present, name a Counter
                      to count aloud, a Recorder to write both totals on the
                      board, and a Checker to check each mock count on which
                      project to fund, then compare winners. Swap the three
                      roles between the two counts so different pupils count,
                      record and check each time.

4. Lead Educator      Lead a discussion on whether the winning choice is fair
                      to communities whose projects were not chosen. Record
                      two fairness reasons on the class chart.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Full class present for the vote and no real money is needed since
  budgeting is numbers on paper.
  If not: If a week is lost, keep only one budget set per group and hold the
  vote and fairness discussion in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: lists the six usable
  costs and works one complete budget set step by step on the board before
  groups solve alone
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: points group work at
  the same six board costs so students solve similar sets themselves
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: vote count and board record
  done by rotating Counter, Recorder and Checker roles instead of alone
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-03 Counting Methods: single-choice vote compared
  with approval vote accepting more than one option, with Counter, Recorder
  and Checker swapping roles between the two counts


==============================================================================
MLS-DIST-03: Explaining Decisions and Recommending a Project
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-DIST-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Completes the reasoned-case half of the objective: each pupil turns the
needs evidence and cost work into a revised letter recommending one project
and answering one objection.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-DIST-02: use the budget sets and the
[changed: EB-04]      vote result. Explain what happens at a town hall
                      meeting,the Gemeindeversammlung / assemblée communale
                      pupils may attend with parents, where the council
                      reports spending and residents ask questions. Every
                      pupil writes answers to two questions about the meeting
                      on scrap paper and holds them up. Look at all answers
                      and re-explain any part many missed before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Each pupil chooses one community project for the next
                      citizens' assembly and writes a one-line recommendation
                      with at least two reasons in a notebook, using evidence
                      from the needs chart and cost sums.

3. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each pupil
                      drafts a one- to two-page letter to the elected
                      representative that states the project, gives reasons
                      with evidence, and answers one objection.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, read each other's draft and check it against
                      the needs evidence, costs, and objection answer. Each
                      partner writes one note on what is missing or unclear,
                      then partners swap roles.

5. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each pupil to revise the
                      draft using the partner's note and write a final copy in
                      the notebook.

6. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading their final letters aloud to a small group of
                      listeners. Showing the letter is a choice: a pupil may
                      lay the letter on the desk for listeners to read, or
                      keep it and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two full quiet writing periods are available for drafting and
  revising.
  If not: If a week is lost, shorten the town hall explanation to half a
  period and combine planning with drafting, keeping one full period each for
  drafting and revising.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: town hall explanation now
  has every pupil answer two questions on scrap paper held up, with
  re-explanation before moving on
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final letters read to
  another class as a real audience
