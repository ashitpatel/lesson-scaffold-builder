# JHS 2 Social Studies - Section 4: Our District Assembly and Community Development

*Teacher's notes for Junior High School, Ghana Education Service syllabus. Suggested length: 7 weeks of 60-minute lessons.*

## 4.1 How a district is governed

Every district has a District Assembly made up of elected Assembly Members, one from each electoral area, and members appointed by the government. The District Chief Executive leads the Assembly's administration. Below the Assembly are Unit Committees, which bring the needs of each community to the Assembly. The Assembly prepares a medium-term development plan, decides which projects to fund each year, and reports on progress at public meetings called town hall meetings.

Pupils should be able to describe the roles of the Assembly Member and the Unit Committee and explain what a development plan is.

## 4.2 The history of local government

Present a lecture on the history of local government in Ghana from the native authorities of the colonial period to the decentralisation reforms of 1988, with dates written on the board. Pupils copy the timeline. Allow two lessons.

## 4.3 Finding our community's needs

Pupils work in groups to list the needs of the community around the school: a borehole, a footbridge, streetlights, a clinic, a library, a market shed. Groups present their lists, and the teacher writes all the needs on the board. Then the class discusses which needs are most urgent.

Each group should also walk through the community after school without the teacher and ask at least ten households about their needs, writing down each household head's name and phone number.

## 4.4 Projects and costs

The District Assemblies Common Fund gives each Assembly money for development. Give the class this example: the Assembly has GH₵ 500,000 for new projects this year. A borehole costs GH₵ 120,000, a footbridge GH₵ 200,000, streetlights GH₵ 90,000, a clinic extension GH₵ 300,000, a library room GH₵ 150,000 and a market shed GH₵ 80,000. Pupils work out several sets of projects that fit within the budget, showing their sums.

## 4.5 Deciding together

Hold a class vote on which project the Assembly should fund first. Each pupil votes for one project and the project with the most votes wins. The class then discusses whether the choice is fair to communities that did not get their project.

Also hold an election for class prefect at this time, with candidates making speeches and pupils voting, so pupils see an election in action.

## 4.6 The town hall meeting

Explain what happens at a town hall meeting: the Assembly reports what was done with the money, and residents ask questions. Pupils should attend the next town hall meeting on their own and write a report on it.

## 4.7 Raising money for the class project

Each pupil should bring GH₵ 5 to start a class fund for a small project of the class's choice, with the class treasurer keeping the money in a box in the classroom.

## 4.8 Culminating task

Each pupil writes a letter of one to two pages to the Assembly Member for the school's electoral area, recommending one project for the community, giving at least two reasons with evidence from the class's needs list and cost work, and answering one objection. Pupils revise the letter after reading a partner's draft.

The goal of the section is for pupils to explain how a district decides among community needs with limited funds and to make a reasoned case for a project.
