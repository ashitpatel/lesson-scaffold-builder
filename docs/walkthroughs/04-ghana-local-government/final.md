==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: JHS 2 Social Studies - Section 4: Community Development and
Local Government
==============================================================================
Goal: Pupils explain how a local council decides among community needs with
limited funds and make a reasoned case for a project.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-COMDEV-01         How the Local Council Works and What Our Community Needs
                      (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-COMDEV-02         Projects, Costs and Deciding Together (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-COMDEV-03         Writing and Revising a Letter to the Council Member (3
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-COMDEV-01, MLS-COMDEV-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-COMDEV-01: How the Local Council Works and What Our Community Needs
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Gives pupils the roles of the council member, community committee and
development plan, and a class list of real community needs, with the most
urgent ones marked, to choose among.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain how a local council works, drawing a council box
[changed: EB-05]      above community circles on the chalkboard. Each area
                      elects one council member, the government appoints
                      others, and a chief administrator runs its work. Draw
                      arrows up, saying they are community committees bringing
                      local needs, and arrows down, saying they are projects
                      funded yearly from the council's several-year
                      development plan. Write town hall meeting beside the
                      down arrows and say the council reports spending there
                      and residents ask questions.

2. Lead Educator      Each pupil writes a short explanation in their notebook,
                      in their own words, of what the elected council member
                      does, what a community committee does, and what a
                      development plan is.

3. Peer Facilitator   In mixed groups of four or five, set by the educator,
                      pupils list the needs of the community around the
                      school, such as a borehole, a footbridge, streetlights,
                      a clinic, a library or a market shed. Pupils take turns
                      writing each need on the group's sheet, and a pupil who
                      did not write reads the list aloud in step 4.

4. Lead Educator      As each group reads its list aloud, write every need on
                      the chalkboard without repeats. Copy the full list onto
                      a sheet of chart paper and keep it as the class needs
                      list for later units.

5. Lead Educator      Lead a class discussion on which needs are most urgent
                      and why. Put a mark on the chart paper beside the needs
                      the class agrees are most urgent.

6. Lead Educator      At the start of the last lesson in week 2, with
[changed: EB-01]      notebooks closed and the chalkboard diagram rubbed off,
                      ask three questions pupils answer from memory on scrap
                      paper: what the elected council member does, what a
                      community committee does, and what a development plan
                      is. Then give the answers so each pupil checks their
                      own. Nothing is collected, marked or recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: A sheet of chart paper is available for the class needs list.
  If not: Write the list on the back of a reused poster or a flattened carton,
  and keep it for later units.
- Needs: Two weeks of lessons are available without interruption.
  If not: If a week is lost, carry the remaining steps into the following week
  so the unit still runs over two weeks. If lessons in those weeks are short,
  do step 2 at the end of the step 1 lesson and run steps 4 and 5 in one
  period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-05 Concrete and Visual Representations: the same
  explanation of the council is now given while drawing a chalkboard diagram
  (council box, community circles, up arrows for committees bringing needs,
  down arrows for funded projects, town hall label), with each part's meaning
  said aloud
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: a short recall in week 2: pupils
  answer three role questions from memory on scrap paper with notes and
  diagram out of sight, then check their own answers; nothing collected,
  marked or recorded


==============================================================================
MLS-COMDEV-02: Projects, Costs and Deciding Together
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Shows pupils, through their own budget sums and a class vote, how a
fixed fund forces a choice among projects and why that choice raises questions
of fairness.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that a national development fund gives each
[changed: EB-03]      local council money for development. Write this example
                      on the chalkboard: the council has 500,000 units for new
                      projects this year. A borehole costs 120,000, a
                      footbridge 200,000, streetlights 90,000, a clinic
                      extension 300,000, a library room 150,000 and a market
                      shed 80,000. Then work one set aloud, step by step:
                      borehole 120,000 plus footbridge 200,000 makes 320,000,
                      plus streetlights 90,000 makes 410,000, which is within
                      500,000.

2. Lead Educator      Write one more set on the chalkboard: clinic extension,
[changed: EB-04]      footbridge and market shed. Every pupil writes the total
                      and "fits" or "too much" on scrap paper and holds it up
                      at the same time. Look at all the papers; if many are
                      wrong, work the sum again on the board before moving on.
                      Nothing is collected or recorded.

3. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson for each pupil to work out, in their
                      notebook, several sets of projects whose total cost is
                      500,000 units or less. Pupils show every sum.

4. Lead Educator      Hold a mock class vote on which project the council
[changed: PB-03]      should fund first, announcing both counting rules before
                      any hands go up. Single choice: each pupil raises a hand
                      for one project only; approval: each pupil raises a hand
                      for every project they accept. Record each count's class
                      totals as tally marks on the chalkboard.

5. Lead Educator      On chart paper, write both counting rules, the winning
[changed: PB-05]      project under each rule, and that the class next
                      discusses whether the result is fair. Write only class
                      totals, never who voted for what.

6. Lead Educator      Lead a class discussion on whether the winning choice is
                      fair to communities that did not get their project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Each pupil has a notebook for budget sums that they will reuse in
  MLS-COMDEV-03.
  If not: Pupils work their sums on the back of used paper sheets and keep the
  sheets for the letter unit.
- Needs: Two weeks of lessons are available without interruption.
  If not: If a week is lost, carry the remaining steps into the following week
  so the unit still runs over two weeks. If lessons in those weeks are short,
  keep the full lesson for step 2, do step 1 at the start of that lesson, and
  run steps 3 and 4 in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one complete set of
  projects is added and checked against the 500,000 budget aloud, step by
  step, before pupils find their own sets in step 3
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil totals one
  more set on scrap paper and holds it up at once; the educator looks at all
  answers and re-works the sum if many are wrong; nothing collected or
  recorded
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-03 Counting Methods: the single-choice
  show-of-hands vote is now labelled a mock vote and is also counted by
  approval voting; both rules are announced before voting and only class
  totals are tallied
- Participatory Budgeting, PB-05 Report-back: report-back step: both counting
  rules, the winner under each and the next step (the fairness discussion) are
  written on chart paper, with class totals only and no individual votes


==============================================================================
MLS-COMDEV-03: Writing and Revising a Letter to the Council Member
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-COMDEV-01, MLS-COMDEV-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the class needs list and each pupil's cost work into the pupil's
own revised letter making a reasoned case for one project.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-COMDEV-01: use the class needs list on
                      chart paper. Recall from MLS-COMDEV-02: use the project
                      costs and each pupil's own budget sums in their
                      notebook. Each pupil chooses one project to recommend to
                      the elected council member for the school's area and
                      writes it with two reasons in their notebook.

2. Lead Educator      Give a full lesson of quiet writing time. Each pupil
                      drafts a letter of one to two pages to the elected
                      council member. The letter recommends the project, gives
                      at least two reasons with evidence from the needs list
                      and the cost work, and answers one objection.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils swap drafts and read the partner's
                      whole letter, looking at how it gives reasons, evidence
                      and an answer to an objection. Partners then return the
                      drafts.

4. Lead Educator      Give a second full lesson for each pupil to revise their
                      own draft after reading the partner's letter and write a
                      final copy.

5. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      reading their final letters aloud to a small group of
                      listeners from the other class. Reading is a choice: a
                      pupil may lay the letter on the desk for listeners to
                      read, or keep it and just listen.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: The class needs list chart from MLS-COMDEV-01 is still available.
  If not: At the start of step 1, rebuild the needs list on the chalkboard
  with the class from memory before pupils choose a project.
- Needs: There is enough paper for a one-to-two page draft and a final copy
  for each pupil.
  If not: Pupils draft in their notebooks and write the final copy on the
  clean side of reused paper.
- Needs: Three weeks of lessons are available without interruption.
  If not: If a week is lost, do step 1 at the start of the drafting lesson and
  step 3 at the start of the revision lesson. Keep both full writing lessons
  so the unit fits in two weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: final letters read to
  another class in the classroom as a real audience, with showing the letter
  optional for each pupil
