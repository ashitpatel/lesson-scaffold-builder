==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 5 Science - How Plants Grow
==============================================================================
Goal: Understand how plants grow, including what plants need and how leaves,
roots, and stems support growth.
Grade band: Primary. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-PLANT-01          What Plants Need and How Light Affects Growth (3 weeks).
                      Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-PLANT-02          Leaves and Seeds in the School Grounds (2 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-PLANT-01.
MLS-PLANT-03          Roots Take In Water and Stems Carry It Up (3 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-PLANT-01.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-PLANT-01: What Plants Need and How Light Affects Growth
3 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Establishes the four needs and shows through shared classroom comparison
that light affects growth.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain that plants need light, water, air, and minerals
[changed: EB-04]      from soil, using the list in section 2. Each pupil
                      copies the four needs into their notebook for use in
                      later lessons. Then ask one question on the four needs
                      that every pupil answers at the same time on scrap paper
                      held up, and look at all the answers during the lesson.
                      Re-explain any need many miss, or move on if most are
                      right, with nothing collected or marked.

2. Lead Educator      Place two shared potted seedlings of tomaat, as raised
                      in nearby Westland kassen, in the classroom, one in
                      light and one in a dark cupboard. Show the class both
                      pots and the chart paper where the class will mark what
                      happens.

3. Lead Educator      Over several lessons, the class observes both seedlings
                      together and marks the change on one shared class chart
                      on chart paper. Keep all observation in class time with
                      reused paper strips for size comparison.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils take turns checking the two seedlings
                      and reporting one change for the shared class chart.
                      Pairs use the same paper strips so every pair reports in
                      the same way.

5. Lead Educator      Ask why the seedling kept in the dark grew poorly, and
[changed: EB-09]      have each pupil answer from the shared chart on scrap
                      paper. Then state using the shared chart that light is
                      needed for growth. Each pupil writes one sentence in
                      their notebook about what happened to the seedling kept
                      in the dark. Nothing is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Two shared seedlings in reusable pots and chart paper are available
  in the classroom.
  If not: Sprout shared seeds in reused bottles for the comparison and keep
  only three shared observations on chart paper within the declared weeks.
- Needs: Full weeks are available for repeated observation.
  If not: If a week is lost, combine two observations into one period and
  shorten the final discussion, without extending the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers one
  question on the four needs at the same time on scrap paper, educator looks
  at all answers and re-explains or moves on
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: asking-then-telling for the
  dark seedling: pupils attempt the why from the chart, then educator states
  that light is needed for growth


==============================================================================
MLS-PLANT-02: Leaves and Seeds in the School Grounds
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-PLANT-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Connects leaves catching light to observed leaf shapes and seed sizes
collected together in the school grounds.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-PLANT-01: use the shared class chart of
[changed: EB-04]      light and the four needs. Explain that leaves use light
                      to help the plant grow. Ask every pupil to hold up a
                      slate showing which need leaves use, look at all slates,
                      and re-explain if many are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together around the schooltuin with
                      the educator staying with all groups to collect fallen
                      leaves and fallen seeds into one shared basket. Pupils
                      must not pick up sticks, tools, or litter and must not
                      lift heavy objects, and must not touch thorns.

3. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, pupils sort the shared seeds by size,
[changed: CL-02]      including tomaat and komkommer seeds from the schooltuin
                      on their desks and sort the shared leaves by shape. One
                      partner places the items while the other checks the
                      order, then they swap roles for the second set. Each
                      pair places one sorted set on the desk for the class to
                      see.

4. Lead Educator      Ask why leaves are green and why leaves are flat for
[changed: EB-09]      pupils to answer from the paragraph in section 3. Then
                      read aloud the paragraph stating leaves are green
                      because of chlorophyll and flat to catch light. Each
                      pupil draws one seed and one leaf in their notebook and
                      copies the two reasons.

5. Lead Educator      Ask pupils to answer in notebooks why leaves are green
                      and why leaves are flat, using the paragraph. Check
                      answers against the paragraph in class without ranking.

6. Lead Educator      Make one class table on chart paper of leaf shapes and
[changed: CL-04]      seed sizes from the pairs' sorted sets. Each pair adds
                      one mark for each shape and size present, with no names
                      on the table. Look at the full table together in class.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Access to schooltuin with fallen leaves and fallen seeds, with the
  educator present.
  If not: The educator brings one shared basket of fallen leaves and seeds
  into class and the sorting and drawing happen inside in one period.
- Needs: Full weeks are available for the walk, sorting, and leaf paragraph.
  If not: If a week is lost, combine sorting and drawing into one period and
  drop the repeat question check, without extending the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers on
  slates which need leaves use, educator looks at all and re-explains if many
  are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-09 Elaborative Interrogation: telling becomes
  asking-then-telling with precise why questions attempted first then the
  chlorophyll and flat answers stated
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: added rotating
  placer-checker roles to the pair sorting
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: one class table of leaf
  shapes and seed sizes built from pairs' sets


==============================================================================
MLS-PLANT-03: Roots Take In Water and Stems Carry It Up
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-PLANT-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Traces water mixed with minerals from soil through roots and up stems,
completing the path from needs to plant parts.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-PLANT-01: use the shared chart of the
[changed: EB-04]      four needs, including water and minerals from soil.
                      Explain that roots grow down into soil and take in water
                      mixed with minerals. Every pupil at the same time holds
                      up on scrap paper which way roots grow and what they
                      take in, you look at all answers and re-explain if many
                      are wrong before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Ask the textbook questions why roots grow down instead
[changed: EB-08]      of up and how water gets from roots to leaves. After
                      each answer from section 7, each pupil turns to their
                      neighbour and says why that step works, then you confirm
                      the correct reasoning. Each pupil copies the answers in
                      their notebook.

3. Lead Educator      Place one white flower such as a white chrysant grown in
                      nearby kassen in a bottle of water coloured with crushed
                      petals or soil at the front of the class. The class
                      watches the flower together from their seats over two
                      days.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, one pupil is the Viewer and the other is the
[changed: CL-02]      Recorder, then they swap roles for the next viewing. The
                      Viewer looks at the shared flower up close and says one
                      change while the Recorder notes it for the class chart.
                      Pairs report only the shared flower change for the class
                      chart.

5. Lead Educator      Explain that stems carry water up from the roots, using
                      the flower change. Each pupil writes one sentence in
                      their notebook stating that stems carry water up from
                      the roots.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: One white flower, one bottle, and material to colour water are
  available.
  If not: Use a green stem cutting in plain water in a reused bottle and
  observe the same two-day change within the declared weeks.
- Needs: Full weeks are available for the two-day flower watch.
  If not: If a week is lost, shorten to one shared observation and combine the
  rota with the final explanation in one period, without extending the unit.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every pupil answers at the
  same time on scrap paper and the educator checks all answers and re-explains
  if many are wrong
- Evidence-Based, EB-08 Self-Explanation: each pupil explains to a neighbour
  why each answer step works and the correct reasoning is confirmed straight
  after
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: named Viewer and Recorder
  roles with swap for the existing paired flower observation
