```
==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 6 Environmental Studies - Water Source Mapping
==============================================================================
Goal: Map the water sources homes and the village use, observe the
surroundings of those sources, and summarise how the class uses them.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-WATER-01          Mapping home water sources (2 weeks). Starts fresh (no
                      earlier unit needed).
MLS-WATER-02          Visiting sources and sharing reports (3 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-03          Percentages and village water facilities map (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-WATER-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-01: Mapping home water sources
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the household-level base map of source locations, distances, and
uses that later visits and class totals build on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Explain the names of home water sources such as
                      tubewell, pukur, and nodi ghat and what each is used
                      for: drinking, cooking, bathing, washing clothes. Each
                      child draws a simple map of their house and surroundings
                      on reused paper with pencil, marking every source the
                      family uses.

2. Lead Educator      Count walking steps from home to each source only with a
                      family member, staying back from water and waste. Do not
                      touch water, waste or heavy objects. Write the number
                      beside its mark with what its water is used for.

3. Lead Educator      Show the shared signs for tap, well, tubewell, and pot
[changed: EB-04]      for stored rainwater using green and red marks for safe
                      and arsenic-unsafe tubewells. Ask every child to draw
                      all four signs on slates and hold them up together, look
                      at every slate, and re-explain any unclear signs before
                      moving on. Each child then redraws each mark on their
                      map using only these signs, marking a summer drinking
                      source separately from a year-round one. Nothing is
                      collected or marked.

4. Peer Facilitator   Swap maps with a partner and read the partner's map
                      using only the signs. Write one note where a sign is
                      unclear, then return the map so the owner can fix it.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open one lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      source names, uses, and signs, answered from memory on
                      scrap paper with maps put away. Show the answers
                      straight after so each child checks their own. Nothing
                      is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Reused paper, pencils, and chalkboard are available for home maps and
  signs.
  If not: Use slates or backs of old charts, and combine drawing and signs
  into fewer periods with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child shows all four
  signs on slates at once, educator looks at all and re-explains before redraw
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of names, uses,
  and signs from memory in week 2, self-checked


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-02: Visiting sources and sharing reports
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Adds direct observation of source surroundings and turns each map into a
shared class report of local water problems and difficult access.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: bring the home source maps and
                      list the nearby sources the class will visit. Group
                      nearby sources so the whole class can cover them
                      together.

2. Lead Educator      Walk with the whole class together to the listed
                      sources, with the educator staying with all the groups.
                      Look at pollution near the pond, waterlogging near taps
                      and hand pumps and flood silt around the tubewell
                      platform, and garbage or animal waste nearby. Do not
                      touch, pick up, or lift waste, stagnant water, or heavy
                      objects.

3. Lead Educator      Add the visit observations to the home maps using the
[changed: EB-04]      shared signs. Discuss what a clean surrounding looks
                      like and which warning signs to record. Every child then
                      holds up a slate naming one warning sign for a described
                      surrounding, the educator looks at all the slates and
                      re-explains unclear signs before moving on.

4. Lead Educator      Each child makes a short water source report from their
                      map and visit on reused paper. The report lists each
                      source, its distance in steps, its uses, and the
                      observed surrounding.

5. Peer Facilitator   Share each report with the whole class in turn. Together
[changed: CL-04]      list what kinds of water problems exist in the area and
                      which sources are hard to reach on chart paper as a
                      class table with only totals and no names.

6. Lead Educator      In week 3, open a lesson with four short questions on
[changed: EB-01]      source surroundings answered from memory on slates, with
                      maps, reports and books put away. Show the answers so
                      each child checks their own. Nothing is collected or
                      marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Dry weather and safe walking paths allow the whole class to visit
  nearby sources together.
  If not: Visit only the closest safe sources in one outing, combine
  observation and map updates in one period, and drop repeat visits.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child answers on
  slates during warning-sign teaching and the educator re-explains before
  moving on
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of source
  surroundings from memory in week 3, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-04 Collective Shared Purpose: joint oral list kept as
  a class table on chart paper with only totals and no names


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-03: Percentages and village water facilities map
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the pooled reports into class-level percentages and a shared
village facilities map kept on the classroom wall.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-02: pool the class reports into
                      class-level totals of source types on chart paper. Keep
                      only totals, with no individual records.

2. Lead Educator      Count what percent of each type of water source is used
[changed: EB-04]      from the pooled totals. Work the divisions on the
                      chalkboard step by step using whole-class numbers. Ask
                      one question that every child answers at the same time
                      on slates, look at all the answers, and re-explain if
                      many are wrong before moving on.

3. Lead Educator      Count what percent of domestic water comes from which
                      source. Each child compares their own household mix with
                      the class average in their notebook.

4. Lead Educator      Draw only main roads on large chart paper for the
                      village outline. Children who already know the village
                      outline help those who do not.

5. Lead Educator      Mark the water tank, well, pump, tap, canal, and pond on
                      the outline using the shared signs plus the river ghat
                      and boat landing used when floods cover paths. Leave the
                      finished map on the classroom wall for the next
                      activities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Chart paper and classroom wall space are available for totals and the
  village map.
  If not: Use the back of an old chart, shorten to one draft round, and finish
  two steps in one period with no extension.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: added whole-class slate
  check of the percentage division with educator review and re-explanation
  before moving on
```
