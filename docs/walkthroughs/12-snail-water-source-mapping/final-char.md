==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 6 Environmental Studies - Water Source Mapping
==============================================================================
Goal: Map the domestic water sources used by households and the village,
observe their surroundings, and pool class findings on access and problems.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-WATER-01          Home Water Sources and Map Signs (2 weeks). Starts fresh
                      (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-WATER-02          Visiting Sources and Sharing Reports (3 weeks). Builds
                      on MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-03          Percentages and Village Water Facilities Map (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-WATER-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-01: Home Water Sources and Map Signs
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the household-level base maps with shared signs, distances and
uses that all later visits and class pooling build on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Discuss where domestic water comes from in the village
                      and in towns. Explain that village families use several
                      sources such as hand pump, well, tubewell, community
                      tap, river and pond, while town homes often use a tap
                      inside the house.

2. Lead Educator      Each child draws a simple map of their house and
                      surroundings in their notebook with pencil or chalk.
                      Each child marks every water source the family uses and
                      notes the distance in walking steps and the use for
                      drinking, cooking, bathing and washing clothes.

3. Lead Educator      Teach the shared signs for tap, well, tubewell and
                      stored rainwater pot, and how to mark a summer drinking
                      source differently from a year-round one. Show one
                      example map on chart paper using only these signs.

4. Lead Educator      Each child copies the shared signs onto their own map so
                      every source uses the same signs. Each child marks which
                      sources give drinking water in summer and which give
                      water all year.

5. Peer Facilitator   Swap maps with another child and read the map using only
                      the signs. Mark anything unclear and return the map so
                      each child corrects the signs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Notebooks or reused paper, pencils or chalk, and chart paper for the
  example map are available.
  If not: Use the chalkboard and reused paper, and combine the drawing and
  signs practice into fewer periods within the same 2 weeks.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-02: Visiting Sources and Sharing Reports
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns the home maps into checked field observations and class reports
that name the area's water problems and difficult access.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: bring the home source maps
[changed: EB-04]      with shared signs. List the sources the class will visit
                      and the warning signs to record, such as pollution near
                      the pond, waterlogging near the tap or hand pump, and
                      garbage or animal waste nearby. Each child holds up a
                      slate naming two warning signs to record, the educator
                      looks at all the slates and re-explains any missed signs
                      before the visit.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to visit the mapped
                      sources, with the educator staying with all the groups.
                      Near water, ponds, wells, garbage or animal waste,
                      students look only and must not touch, pick up or lift
                      anything.

3. Peer Facilitator   In mixed pairs, each child adds the visit observations
[changed: CL-02]      to their own map, marking pollution sources,
                      waterlogging, and garbage or animal waste near each
                      source. One partner reads out the warning signs while
                      the other checks them on the map, then they swap roles
                      for the second map.

4. Lead Educator      Each child makes a short water source report from their
                      map and visit, noting sources, uses, surroundings and
                      access difficulty.

5. Lead Educator      Hear the reports together in class and list what kinds
                      of water-related problems exist and which sources have
                      difficult access. Keep the list on chart paper for the
                      next unit.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Safe walking access to nearby sources and dry weather for class
  visits.
  If not: Visit only the nearest safe sources in one walk, shorten the
  observation list, and combine the report writing and sharing into shared
  periods within the same 3 weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child shows two
  warning signs on slates during the visit briefing; the educator looks at all
  answers and re-explains gaps before leaving
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: map updating done in mixed
  pairs with reader and checker roles that swap


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-03: Percentages and Village Water Facilities Map
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Pools the reports into class percentages and a shared village facilities
map that shows the area-wide pattern of sources.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-02: use the class list of water
                      source reports and problems. Pool the reports into
                      class-level totals of source types on chart paper.

2. Lead Educator      Work one percentage example on chart paper step by step
[changed: EB-03]      using the class totals, saying each step aloud. Each
                      child then works one remaining percentage in their
                      notebook from the totals. Write the checked percentages
                      on chart paper.

3. Lead Educator      Ask each child to compare their own household mix of
                      sources with the class average in their notebook. Each
                      child notes one difference between their home mix and
                      the class mix.

4. Lead Educator      Draw a simplified chart of water facilities with only
                      main roads, then mark the water tank, well, pump, tap,
                      canal and pond. Ask children who already know the
                      village outline to help those who do not.

5. Lead Educator      Finish the village water facilities map so it can be
                      read with the shared signs. Keep the finished map on the
                      classroom wall for the next activities.

6. Lead Educator      Arrange a 10-minute session with another class in the
[changed: CL-05]      classroom, with the educator present. Pairs take turns
                      showing the finished village water facilities map to
                      small groups of listeners and naming one facility using
                      the shared signs. Taking part is a choice: any child may
                      show the map, hold it, or just listen with the group.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Class time for arithmetic and wall space for the village map.
  If not: Do the percentages orally on the chalkboard, shorten the village
  chart to the main sources, and finish it in fewer periods within the same 2
  weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one complete
  percentage example is worked step by step, then each child solves one
  similar percentage before the class list is finished
- Connected Learning, CL-05 Real Audience Production: finished village map
  shown to another class as a real audience
