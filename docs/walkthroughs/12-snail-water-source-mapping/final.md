==============================================================================
LESSON SCAFFOLD: Grade 6 Environmental Studies - Water Source Mapping
==============================================================================
Goal: Map household and village water sources, observe their surroundings, and
summarise class patterns of use, problems and access.
Grade band: Middle. Period: 45 minutes.

UNIT                  WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MLS-WATER-01          Household Water Maps with Shared Signs (2 weeks). Starts
                      fresh (no earlier unit needed).
MLS-WATER-02          Visiting Sources and Recording Surroundings (2 weeks).
                      Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
MLS-WATER-03          Class Reports, Percentages and Village Facilities Map (3
                      weeks). Builds on MLS-WATER-02.

Units that build on the same earlier unit, or start fresh, can run at the same
time.

The LSB framework functions purely as an adaptive, supportive scaffolding
framework for lesson content delivery. It does not prescribe operational
classroom routines, behavior management habits, or physical school rules.


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-01: Household Water Maps with Shared Signs
2 weeks. Starts fresh (no earlier unit needed).
==============================================================================
Goal: Builds the household-level base map of source types, walking-step
distances and uses that later visits and class totals build on.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Discuss where domestic water comes from at home and in
                      the village. List family sources children name: hand
                      pump, well, tubewell, community tap, river, pond.
                      Explain each child will map house surroundings with
                      sources, walking-step distances, and uses.

2. Lead Educator      Each child draws a simple map of house surroundings in
                      the notebook with pencil. Mark every family water source
                      with distance in walking steps and use: drinking,
                      cooking, bathing, washing clothes. Keep the map in the
                      notebook with the student's name on it.

3. Lead Educator      Introduce the shared signs for tap, well, tubewell, and
[changed: EB-04]      pot for stored rainwater. Mark summer-only drinking
                      sources separately from year-round ones. Name three
                      sources in turn and have every child draw the sign on a
                      slate and hold it up, look at all slates and re-explain
                      any sign many miss, then each child redraws source marks
                      on the map using only these signs.

4. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, swap maps and read the partner's map using
                      only the signs. Check that each source, distance, and
                      use can be understood without extra explanation. Mark
                      any unclear sign on chart paper scrap for the owner to
                      fix.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, ask four questions from memory with notebooks
[changed: EB-01]      and maps put away: name two family sources, draw the
                      sign for well, draw the sign for tubewell, and tell what
                      summer-only means. Children answer on scrap paper or
                      slates. Show the answers on the board so each child
                      checks their own. Nothing is collected, marked or
                      recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Notebooks, pencils, chalk and chart paper are available for maps and
  signs.
  If not: Use slates or reused paper for maps and draw the sign set on the
  board; combine steps 2 and 3 in one period.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child shows each
  sign on a slate during teaching, educator looks at all slates and
  re-explains missed signs before redrawing
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: short week-2 recall of sources and
  signs from memory on scrap or slates, self-checked with answers shown


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-02: Visiting Sources and Recording Surroundings
2 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-01.
==============================================================================
Goal: Adds direct observation of source surroundings and warning signs to the
household maps, giving the evidence for class problem lists.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-01: bring the household maps with
[changed: EB-04]      shared signs, distances, and uses. List the nearby
                      sources the class will visit together and agree what
                      warning signs to look for: pollution, waterlogging,
                      garbage, animal waste. Every child holds up a slate
                      naming two warning signs at the same time, the educator
                      looks at all slates and re-explains any missed sign
                      before moving on.

2. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to visit nearby tap, hand
[changed: CL-02]      pump, and well sources, with the educator staying with
                      all groups. In pairs, one child observes and names
                      warning signs while the other records them on a slate,
                      then they swap roles at the next source. Children must
                      not touch, drink, pick up, or lift water, waste, tools,
                      or heavy objects.

3. Lead Educator      Take the whole class together to visit nearby pond,
[changed: CL-02]      river, or canal sources, with the educator staying with
                      all groups. In the same pairs, swap the observer and
                      recorder roles from the last visit while noting
                      pollution, garbage, plants, and animal waste. Children
                      must not touch water or plants, pick up waste, or lift
                      heavy objects.

4. Lead Educator      Discuss what a clean surrounding looks like for each
                      source type. Add the visit observations and warning
                      signs to each child's map in the notebook. Keep added
                      notes brief with no extra teacher paperwork.

5. Lead Educator      In week 2, open a later lesson with four questions on
[changed: EB-01]      warning signs and clean surroundings answered from
                      memory on slates with maps and notebooks put away. Then
                      show the answers so each child checks their own. Nothing
                      is collected or marked.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Footpaths to nearby sources are passable and sources are accessible
  in the unit weeks.
  If not: Combine the two visits into one shorter visit to the nearest sources
  and discuss the rest from maps; drop one source type rather than extend
  weeks.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child answers
  warning-sign check on slates at the same time and the educator re-explains
  before the visits
- Evidence-Based, EB-01 Retrieval Practice: low-stakes recall of warning signs
  and clean surroundings from memory in week 2, self-checked
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: paired observer and
  recorder roles with swap at next source during tap, hand pump and well visit
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: same pairs swap observer
  and recorder roles for pond, river and canal visit


==============================================================================
MLS-WATER-03: Class Reports, Percentages and Village Facilities Map
3 weeks. Builds on MLS-WATER-02.
==============================================================================
Goal: Turns individual maps and visit notes into pooled class totals,
percentages, problem lists, and one wall village facilities map.

WHO                   WHAT TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lead Educator      Recall from MLS-WATER-02: bring maps with visit
                      observations and warning signs. Each child makes a short
                      water source report from the map and visit in the
                      notebook. State source types, uses, distances in steps,
                      and surroundings without home addresses.

2. Peer Facilitator   In turn, share water source reports with the class,
                      stating source types and surroundings only. List on
                      chart paper the kinds of water problems heard and which
                      sources have difficult access. Keep names on own work
                      only and record class totals only.

3. Lead Educator      Pool the reports into class totals on chart paper with
[changed: EB-03,      chalk tallies in mixed pairs, with one child counting
CL-02]                aloud and the partner marking the tally, then swapping
                      roles for each source type. Work one source-type percent
                      fully on the board step by step, saying each step aloud.
                      Each child then works the rest of the percents in the
                      notebook and compares the household mix with the class
                      average.

4. Lead Educator      Draw a simplified village facilities chart on chart
[changed: EB-04]      paper showing only main roads. Mark the water tank,
                      well, pump, tap, canal, and pond with the shared signs.
                      Have every child hold up a scrap showing the sign for
                      one named source, look at all scraps, and re-explain any
                      sign many miss before continuing. Keep the finished map
                      on the classroom wall for the next activities.

5. Peer Facilitator   In pairs, children who know the village outline help
                      those who do not to place roads and signs. Check each
                      mark uses the shared signs and can be read by another
                      child. Fix unclear marks on the wall chart with chalk.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START
- Needs: Classroom wall space is available to keep the village facilities
  chart for later activities.
  If not: Keep the chart folded and bring it out per lesson; shorten
  percentage work to one source-type calculation and one use calculation.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGES FROM THE SOURCE
- Evidence-Based, EB-03 Worked Examples, then Practice: one percent worked
  fully step by step before children work the remaining percents themselves
- Evidence-Based, EB-04 Checking for Understanding: every child shows the sign
  for a named source on scrap, educator looks at all and re-explains missed
  signs
- Connected Learning, CL-02 Structural Peer Roles: class tally done in mixed
  pairs with rotating counter and recorder roles
