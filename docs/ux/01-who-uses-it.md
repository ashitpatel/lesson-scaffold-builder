# Who uses the Lesson Scaffold Builder

> **Status:** a working command-line pipeline, run live on nine test lessons. There's no graphical interface yet. This page describes who it's for, so the design can be reviewed with practitioners before one is built.

The Lesson Scaffold Builder (LSB) takes a **long, lecture-heavy piece of teaching material** (a textbook lesson, a syllabus section, a teacher's guide) and turns it into a **small set of Micro-Lesson Scaffolds (MLS units)**. Each unit is a 2-to-4-week block of 3 to 7 concrete classroom steps. The units are linked as a dependency graph and printed as offline teacher cards. Along the way it removes what the material asks for that is unsafe, costly, device-bound or region-specific. Where they genuinely fit, it adds **evidence-based practices**, **Connected Learning** and **participatory budgeting**.

## The four kinds of user

### 1. Curriculum adapter: NGO programme staff, district trainers, teacher educators, textbook teams
- **What they do today:** turn official textbooks and guides into something teachers in their programme can actually run, usually by hand.
- **What they want:** a first draft of a runnable sequence for a whole lesson, with the reasoning shown, that they can check and edit.
- **How they use it:** run a lesson through the pipeline → read the teacher cards, the source flags and what was left out → edit or re-run → hand the cards to schools.
- **What they need to trust it:** to see which part of the source each step came from, what was dropped and why, and that nothing unsafe or invented was added.

### 2. Teacher (the `Lead_Educator` on every card)
- **What they want:** to know what to do on Monday, in plain words, with materials they already have.
- **How they use it:** receive one printed card per unit and run the steps in order. The "Before you start" box says what the unit relies on and what to do if it isn't there.
- **What matters:** the core teaching (practice, mastery checks, sustained writing) isn't cut; nothing needs a device, a purchase or a record about individual children.

### 3. School leader
- **Not a direct user**, but named whenever a step involves outside adults, a visit or anything beyond the classroom. Cards keep approvals and outside contact with the educator, never with students.

### 4. Community volunteer (the `Community_Volunteer` role)
- **Not a direct user.** Some steps give a volunteer a role, always with the educator present.

## Who it is **not** for
- **Students** never see the tool. They get roles inside steps (Recorder, Counter, a choice of theme, a vote on a class decision).
- **Grading or tracking.** The pipeline removes individual records, name lists and score charts from sources rather than producing them.
- **Replacing curriculum decisions.** It structures how a lesson is delivered; it doesn't decide what a system should teach, and it doesn't prescribe classroom routines or school rules.
